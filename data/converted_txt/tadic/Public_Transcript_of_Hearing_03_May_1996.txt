 22   Rule 51; whether or not there is to be some limitation in the future

 24   court, and whether or not the accused can subsequently be arrested

  1   withdraw.
  2   		     Your Honours, firstly, dealing with the question of Rule 
  3   51, the Prosecution considers that this is more in the nature of 
  4   withdrawal of an indictment under Rule 51 than it is an amendment to 
  5   the indictment.  True it is that the indictment will be drawn only in 
  6   part and will continue on, and the part that is not withdrawn will 
  7   continue on through the course of the trial.  In our submission, Rule 
  8   51 is the appropriate Rule to apply for the purposes of withdrawal.  
  9   We would urge the Chamber to interpret the provisions of withdrawal 
 10   of the indictment to include by way of interpretation the whole as 
 11   well as the part.
 12   		     Your Honours, sometimes in statutes of interpretation 
 13   that we are all accustomed to seeing, we see such a provision, that 
 14   when reading a piece of legislation the whole is to include the part. 
 15    We would urge that interpretation on the Chamber, there being no 
 16   other provision we can see in the Rules which are appropriate having 
 17   regard to the fact that we consider that this is not an amendment but 
 18   in fact a withdrawal.
 19   		     Your Honours, dealing then with the question of whether 
 20   or not there should be some time limit on whether or not the 
 21   prosecution can commence again -- in particular this is referred to 
 22   by way of an observation of the Defence, them not objecting to 
 23   Prosecutor's motion as such, but saying under paragraph 11.2 that at 
 24   a certain point in time the accused  should know whether or not he 
 25   still faces charges -- your Honours, there is no provision in the 
  1   Statute or anywhere else to limit the prosecution in bringing 
  2   charges.  In other words, there is no statute of limitation as such 
  3   on these types of offences.  We would argue, your Honours, that it 
  4   would be not only difficult to create what is, in effect, a statute 
  5   of limitation on the prosecution, but it may well be beyond the power 
  6   of the Chamber to do so, having regard to the absence of such a 
  7   provision in either the Statute or the Rules.
  8   		     In our submission, this is in some jurisdictions dealt 
  9   with on the basis of a pure common law dealing with the matter, by 
 10   the court saying that the prosecutor is being unreasonable after the 
 11   extensive length and the period of time in bringing these charges to 
 12   proceed with the prosecution and, in particular, having regard to the 
 13   fact that the accused is being convicted, should that be the case.  
 14   In those circumstances, what happens in those jurisdictions is that 
 15   the court simply refuses to lend its jurisdiction to the hearing on 
 16   the basis that the prosecution is unreasonably bringing the 
 17   prosecution at such a late stage, or having regard to the fact that 
 18   the person has previously been prosecuted and there is, in fact, a 
 19   stay of proceedings sorted.
 20   		     In our submission, that would be an appropriate remedy 
 21   available to the accused should the prosecution do that at such a 
 22   time that it is considered to be unreasonable.  In our submission, 
 23   this would afford the accused adequate protection in those 
 24   circumstances and is certainly preferable to the Chamber having to

  1   		     Your Honours, with respect to the final matter, that is

  5   might result from it.

  7   might be a situation where statute of limitation has been completed. 
  8    I think that is mixing up two totally different things.  The statute 
  9   of limitation is at least in my idea, but I do not know whether it is 
 10   different in other systems, a totally different issue compared to the 
 11   avoiding of undue delay during trial.  So, I think that the statute 
 12   of limitation, as  the French call it "description", means you lose 
 13   your right to prosecute after a certain period of time, a period of 
 14   time that is usually far longer than what we are talking about here. 
 15    We are discussing here that the accused is entitled to have a trial 
 16   without undue delay.  If the delay becomes too long, and as I told 
 17   you in my opinion also the German procedure has to be taken into 
 18   consideration, then it is not a matter of statute of limitation; it 
 19   is not a matter of "description" because that starts on from the 
 20   moment the offence has been committed.  Undue delay provisions 
 21   usually relate to the starting of the proceedings and the time the 
 22   proceedings take.  So I would say that if finally there would be an 
 23   undue delay, this has nothing to do with statute of limitation, but 
 24   exclusively with the provision of Article 21 sub (4) (c) of the 
 25   Statute.
