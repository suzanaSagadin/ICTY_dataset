 3                          [Appeals Chamber Decision]
 4                          --- Upon commencing.
 5            JUDGE CASSESE:  Good afternoon.  Will the registrar please call
 6    the case.
 7            THE REGISTRAR:  The Prosecutor against Dusko Tadic, case number
 8    IT-94-1-AR 72.
 9            JUDGE CASSESE:  Thank you.  May I have the appearances, please.
10    For the Prosecutor.
11            MR. NIEMANN:  If Your Honour pleases, my name is Niemann and I
12    appear with Mr. Fenrick.
13            JUDGE CASSESE:  Thank you.  Defence, the appearances.
14            DEFENCE COUNSEL:   [Microphone not activated].
15            JUDGE CASSESE:  Thank you.
16            Mr. Tadic, can you hear me in a language accessible to you?  Thank
17    you.
18            The Appeals Chamber of the International Tribunal is sitting today
19    to render its judgement on the interlocutory appeal lodged by the accused
20    against the decision of the Trial Chamber of the 10th of August, 1995.
21    This decision, as you may know, confirmed the jurisdiction of the
22    International Tribunal in the Tadic case.  If I may say so, this appeal
23    marks an important and historic moment in the development of international
24    law.  Indeed, it is the first time since the trials at Nuremberg at Tokyo
25    at the end of the Second World War that an international appellate court
 1    has been able to pronounce upon the current status of international
 2    criminal law and, in particular, international humanitarian law.
 3            Our decision, the decision of the Appeal Chamber amounts to 76
 4    pages and is accompanied by separate opinions by Judges Lee, Abisab 
 5    and Sidva , as well as a declaration by Judge Duschene .  I shall
 6    therefore refrain from reading the full text of this decision, the marshal
 7    because a limited number of copies will be available for distribution some
 8    time after this hearing.  I propose simply to summarise now the main
 9    points of our conclusions, and then read out the final part or
10    dispositif.
11            The accused has challenged the jurisdiction of this Tribunal on
12    three grounds both before the Trial Chamber and on appeal.  Those grounds
13    are:
14                1.  The unlawful establishment of the Tribunal by the Security
15    Council.
16                2.  The improper grounds of primacy over national courts also
17    having jurisdiction, and;
18                3.  Lack of subject matter jurisdiction.
19            The Trial Chamber rejected these arguments in toto.  The Appeals
20    Chamber today upholds and confirms the jurisdiction of the Tribunal but
21    relies on different reasons on a number of points.
22            The Trial Chamber held that it was not competent to determine the
23    first ground of the challenge to jurisdiction, that of the lawfulness of
24    establishment by the Security Council on the basis that this was not a
25    true challenge to jurisdiction as such and that, in any event, the
 1    question at issue was political and thus nonjusticeable.
 2            The Appeals Chamber disagrees with the analysis of the Trial
 3    Chamber on this point and by a majority of 4:1 concludes that this
 4    Tribunal is empowered to examine the question of whether or not the
 5    actions of the Security Council are lawful, that is to say, in compliance
 6    with the United Nations Charter.  However, we can exercise this power only
 7    as part of what is called in French competence de la competence  ^ ,
 8    namely as part not of our primary jurisdiction, but as part of our
 9    incidental jurisdiction.
10            To put it differently, we are authorised to exercise this power of
11    judicial review of the actions of a political body, the Security Council,
12    merely to the extent necessary to satisfy ourselves that the International
13    Tribunal has authority to adjudicate.  In exercising this incidental
14    jurisdiction, the Appeals Chamber has unanimously concluded that the
15    decision of the Security Council to establish the Tribunal was a
16    legitimate measure aimed at the restoration of peace and security in the
17    former Yugoslavia, a measure duly authorised under the charter of the
18    United Nations.  More specifically, our Chamber has concluded that the
19    Security Council has lawfully acted under Article 41 of the United Nations
20    Charter, thus the International Tribunal has been duly established by
21    law.
22            The second challenge to the jurisdiction of the Tribunal was based
23    on an objection to the ground of primacy to the Tribunal over the
24    jurisdiction of national courts.  The appellant asserted, among other
25    things, that this ground of primacy violates the concept of state
 1    sovereignty.  The Appeals Chamber stands in full agreement with the
 2    decision of the Trial Chamber on this point.
 3            One of the reasons supporting this conclusion is that state
 4    sovereignty must give way in cases where the nature of the alleged
 5    offences does not affect the interests of one state alone, but shocks the
 6    very conscience of mankind.
 7            Turning to the last ground raised by the accused, the alleged lack
 8    of subject matter jurisdiction, the Appeals Chamber has followed the
 9    approach taken by the Trial Chamber and examined in detail the scope of
10    its jurisdiction under Article 2, 3, and 5 of our Statute.  Prior to doing
11    so, the Appeals Chamber considered as a preliminary issue a new argument
12    by the accused.  Mr. Tadic now claims that there was no armed conflict in
13    the region of Prijedor where the crimes with which he is charged are said
14    to have been committed.
15            The Appeals Chamber finds that an armed conflict exists wherever
16    there is resort to armed force between states or protracted armed violence
17    between governmental authorities and organised armed groups or between
18    such groups within a state.  In such cases, international humanitarian law
19    applies from the initiation of such conflict and extends until a general
20    conclusion of peace is reached or a peaceful settlement is achieved.
21            As for the geographical frame of reference, we find that
22    international humanitarian law applies in the whole territory of the
23    warring states or, in the case of civil strife, in the whole territory
24    under the control of one of the conflicting parties whether or not actual
25    combat takes place there.
 1            Applying this concept to the situation in the former Yugoslavia,
 2    the Appeals Chamber finds that there did exist an armed conflict in the
 3    region of Prijedor at the time of the alleged offences.
 4            The Appeals Chamber then turned to the question of whether the
 5    characterisation of such conflicts as internal or international was of
 6    relevance.  On this point, the Appeals Chamber again differs from the
 7    decision of the Trial Chamber.  The Appeals Chamber finds in the present
 8    state of development of law that this question is of relevance but only to
 9    jurisdiction under Article 2 of the Statute, the Article relating to grave
10    breaches of the Geneva Conventions.
11            For the Tribunal to have jurisdiction under Article 2, the alleged
12    offences must have been committed within the context of an international
13    armed conflict.  By contrast, under both Articles 3 and 5 plus, of course,
14    Article 4 on genocide, the Tribunal has jurisdiction in respect of any
15    armed conflict be it international or internal.
16            When examining the question of its jurisdiction under Article 3 of
17    the Statute, the Appeals Chamber holds that Article 3 is very broad in
18    scope.  It is a residual or umbrella provision which grants our Tribunal
19    the power to prosecute all serious violations of international
20    humanitarian law.
21            The Appeals Chamber has set out in its decision the conditions
22    that must be fulfilled for a violation of international humanitarian law
23    to be subject to Article 3.  Put briefly, they are as follows:
24                1.  The violation must constitute an infringement of a rule of
25    international humanitarian law.
 1                2.  The rule must be customary in nature or if it belongs to
 2    treaty law, certain specific conditions must be met.
 3                3.  It must involve a breach of a rule protecting important
 4    values with grave consequences for the victim.
 5                4.  The violation of the rule must entail the individual
 6    criminal responsibility of the person breaching the rule.
 7            After reviewing the customary rules of international humanitarian
 8    law governing armed conflicts and individual criminal responsibility for
 9    such crimes, the Appeals Chamber concludes that the Tribunal has
10    jurisdiction under Article 3 over the acts alleged in the indictment
11    regardless of whether they occurred within an international or an internal
12    armed conflict.
13            Finally, the Appeals Chamber confirms the decision of the Trial
14    Chamber as to the scope of the jurisdiction under Article 5 of the
15    Statute, the Article on crimes against humanity.
16            I will now read the dispositif of the decision of the Appeals
17    Chamber in full.
18            The Appeals Chamber:
19                1.  By four votes to one decides that the International
20    Tribunal is empowered upon the plea challenging the legality of the
21    establishment of the International Tribunal.
22                2.  Unanimously decides that the aforementioned plea is
23    dismissed.
24                3.  Unanimously decides that the challenge to the primacy of
25    the International Tribunal over national court is dismissed.
 1                4.  By four votes to one decides that the International
 2    Tribunal has subject matter jurisdiction over the current case.
 3            Accordingly, the decision of the Trial Chamber of the 10th of
 4    August, 1995 stands revised.  The jurisdiction of the International
 5    Tribunal is affirmed, and the appeal is dismissed.
 6            As I mentioned before, the full text of our decision including the
 7    separate opinions of Judge Lee, Abisab , and Sidva together with
 8    the declaration of Judge Duschene will be available in English from
 9    the press office later today and tomorrow.  The declaration of Judge
10    Duschene is also available in French.  Translations into French and into
11    a language understood by the accused are being prepared and will be
12    available shortly.
13            We have thus come to the end of this sitting.  I therefore declare
14    the session closed.
