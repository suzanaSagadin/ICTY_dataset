  9   THE PRESIDING JUDGE:  Will the Prosecutor now present his application? 
 10   MR. GOLDSTONE:  Your Honours, in the aftermath of the horrors of the 
 11   Second World War, and the trials by the victorious powers of the 
 12   Nazi leaders at Nuremberg, it was generally anticipated by the 
 13   international community that a new era had begun.  An era in 
 14   which the human rights of all citizens in all countries of the 
 15   world would be universally respected.  It was not to be.  The 
 16   past five decades have witnessed some of the gravest violations 
 17   of humanitarian law.  Those responsible have too frequently 
 18   escaped trial and punishment by national courts.  Indeed, in 
 19   many cases they have been in positions of leadership and power 
 20   in their own countries and they have effectively placed 
 21   themselves above the law. 
 22   		There was no mechanism devised by the international 
 23   community for establishing the guilt of perpetrators and 
 24   punishing them.  Justice was denied to millions of victims of 
 25   murder, disappearances, rape and torture.  A catalogue of these 
  1   gruesome violations of international humanitarian law is to be 
  2   found in the annual surveys produced year after year by some 
  3   governmental and inter-governmental agencies and by national and 
  4   international non-governmental organisations. 
  5   		This Tribunal is the first attempt by the United Nations 
  6   to enforce international humanitarian law.  It is the first 
  7   recognition of the reality that if international criminal law is 
  8   to have relevance and is to command respect, it must be capable 
  9   of enforcement by the international community.  This remarkable 
 10   step by the United Nations is the consequence of events in the 
 11   former Yugoslavia since January 1991.  In describing these 
 12   events in a provisional order which was granted by the 
 13   International Court of Justice in The Hague, the judges referred 
 14   to the words of the General Assembly in a resolution of 11 
 15   December 1946 in the context of the Genocide Convention.  It was 
 16   there stated that "the conscious of mankind is shocked by the 
 17   events and they are contrary to moral law and the spirit and 
 18   aims of the United Nations".
 19   		Your Honours, the proceedings today constitute the first 
 20   public hearing by a Trial Chamber of this Tribunal. The 
 21   application is brought pursuant to Article 9 of the Security 
 22   Council Statute which confers upon this Tribunal not only 
 23   concurrent jurisdiction with all national courts but primacy 
 24   over them in respect of both the investigation and prosecution 
 25   of persons whose alleged offences fall within the competence of 
  1   the Tribunal.  In terms of Rule 9, the Prosecutor is given the 
  2   authority to apply to a Trial Chamber for the issue of a formal 
  3   request that a national court defer an investigation and 
  4   prosecution to this Tribunal. 
  5   		In the present case we rely upon the provisions of Rule 
  6   9(iii) which provide that such an application may be made by the 
  7   Prosecutor where in the national investigations or in criminal 
  8   proceedings instituted in a national court, I quote:  "What is 
  9   in issue is closely related to, or otherwise involves, 
 10   significant factual or legal questions which may have 
 11   implications for investigations or prosecutions before the 
 12   Tribunal".
 13   		Your Honours, when I took office on 15th August of this 
 14   year, lawyers and investigators in my Office were already 
 15   occupied with an investigation into allegations of serious 
 16   violations of humanitarian law in the Prijedor region of 
 17   Bosnia-Herzegovina.  The allegations came from a number of 
 18   sources including the report of the United Nations Commission of 
 19   Experts established pursuant to Security Council resolution 780 
 20   of 1992. 
 21   		The Deputy Prosecutor, Mr. Blewitt, became aware of the 
 22   arrest in Germany of Tadic.  The German authorities were 
 23   subsequently requested to provide the Prosecutor with all 
 24   relevant information concerning the investigation into crimes 
 25   allegedly committed by Tadic in Prijedor.
  1   		The Germany authorities complied with that request and 
  2   provided my Office with relevant information.  In addition, 
  3   statements have been obtained by my Office from additional 
  4   witnesses who are presently in the Netherlands, Norway, Sweden 
  5   and Switzerland.  My Office is furthermore in possession of 
  6   documents relevant to the events in Prijedor and, in particular, 
  7   the allegations against Tadic.
  8   		I am presently considering the issue of one or more 
  9   indictments against individuals who were involved in the serious 
 10   violations of humanitarian law in Prijedor. As appears from the 
 11   declaration of Michael Keegan, which is attached to my 
 12   Application, a policy was executed in Prijedor which involved 
 13   the widespread and systematic persecution of the non-Serb and, 
 14   particularly, the Muslim community - a policy which has commonly 
 15   come to be described as "ethnic cleansing".  According to the 
 16   report of the Commission of Experts, over 43,000 of the 
 17   approximately 50,000 Muslims who lived in Prijedor in 1991 have 
 18   left Prijedor or had been killed by June 1993. 
 19   		Witnesses available to the prosecution are able to 
 20   describe the events which led on 30 April 1992 to the overnight 
 21   seizure of power in Prijedor by Serbs and the announcements 
 22   broadcast on local radio to the effect that the area was 
 23   thenceforth to be called Srpske Opstina Prijedor.  Serbian flags 
 24   were flying on all official buildings and all non-Serbian 
 25   officials, including policemen, were dismissed. 
  1   		Three major concentration camps (as always they are 
  2   referred to, logori) were set up in the Prijedor area - in 
  3   Omarska, a former mine complex, Keraterm, a former ceramics 
  4   factory, and Trnopolje, a complex of several buildings.  At 
  5   Omarska and Keraterm mostly male members of the non-Serb 
  6   population of Prijedor were incarcerated - most of them between 
  7   the ages of 16 and 60.  Many leaders and prominent citizens in 
  8   the non-Serb population were taken to Omarska.  Women were 
  9   generally taken to Trnopolje. 
 10   		The serious atrocities which allegedly were carried out 
 11   at the three camps are described in some detail in the 
 12   declaration of Mr. Keegan.  They are such that, in our opinion, 
 13   if they are proved, the persons who ordered the atrocities and 
 14   those who executed them are guilty of serious violations of 
 15   humanitarian law and have committed offences which fall within 
 16   the jurisdiction of this Tribunal.
 17   		I regard it as my duty as Prosecutor to issue indictments 
 18   against such people if their involvement in these events can be 
 19   proved by sufficient evidence.  The investigations in this 
 20   regard are still ongoing.  I will come now, your Honours, to the 
 21   case against Tadic.
 22   		Again, as appears from the declaration of Mr. Keegan, my 
 23   Office is in possession of witnesses' statements which directly 
 24   implicate Tadic in some of the atrocities committed in Prijedor. 
 25    The allegations against him are as follows: 
  1   		He was involved directly in the events which led to the 
  2   removal of the majority of the Muslim population in Prijedor 
  3   and, effectively, the destruction of that community.  The first 
  4   of them, according to witnesses, was the organisation of the 
  5   Serbian referendum in November 1991.  Tadic helped create what 
  6   were, in effect, death lists of Muslim intellectuals and other 
  7   prominent citizens of Prijedor.  He was involved in the forced 
  8   removals of Muslims from the villages of the Prijedor region and 
  9   the looting and destruction of Muslim houses. 	I would emphasise 
 10   that according to eye-witnesses, when he participated in those 
 11   events, Tadic wore a military uniform and was, apparently, in 
 12   command of a group involved in the actions. 
 13   		Again, eye-witnesses are able to testify to Tadic and the 
 14   group under his command summarily executing unarmed non-Serbs.  
 15   He was directly involved in directing the transfer of non-Serbs 
 16   from the villages in the Prijedor region to camps.  He was 
 17   involved in the assaults of prisoners at the camps and at the 
 18   military barracks in Prijedor.  He was seen on numerous 
 19   occasions in the Trnepolje camp and at Omarska camp.  There is 
 20   an allegation that Tadic was involved in incidents of rape at 
 21   Omarska and that is being investigated.
 22   		The gruesome nature of the events which witnesses can 
 23   describe is illustrated in the following extract from the 
 24   affidavit -- from the declaration of Mr. Keegan, I quote: 
 25   		“Among the most widely witnessed events that Tadic was 
  1   involved in was the murder of Emir Karabasic, Jasmin Hrnjic and 
  2   Enver Alic ...  Three prisoners were brutally beaten and 
  3   tortured by Tadic and others, using metal rods, truncheons, and 
  4   knives, to the point of unconsciousness.  
  5   		Tadic then forced a fourth prisoner to drink motor oil 
  6   from the garage and then bite off the testicles of the 
  7   unconscious prisoners.  The prisoners died as a result of their 
  8   torture". 
  9   		Your Honours, I would also emphasise that Tadic was not a 
 10   regular official at any of the three camps to which I have 
 11   referred.  It appears that he was in a position of some 
 12   leadership in the area, in the Prijedor region, and was involved 
 13   in directing the activities of those with him on his visits to 
 14   the camps as well as the activities of the regular camp 
 15   officials.
 16   		I submit that the evidence of eye-witnesses, if accepted 
 17   by a Trial Chamber of this Tribunal, will make Tadic guilty of 
 18   some of the most serious offences falling within the competence 
 19   and jurisdiction of this international Tribunal.
 20   		We anticipate that within a short time indictments will 
 21   be submitted by the Prosecutor to the Registrar against Tadic 
 22   and other persons who have been identified as having been 
 23   involved in the "ethnic cleansing" in Prijedor Opstina.  In 
 24   support of such indictments I will rely on the evidence of the 
 25   witnesses located by the German investigators in Germany and 
  1   other witnesses who have been located and interviewed by members 
  2   of the staff of my Office in Germany and elsewhere.
  3   		The indictments will include serious offences in respect 
  4   of which the German authorities have no evidence and the other 
  5   accused will be persons who are outside Germany.
  6   		It is not possible during this Application, which is in 
  7   public, to disclose details of the investigations undertaken by 
  8   my Office, nor is it possible to disclose the identity of other 
  9   persons under investigation and who are likely to be included in 
 10   the indictment or indictments which will follow.
 11   		I can say, however, that the involvement of Tadic in the 
 12   factual circumstances under investigation by my Office is very 
 13   significant and it will adversely affect the investigation and 
 14   prosecution of other individuals if Tadic is prosecuted and 
 15   brought to trial in Germany.
 16   		We submit, therefore, that having regard to the scale and 
 17   nature of the atrocities alleged to have taken place in Prijedor 
 18   and the relevance of those events in respect of "ethnic 
 19   cleansing" in Bosnia-Herzegovina, this Tribunal is the proper 
 20   forum (and, we would submit, the only proper forum) for trying 
 21   those accused (including Tadic) of playing leading roles in 
 22   respect of those events.  It would certainly not be convenient 
 23   or in the interests of justice if Tadic were to be tried in 
 24   Germany only to be indicted and again tried in this court. 
 25   		The legal issues which will arise in a trial of Tadic are 
  1   central to the jurisdiction of this Tribunal.  They will involve 
  2   issues of international humanitarian law which are applicable to 
  3   the crimes alleged and the nature and manner of their proof.  
  4   So, both substantive and procedural legal issues which would 
  5   arise in a trial against Tadic will arise in a case against 
  6   those who acted in concert with him or acted in pursuance of a 
  7   common policy.
  8   		The factual issues speak for themselves.  The events in 
  9   Prijedor during 1992 form an important, a very important, area 
 10   of investigation by my Office.
 11   		It follows very clearly, in our submission, that the 
 12   Tadic investigation by Germany concerns an issue closely related 
 13   to and involves significant factual or legal questions which 
 14   will have implications for investigations currently undertaken 
 15   by me. 
 16   		The Tadic case therefore falls within the provisions of 
 17   Rule 9(iii) and, in terms of Article 9(2) of the Statute and 
 18   Rule 10(A), this Chamber may issue a formal request to Germany 
 19   that its national courts defer to the competence of this 
 20   Tribunal.
 21   		Your Honours, until an indictment has been issued against 
 22   Tadic, a warrant for his arrest cannot be issued under Article 
 23   19 of the Statute.  However, to the extent that it may be or 
 24   become necessary, as Prosecutor, I am authorised by Rule 40(i), 
 25   in a case of urgency, to request any State to arrest a suspect 
  1   provisionally.  I will, therefore, request Germany to effect the 
  2   provisional arrest of Tadic if that is considered necessary by 
  3   me for any reason prior to the issue of an indictment. 
  4   		We request, therefore, an order for the deferral by 
  5   Germany and by the German courts to this Tribunal. Your Honours, 
  6   the basis of the Application and in the Application that was 
  7   handed to the Registrar some weeks ago, and in consequence of 
  8   which this sitting of this Trial Chamber was arranged by the 
  9   President, the basis was that the German authorities were 
 10   conducting an ongoing investigation against Tadic. 
 11   		During the course of yesterday, the Press Officer of the 
 12   Tribunal, Mr. Chartier, brought to the attention of my Office a 
 13   report from a German press agency, Deutsche Press Agente, to the 
 14   effect that yesterday Mr. Tadic was, in fact, indicted before a 
 15   court in Germany. 
 16   		My friend, Mr. Wilkitzki, who appears for the Federal 
 17   Government in Germany, confirmed the correctness of that 
 18   allegation this morning. 
 19   		In order to bring the Application correctly within the 
 20   factual situation, we have prepared an amended Application which 
 21   records the fact that Tadic was indicted in Germany on 7 
 22   November 1994.  I beg leave to hand up the new amended 
 23   Application.  (Handed)  The relief claimed in the Application is 
 24   clearly not different in any way from the relief claimed in the 
 25   original Application.  That relief, your Honours, is that the 
  1   formal request be issued by this Chamber for the deferral of the 
  2   Tadic proceedings by Germany pursuant to Rule 10 of the Rules 
  3   and we, accordingly, move for such an order.
