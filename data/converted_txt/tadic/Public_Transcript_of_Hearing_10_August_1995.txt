 1                                  10 August 1995
 2                               [Decision on Prosecution Motions]
 3            JUDGE McDONALD:  Good morning, pending before the Trial Chamber
 4    are two motions, a determination of which has been reserved to be
 5    delivered today.  I will deal with the two motions separately.
 6            The first motion is a motion requesting protective measures for
 7    victims and witnesses filed by the Prosecutor on 18 May 1995 which
 8    contains 13 separate prayers for relief in respect of 7 alleged victims or
 9    witnesses who are referred to by the pseudonyms A, F, G, H, I, J, and K,
10    and one prayer concerning all witnesses who may testify in this case.
11            The Defence has filed a response objecting in part and agreeing in
12    part to the protective measures sought.  Two briefs have been submitted by
13    amicus curiae, and one by Professor Christine Cinkin , Dean and
14    Professor of International Law, University of South Hampton, United
15    Kingdom; and a joint brief filed by Rhonda Copeland, Felice Garr,
16    Jennifer Green and Sarah Hosan , all from the United States of America
17    on behalf of Jacob Blaustein Institute for the Advancement of Human
18    Rights of the American Jewish Committee of New York; the Centre for
19    Constitutional Rights New York; The International Women's Human Rights Law
20    Clinic of the City, University of New York, New York; and The Women's
21    Refugees Project of the Harvard Immigration and Refugee Programme; and
22    Cambridge and Summerville Legal Services, both of Cambridge
23    Massachusetts.
24            At the request of the Prosecutor, which was not opposed by the
25    Defence, the motion was heard in camera on 21 June 1995.  Since that date,
 1    additional confidential filings giving details of prior media contact, if
 2    any, with the pseudonym witnesses have been made by both parties pursuant
 3    to an order of this Trial Chamber of 23 June 1995.  In the same filing,
 4    the Prosecutor has amended two of its prayers for relief.  The Prosecutor
 5    has also withdrawn the request for relief in respect of the witness
 6    pseudonymed A and now only seeks delayed disclosure to the accused of the
 7    identity of the witness pseudonymed F and not non-disclosure based on
 8    evidentiary issues surrounding the testimony of that witness.
 9            The majority decision of myself and Judge Vohrah and a separate
10    opinion from Judge Stephen in which he dissents on the issue of anonymity
11    are handed down today.  The full and authoritative English text will be
12    available from the Press and Information Office after the close of this
13    hearing and an official translation into French, and a translation into
14    the language of the accused will be available within the next few days.
15            The order of the Tribunal in this matter is as follows:  Having
16    considered the written and oral submissions of the parties and of the
17    amicus curiae, the Trial Chamber, pursuant to Rule 75 of the Rules of
18    Procedure and Evidence orders as follows:
19                1.  The identity of Witness A shall be released immediately.
20                2.  The names, addresses, whereabouts and other identifying
21    data concerning persons given pseudonyms F, G, H, I, J, and K, shall not
22    be disclosed to the public or the media.
23                3.  All hearings to litigate the issue of protective measures
24    for the pseudonym witnesses shall be in closed session.
25                4.  The names, addresses, whereabouts and other identifying
 1    information concerning F, G, H, I, J, and K shall be sealed and not
 2    included in any of the public records of the International Tribunal.
 3                5.  To the extent the names of or other identified data
 4    concerning any of these victims and witnesses are contained in existing
 5    public documents of the International Tribunal, those names and other
 6    identifying data shall be expunged from those documents.
 7                6.  Documents of the International Tribunal identifying these
 8    witnesses shall not be disclosed to the public or the media.
 9                7.  The testimony of Witnesses F, G, H, and I may be given by
10    one-way closed circuit television or such other method as will avoid the
11    retraumatisation of these witnesses.
12                8.  The testimony of witnesses F, G, H, I, J, and K shall be
13    heard in closed session; however, edited recordings and transcripts of
14    these sessions shall be released to the public and the media after review
15    by the Victims and Witnesses Unit of the International Tribunal.
16                9.  The pseudonyms F, G, H, I, J, and K shall be used whenever
17    referring to these witnesses in proceedings before the International
18    Tribunal and in discussions among parties to the trial.
19                10.  The Prosecutor shall disclose to Defence counsel and the
20    accused the name and complete statement of Witness F not less than 30 days
21    in advance of the firm trial date.  The Prosecutor may redact from Witness
22    F's statement Witness F's current address and whereabouts and information
23    disclosing the present address and whereabouts of the witness's
24    relatives.
25                11.  The Prosecutor may withhold from the Defence and the
 1    accused the current identity of and other identifying data concerning
 2    Witness G, and the names of and other identifying data concerning
 3    Witnesses H, J, and K.
 4                12.  The Prosecutor shall disclose to the Defence and the
 5    accused the complete statements of Witnesses G and I redacted only as may
 6    be necessary to preserve the anonymity of Witnesses H, J, and K and the
 7    current identity of Witness G not later than 30 days in advance of the
 8    firm trial date.
 9                13.  The Prosecutor shall provide the Defence with details of
10    the general locality for Witnesses J and K not less than 30 days in
11    advance of the firm trial date.
12                14.  The testimony of witnesses H, J, and K may be given using
13    voice and image altering devices to the extent necessary to prevent their
14    identities from becoming known to the accused.
15                15.  The accused, the Defence counsel, and their
16    representatives who are acting pursuant to their instructions or requests
17    shall not disclose the names of any of these victims and witnesses or
18    other identifying data concerning these witnesses to the public or to the
19    media except to the limited extent such disclosure to members of the
20    public is necessary to investigate the witnesses adequately.
21                16.  Such disclosure shall be done in such a way as to
22    minimise the risk of the victims and witness's names being divulged to the
23    public at large or to the media.
24                17.  The accused, the Defence counsel, and their
25    representatives who are acting pursuant to their instructions or requests
 1    shall notify the Office of the Prosecutor of any requested contact with
 2    Prosecution witnesses or the relatives of such witnesses and that the
 3    Office of the Prosecutor shall make arrangements for such contact.
 4                18.  The public and the media shall not photograph, video
 5    record or sketch the six protected witnesses appearing in this matter
 6    while they are in the precincts of the International Tribunal.
 7            That concludes reading the order regarding the first motion.  The
 8    second motion pending before the Trial Chamber is a motion filed by the
 9    Defence on 23 June 1995 contesting the jurisdiction of the Tribunal.
10            The Defence seeks dismissal of all or part of the charges first on
11    the basis of the alleged unlawful establishment of the Tribunal.
12    Secondly, that its primacy over domestic courts constitutes and
13    infringement of state sovereignty.  And thirdly, that the Tribunal does
14    not have subject matter jurisdiction over the offences as charged.
15            The Prosecutor has filed a response objecting to all respects of
16    the Defence motion.  A brief has also been submitted by the government of
17    the United States as amicus curiae.  A public hearing of the issues was
18    held on 25th and 26th July, 1995.  The unanimous decision of the Trial
19    Chamber on this matter is handed down today.
20            Again, the full and authoritative English text will be available
21    after the close of this hearing, and an official translation into French
22    and a translation into the language of the accused will be available
23    within the next few days.
24            The order of the Trial Chamber in this matter is as follows:
25    Having considered the written and oral submissions of the parties and of
 1    the amicus curiae, the Trial Chamber, pursuant to Rule 72 of the Rules of
 2    Procedure and Evidence, dismisses the motion insofar as it relates to
 3    primacy jurisdiction and subject matter jurisdiction under Articles 2, 3,
 4    and 5 of the Statute and decides it to be incompetent insofar as it
 5    purports to raise other issues and denies the relief sought by the
 6    Defence.  The parties have 15 days in which to file a notice of appeal
 7    against this decision on jurisdiction.
 8            If there are no other matters to be brought to the attention of
 9    the Trial Chamber, we will stand adjourned.
10                          ---  Whereupon the hearing adjourned.
