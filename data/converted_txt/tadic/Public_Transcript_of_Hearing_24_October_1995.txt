  3   Statute are the proper standard by which this Trial Chamber should 
  4   consider the  matter. 
  5        As stated in paragraph 106 of the Secretary General's Report:  "It 
  6   is axiomatic that the International Tribunal must fully respect the 
  7   internationally recognised standards regarding the rights of the 
  8   accused at all stages of its proceedings.  In the view of the 
  9   Secretary General, such internationally recognised standards are, in 
 10   particular, contained in Article 14 of the International Covenant on 
 11   Civil and Political Rights. 
 12        Paragraph 106 of the Secretary General's Report speaks in 
 13   reference to Article 21 of the Statute which is entitled "Rights of 
 14   the Accused".  Paragraph 4(a) of that Article is derived from 
 15   Article 14(3)(a) of the Covenant of Civil and Political Rights and 
 16   is the basic right from which the requirements for the indictment 
 17   before the Tribunal is derived. 
 18        The question is, what does the language of Article 14(3) of the 
 19   International Covenant on Civil and Political rights mean?  What 
 20   does it require?  In part, the relevant part of that language is 
 21   that the accused has the right to be "informed promptly and in 
 22   detail in a language which he understands of the nature and cause of 
 23   the charge against him".  The term "nature and cause of the charge" 
 24   means, according to the comments to the Covenant, "not only the 
 25   exact legal description of the offence but also the facts underlying 
  1   it". 
  2        The comments go on to say that the information must be sufficient 
  3   to allow for the preparation of a defence pursuant to Article 
  4   14(3)(b), that is, the right mentioned earlier of the accused to 
  5   have adequate time and facilities  for the preparation of a defence.
  6        The comments go on to state that the "information must thus be 
  7   provided with the lodging of the charge or shortly thereafter, with 
  8   the opening of the preliminary judicial investigation or with the 
  9   setting of some other hearing that gives rise to a clear official 
 10   suspicion against an accused person". 
 11        As with the other provisions addressing fundamental rights in the 
 12   International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, this 
 13   commentary addresses the observation of an accused's right to 
 14   detailed information regarding the crimes with which he or she is 
 15   charged in general terms, and it must do so because it must account 
 16   for a variety of judicial and penal systems.
 17        Thus, Article 14 of the International Covenant does not demand 
 18   that the rights set forth in paragraph 3(a) be fulfilled in any 
 19   specific format or by any particular document.  Rather, it only 
 20   requires that the accused be informed of the nature and cause of the 
 21   charges against him or her, in detail, at or near the time that the 
 22   accused is the subject of official charges or initial proceedings.
 23        It is clear that the Tribunal's process, as a whole, meets those 
 24   requirements.  The indictment is simply a part of the process and it 
 25   is designed, as stated above, to place the accused on notice of the 
  1   nature of the charges alleged against him or her with sufficient 
  2   specificity so that the accused can understand the nature of the 
  3   charges and be able to enter pleas to each charge.

  6   essential issue here for the Defence.  It is our submission that the

  8   anticipate what defences the accused might like to allege in a 
  9   particular case.
