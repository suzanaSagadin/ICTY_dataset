13            The second point relates to a video-conference link witness, and
14    since it's always possible that things will accelerate beyond our present
15    expectations this week, I must mention it now.  It's something that would
16    ordinarily be easier for me to mention in closed session, by think I can

18    closed session in the absence of the accused.
19            Where a witness is granted voice distortion as well as facial
20    distortion, it is not possible for there to be a broadcast of the
21    proceedings publicly.  I've explored this, or we both have explored this
22    with the technical staff this morning, and it isn't possible.  We've
23    considered with the staff and with the video -- I beg your pardon, with
24    the interpretation booths whether one can suppress the original voice of
25    the witness and simply put out by way of broadcast the same words in the
 1    mouths of the interpreters.  That isn't possible either.
 2            The Chamber might think that with such a witness it's placed in
 3    this unfortunate position of either having to have closed-session
 4    testimony, because that's the only way the evidence could be given - and
 5    this is something that happened once, as I recall, in the Kordic case - or
 6    obliging the witness to surrender his protection of voice distortion
 7    already granted, or obliging the Prosecution to do without the evidence
 8    altogether.
 9            We've been giving urgent thought to this, and it seems to us there
10    is a way around this technical problem which, as I understand it, is
11    incapable of resolution otherwise.  And the way or ways around it are as
12    follows:  One, that the hearing should be in closed session but that,
13    subject to any particular necessary redactions, a transcript could be
14    produced immediately thereafterwards because the evidence as spoken would
15    be, under the ruling of the Court, evidence that should not be in closed
16    session.
17            Alternatively, and I've asked the technical staff to consider
18    this:  It might be possible, the evidence having been given in closed
19    session, for there to be a delayed transmission which would only have the
20    original language spoken by someone else other than in the voice of the
21    witness, and thereby the Chamber would meet its duties of ensuring a
22    public trial by a delayed broadcast which would have questions and
23    answers, the answers not being distorted but being in the voice of an
24    interpreter.
25            That technical possibility has only recently been raised by me
 1    with the technical staff, and I don't know whether there is a solution
 2    along those lines that's possible.  That's a problem that may arise and
 3    could arise as early as this week, in theory.
 4            JUDGE KWON:  How long?
 5            MR. NICE:  Well, as long as it would take for the necessary
 6    delayed broadcast by the technical people to be prepared.
 7            If I can explain --
 8            JUDGE MAY:  Who would give the answers?

10    explain?  And if I get it wrong, it will be down to me.  The problem
11    arises because once a witness speaks, his voice is part of the recording
12    and part of the record and part of the general transmission, and it's not
13    possible to arrest it at that stage even if, for example, here the words
14    heard were not the distorted words but the words of the interpreter.  One
15    can imagine circumstances where you have a witness speaking, a distorted
16    voice, you suppress the distorted voice, you let the language speaker
