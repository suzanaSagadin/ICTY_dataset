 4            JUDGE MERON:  I would now like to start from giving a brief
 5    background of the case that we will be hearing this morning.
 6            Let me first remind you that we are here on an interlocutory
 7    appeal from the Trial Chamber's decision to impose counsel on
 8    Mr. Milosevic for the presentation of the Defence case of the trial.  This
 9    question has been percolating in one form or another since very early on
10    in Mr. Milosevic's trial.  During the first stage of the proceedings, the
11    Trial Chamber rejected the Prosecution's motion that Defence counsel
12    should be imposed on Mr. Milosevic, finding that imposition of counsel, in
13    the Trial Chamber's words, is "not normally appropriate in adversarial
14    proceedings such as these."
15            After the close of the Prosecution's case, however, the Trial
16    Chamber came to rethink its earlier position.  Due to multiple delays
17    caused mainly by Mr. Milosevic's ill health, the Trial Chamber has become
18    increasingly concerned about his ability to present a successful defence.
19    In reviewing the issue anew, the Trial Chamber ordered that medical
20    examinations be performed on Mr. Milosevic and ordered briefing from the
21    parties on the question of whether Mr. Milosevic was capable of properly
22    presenting the defence portion of his case at trial.
23            After reviewing the reports of the medical doctors and taking the
24    parties' legal positions into account, the Trial Chamber ultimately
25    decided to impose counsel on Mr. Milosevic for the defence phase of his
 1    trial.  It rested its decisions largely on the ground that Mr. Milosevic's
 2    health would be seriously endangered by the continued burden of conducting
 3    his own defence at a level that would permit the trial to be completed
 4    within a reasonable time.  The Trial Chamber then granted certification to
 5    appeal its decision on an interlocutory basis, and assigned counsel has
 6    appealed the ruling on Mr. Milosevic's behalf.
 7            Assigned counsel has challenged the Trial Chamber's ruling on
 8    several distinct grounds.  First, assigned counsel contends that
 9    Mr. Milosevic's right to a fair trial cannot override his right to defend
10    himself since neither the letter nor the spirit of the ICTY Statute allows
11    the Tribunal to frame an overarching right to a fair trial as superior to
12    the individually enumerated trial rights.
13            The Prosecution responds that Mr. Milosevic's right of the
14    exercise of his trial rights is not absolute but is subject to the control
15    of the Trial Chamber to ensure a fair and expeditious trial in the
16    interests of justice.  The fairness of a trial, the Prosecution contends,
17    must be judged not merely by reference to the accused but also by
18    reference to the Tribunal and its own interest in legitimacy.
19            Second, assigned counsel argues that Mr. Milosevic's right to
20    defend himself is more important than the need to continue -- to conclude
21    the trial as speedily as possible.  Enshrining individual rights, assigned
22    counsel argues, is an empty exercise unless the Tribunal is prepared to
23    enforce those rights even when they prove inconvenient, particularly when
24    any inconvenience is not caused by the intentional actions of the accused.
25            The Prosecution responds that the Defence underplays the
 1    importance of the continuation and completion of this trial.  Furthermore,
 2    the Prosecution contends there are additional reasons quite apart from
 3    those cited by the Trial Chamber that justify the imposition of counsel on
 4    Mr. Milosevic.  Specifically, the Prosecution argues Mr. Milosevic has
 5    intentionally obstructed his trial by intentional manipulation of his
 6    medication regime so as to produce periods of ill health and by
 7    systematically disruptive conduct when he's actually in the courtroom.
 8            Assigned counsel's third ground of appeal is that once the Trial
 9    Chamber decided that Mr. Milosevic was so sick as to be unable to
10    represent himself, the Trial Chamber should have considered the
11    preliminary issue of whether he was even fit enough to stand trial.  In
12    particular, if Mr. Milosevic is so unwell as to be unable to conduct his
13    own defence, assigned counsel suggests that he may well also be unable to
14    exercise his right to testify at trial over a prolonged period.
15            The Prosecutor responds that testifying is unlikely to be as
16    stressful and difficult as conducting his own trial from beginning to end.
17    There is no indication, the Prosecutor concludes, that Mr. Milosevic's
18    inability to act as a full-time lawyer in a highly complicated case casts
19    doubt on his ability to testify in court.
20            Fourth, assigned counsel contends that the Trial Chamber should
21    have allowed Mr. Milosevic to obtain his own medical report so that he
22    could challenge the finding of unfitness to represent himself.  Because
23    Mr. Milosevic is concerned that the doctors who examined him are biased
24    against him, he should have had the opportunity, as a procedural matter,
25    to present his own evidence on a question of such great importance.
 1            The Prosecutor responds that Mr. Milosevic did not raise this
 2    question in a timely manner, and further, that there is no indication that
 3    the examining doctors were anything but fair in their analysis.
 4            Fifth, assigned counsel argues that the Trial Chamber should have
 5    requested an updated medical opinion before assigning counsel to
 6    Mr. Milosevic.  Because a month and a half had passed since the doctors'
 7    examinations and since Mr. Milosevic had successfully presented a two-day
 8    opening Defence statement, the Trial Chamber should have made sure that he
 9    was still unfit to represent himself on September 2 when the order issued.
10            The Prosecution responds that Mr. Milosevic's health has shown
11    every sign of degrading, not improving, and further argues that repeatedly
12    revisiting the question of Mr. Milosevic's health would only jeopardise
13    the successful completion of the trial.
14            Sixth, assigned counsel argues that the Trial Chamber should have
15    sought to create some modified working regime within which Mr. Milosevic
16    could have continued to represent himself.  Assigned counsel suggests that
17    the Trial Chamber could have reduced the number of trial days per week or
18    given a recess for Mr. Milosevic to recover fully, or assigned stand-by
19    counsel to assist Mr. Milosevic.
20            The Prosecutor responds that the trial is already on a reduced
21    working schedule of three days a week to accommodate Mr. Milosevic's
22    health and that there is no indication that Mr. Milosevic would cooperate
23    with stand-by counsel any more than he intends to with assigned counsel.
24            Seventh, assigned counsel argues that assigning counsel to
25    Mr. Milosevic against his will is going to cause enormous practical
