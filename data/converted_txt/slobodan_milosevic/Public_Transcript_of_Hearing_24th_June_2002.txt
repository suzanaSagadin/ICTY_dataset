 1                          Monday, 24 June 2002
 2                          [Rule 77 Hearing]
 3                          [Open session]
 4                          [The witness entered court]
 5                          --- Upon commencing at 1.18 p.m.
 6            JUDGE MAY:  The transcript should record that the witness K12 is
 7    present.  The purpose of this hearing is to review the position of his
 8    contempt, to give counsel representing him an opportunity to make
 9    submissions, bearing in mind that the Trial Chamber was acting summarily
10    in the exercise of its inherent powers in relation to contempt in the face
11    of the Court.
12            I should have asked you to introduce yourself, Ms. Heemskerk.
13    Could you like to do that, please, for the record.
14            MS. HEEMSKERK:  Thank you very much, Your Excellency.  May it
15    please the Court, my name is Rebecca Heemskerk and I'm Defence lawyer for
16    Mr. K12 and I'm an attorney here in the Netherlands, in Maastricht.
17            JUDGE MAY:  Thank you.  Mr. Nice, is there anything you want to
18    add?  I think we asked for a report.
19            MR. NICE:  Yes, Your Honours did ask for a report and I'm aware
20    that we haven't provided you with one.  The reason we haven't provided you
21    one is because things have taken on a life of their own with, first of
22    all, the request for clarification that we made on, I think, the
23    6th of June in a short closed session, and then the notice of appeal which
24    followed fairly shortly after that.  And then the application for delay.
25            The Court will be heartened to know that since Ms. Heemskerk has
 1    been involved, she has been in contact with us, and indeed we've had a
 2    meeting here at The Hague last week and she was good enough to turn up and
 3    come and see us.  We were able to identify the certain transcripts or a
 4    transcript and, later, further transcripts that were in closed session
 5    that she hadn't previously had, and although I wasn't able to get those
 6    transcripts to her on the day of our meeting, it looks as though now she's
 7    got access to all the transcripts.
 8            We weren't able to resolve between ourselves a common approach to
 9    the factual legal position in which the Chamber finds itself, and it may
10    be that it would be the most sensible course for her now to make
11    representations, our having leave to reply to them in due course.
12            JUDGE MAY:  Yes.
13            MR. NICE:  There is a translation of the magazine article that was
14    waved at the Chamber and requested by His Honour Judge Kwon in the course
15    of one of the proceedings.  You don't have it with you.  I don't --
16            JUDGE MAY:  We have it, in fact.  We've been handed it.
17            MR. NICE:  Very well.
18            JUDGE MAY:  Now, Ms. Heemskerk, what would you like to say about
19    the matter?  We will -- since the accused was not represented when our
20    finding was made, we will hear any submissions which you think it proper
21    to make to us.  Could you remember to put your microphone on.
22            MS. HEEMSKERK:  Sorry.  Thank you, Your Excellency.  Well, the
23    only submissions that I'd like to make at this point do not really have to
24    do anything with the proceedings, how they went on the 4th of June and, I
25    believe, the 6th of June.  As your Trial Chamber has been notified, I
 1    heard, we filed an appeal against the way the proceedings happened.  But
 2    today, I think the issue really or truly is what the position of my client
 3    is, if he still refuses to testify as he has conceded to this Court on the
 4    4th of June or, actually, tried to explain.
 5            I've talked to my client long and hard, and there is still the
 6    difficulty with his brother.  And that is what he hinted at I think the
 7    last time that he was here in the Trial Chamber.
 8            I could explain what his position is.
 9            JUDGE MAY:  Yes, if you would.
10            MS. HEEMSKERK:  Okay.  Well, the thing is --
11            JUDGE MAY:  We are in open session.  Do you want to -- do you want
12    to go into closed session?
13            MS. HEEMSKERK:  Yes.  Then I'd like to make an application to go
14    to closed session so we can discuss the delicacies of it.
15            JUDGE MAY:  Yes
