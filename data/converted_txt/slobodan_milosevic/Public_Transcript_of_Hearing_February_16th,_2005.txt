25       Q.   Please tell us, Kosovo as an internal issue of a sovereign state,
 1    Federal Republic of Yugoslavia, how was that issue internationalised?  How
 2    did it reach the Security Council's agenda?
 3       A.   Kosovo reached the Security Council's agenda through the Contact
 4    Group, which was established for Bosnia and Herzegovina, however in 1997
 5    started looking into the situation in Kosovo and Metohija.  We objected to
 6    that, however, the answer was that the Contact Group could look into and
 7    discuss any issue it wanted.  As the practice of the Security Council was
 8    to get the views of the Contact Group prior to the issue being put on the
 9    agenda of the Security Council, all of these issues were discussed there
10    first and then permanent members and later on also temporary members of
11    the Security Council took into account and complied with the opinion of
12    the Contact Group, which we opposed.  We protested concerning that,
13    however, they disregarded that protest of ours.

15    violation of the principle of the UN not to discuss internal issues of one
16    state?

18    already said, the Contact Group believed it was entitled to discuss about
19    the internal issues of our country.  And after that initial occasion, they
20    simply introduced it into the regular practice of Security Council through
21    the back door, and from then on it became a regular practice.

19    civilians who died as a result of the infighting were discovered.  This
20    situation was abused by the Bosnian Muslim government to further its
21    propaganda campaign of the alleged mass killings and disappearance of
22    Muslims from the area."
23            Mr. Jovanovic, you know of the admissions that have been made by
24    the Republika Srpska publicly in recent months about 7.000 dead.  Now, can
25    you help us, please, with how you came to write that paragraph, blaming
 1    the Muslims for their own deaths?  Because just to remind you, this isn't
 2    a letter that comes from the government; this comes from you.
 3       A.   May I explain straight away?  This is an omission, an omission on
 4    my part, because this document was not intended for the Security Council
 5    at all.  It was intended for head of the Russian mission who, it so
 6    happened, was then president of the Security Council as well.  The point
 7    was that he knew that soon a Security Council session would be held
 8    precisely dedicated to Srebrenica, and he asked us to give him any kind of
 9    information about what had happened there.  I asked my Foreign Ministry
10    for this information, and after a long time and after a lot of insistence,
11    instead of information, I got the information that the leadership of
12    Republika Srpska had sent.  It was a rather long text.
13            Then at the senior staff meeting of our mission, I told my deputy
14    chief of mission to translate those parts that seemed logical to him and
15    to send them, of course with my signature, to the head of the Russian
16    mission so that, if he wished, he could use some of it in his speech
17    there.  However, my deputy chief of mission misunderstood this, that he
18    was not supposed to send it to him in his capacity as president of the
19    Security Council but rather in his capacity as head of the Russian
20    mission.  I signed this hastily because I didn't have time to check out
21    before that whether he had done this properly.
22            Proof of this can be found with all the members of the staff of
23    the mission then.  The following day everybody realised what happened.  My
24    omission was obvious and I was wondering after that what to do, whether to
25    withdraw this or to let things go as they were.  Unfortunately, this had
 1    already been circulated, it had produced a certain effect, and in this
 2    vacillation as to what I was supposed to do, I let things go along the way
 3    they did, and I'm sorry about the omission.
