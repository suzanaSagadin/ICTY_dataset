    10          of the perpetrator towards the act in the form of dolus

    20          clearly help us to define the liability of an

    22                I shall present the reasons very briefly.  A
    23          criminal act, a premeditated act is done by someone who
    24          is aware of that act and who wishes to commit it, or who
    25          was aware that a harmful act can ensue as a result, and,
     1          in the case of both forms of premeditation there is a
     2          common intellectual component, consciousness, awareness
     3          of the act, and there is a different wilful component.
     4          In one form he wishes the consequence, and in the other
     5          case, he agrees to it without wanting it.  In the first,
     6          we call direct premeditation, and in the second,
     7          possible premeditation.  Those are categories on which
     8          criminal law rests, but, regardless of which form of
     9          premeditation we have in mind, there must be awareness
    10          of the offence of the act.  Whether that be proven by
    11          direct evidence or indications is less important.  It is
    12          a factual situation.
