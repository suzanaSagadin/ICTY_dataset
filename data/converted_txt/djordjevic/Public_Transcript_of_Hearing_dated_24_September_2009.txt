15             THE REGISTRAR:  Your Honours, we're back in open session.
16             JUDGE PARKER:  We have now concluded the evidence of a witness
17     who was heard in private.  I correctly understand, do I, Ms. Petersen,
18     that there is no other witness available for today or tomorrow?
19             MS. PETERSEN:  That's my understanding, Your Honour, although
20     Chester Stamp is here, and I think he would probably be best situated to
21     answer those questions.
22             JUDGE PARKER:  I think he'll be very grateful to you for that
23     suggestion.
24             Mr. Stamp.
25             MR. STAMP:  Thank you very much, Your Honours.  I -- I think I
 1     should indicate to the Court what the position is in relation to the
 2     witness.  We are now reaching the end of the case.  We have called quite
 3     a few witnesses.  And as, no doubt, Your Honour will have experienced, at
 4     this point in time is when we have the witnesses with the greatest
 5     difficulties scheduled.  We have, for a variety of reasons, including
 6     scheduling reasons, withdrawn some from the list in order not to waste
 7     time because some of the evidence in relation to those witnesses was
 8     marginal.  However, there remains some very important witnesses that have
 9     presented some difficulties in scheduling, and for that reason we are not
10     in a position to put up any further witnesses this week, and we only have
11     one for next week.
12             After those two witnesses, there are three -- there would be
13     three remaining witnesses that we have not withdrawn from the list.  One
14     of them we probably will have to withdraw because he cannot be located or
15     we have not been able to locate him as yet, and the other two were
16     witnesses who had previously been scheduled.  Indeed one had been
17     subpoenaed to attend in March or February, that is K87, but who could not
18     attend because of reasons of his health.  And another witness Colonel
19     Crosland, who's scheduling presented us with some difficulties in making
20     arrangements for him to attend with the authorities of the United
21     Kingdom.
22             I wish to assure the Court that in respect of these two last
23     witnesses -- or I think they will be the two last witnesses because I
24     don't think we will be able to call the third one, we have done all that
25     is necessary and practicable to get them here as early as possible to
 1     testify before the Court.  We have been assured by K87 that he will be in
 2     a position, having seen his doctors next week, to indicate to us when he
 3     would be able to testify.  And our assessment is, based on our
 4     conversations with him, that he should be able to testify in the week
 5     after he sees his doctor.  I won't go into his medical problems.  I think
 6     the return -- the notation on the served subpoena to him from -- just
 7     before the Court would indicate the serious nature or the extent of his
 8     health situation.  So that is on the record.
 9             And in respect to the other witness, Colonel Crosland, we have
10     been in very serious discussions with the UK authorities to get him here
11     and the time he will come, when he will come; and having regard to
12     certain inescapable obligations that he has, he has not been able to give
13     us any date earlier than the 26th of October.  And that presents a
14     problem, Your Honour, in respect to the scheduling, but we have tried, I
15     can assure the Court, and done everything possible to see if he could be
16     fitted in or brought before.  And it is, we are told, the best in the
17     circumstances or the earliest date that can be arranged for him to
18     attend.
19             Your Honour, there is no issue with him in terms of his
20     willingness to come at the present moment; the real issue is when.  And
21     this is a witness who -- who has testified before this Tribunal in at
22     least four other major cases, so there is no issue about his cooperation.
23     And I am assured that had it been feasible for him to attend before, he
24     would have done so or would have indicated so to us.  I mention this,
25     Your Honour, because I -- I don't think and we have not sought a subpoena
 1     for him because he's willing to attend.  It's just that we have been told
 2     by the UK authorities that this is the earliest date that he can.
 3             Those are the reasons for the delay which the Court asks -- well,
 4     which the Prosecution asks the Court to accept and understand.  I know
 5     that we have indicated from the beginning that we do our best to meet the
 6     requirement of the Court that we proceed expeditiously and not waste
 7     time, and that is why I rise just to indicate what the reasons are for
 8     the gaps in the last couple weeks of the Prosecution's case.  May it
 9     please Your Honours.
10             JUDGE PARKER:  Just before you sit, Mr. Stamp, first you've
11     mentioned the witness K87.
12             MR. STAMP:  Yes.
13             JUDGE PARKER:  When is it that you expect to know the outcome of
14     his medical examination?
15             MR. STAMP:  On the 29th, 29th of September.
16             JUDGE PARKER:  Thank you.  And you're hoping to have him
17     available about a week following that?
18             MR. STAMP:  Indeed, Your Honour.
19             JUDGE PARKER:  Yes.  And how long would you anticipate his
20     evidence to be?
21             MR. STAMP:  I --
22             JUDGE PARKER:  Including cross-examination, re-examination?
23             MR. STAMP:  I think two days could be reasonably scheduled for
24     him.
25             JUDGE PARKER:  Now, Mr. Crosland you indicate his availability
 1     not until the 26th of October.  How long would you expect his evidence to
 2     be?
 3             MR. STAMP:  Two days, Your Honours, I think could be ...
 4             JUDGE PARKER:  We would then expect to have reached the end of
 5     evidence by about the 27th/28th of October?
 6             MR. STAMP:  Yes, Your Honours.  As I indicated, there might be
 7     another witness, but if that witness -- if the issues with that witness
 8     cannot be resolved by then, we would be -- we would withdraw that
 9     witness.
10             JUDGE PARKER:  Can I be clear, is that the witness you've not
11     been able to locate --
12             MR. STAMP:  That is the one.
13             JUDGE PARKER:  -- or is there yet a further one?
14             MR. STAMP:  That is the one.
15             JUDGE PARKER:  Yes.
16             Well, we will learn during next week the position about K87 it
17     seems.
18             I'm sure both Defence counsel and Prosecuting counsel realise
19     that this is introducing an unfortunate delay into the progress of the
20     trial.  The Chamber's anxiety is to try and conclude the evidence in this
21     case as quickly as possible.  I appreciate that by some standards we have
22     moved with unusual speed given the amount of evidence that has been heard
23     so far, yet by other standards - and especially in the view of the
24     accused - this matter has now gone on for very many months.  And clearly
25     it is in his interests if not that of the whole Tribunal to conclude the
 1     evidence as quickly as possible.
 2             It's not possible at this moment to make precise date orders, but
 3     can we indicate that in view of the significance of two of the three
 4     witnesses mentioned by Mr. Stamp, that the Chamber feels that it is in
 5     the interests of justice that those witnesses give their evidence and
 6     that, therefore, the Prosecution should have the opportunity of
 7     presenting that evidence.  That may mean that we do not finish until
 8     close to the end of the month of October the Prosecution case.
 9             If that is the way it works out, as seems likely, the Chamber
10     would be aware that it will mean that for most of October both the
11     Prosecution and the Defence will be able to give attention to the next
12     stages of the trial, even though the Prosecution has not formally
13     concluded its case.  Therefore, counsel can expect that the moment we
14     conclude the Prosecution evidence, the Chamber will fix a very tight time
15     schedule for the processes that would follow which will have to be
16     followed before we can commence the Defence case, if there is to be one.
17             The Defence will be giving attention, A, to the question of Rule
18     98 bis, which may or may not commend itself to them.  The Defence will be
19     required, of course, to give attention to the provisions of the Rules
20     stating the Defence case, identifying the Defence witnesses, and
21     identifying Defence exhibits, if it is that the Defence is to lead
22     evidence.  So what the Chamber is saying now, to avoid any unnecessary
23     waste of time those things ought to be looked at through October even
24     though there may still be one or even two witnesses to give evidence.  If
25     I'm correct, the statements of those witnesses have been known, they are
 1     available, so that it will be possible for both the Defence and
 2     Prosecution to form judgements about these other matters; for the
 3     Defence, if it is going that way, to prepare for the eventuality of
 4     leading Defence evidence, et cetera, so that it can be expected that we
 5     will move quickly in early November through those processes.  And should
 6     it be that there is evidence to be led by the Defence, that point will be
 7     reached well in the course of November and will be underway with Defence
 8     evidence if that's the way that it pans out in November.  So that I would
 9     suggest it will be possible to minimise the loss of time without any
10     undue difficulty and without any unfairness to the Defence so that we can
11     get on with the business of reaching the end of this trial so that a
12     decision can be reached, which is I'm sure where the accused wishes us to
13     go as quickly as possible and which is in the interests of justice and of
14     this Tribunal.
15             With those general words, we can't make specific time scheduling
16     orders now.  It certainly may become more possible when we know about the
17     situation of K87, because it seems we can be confident about the dates
18     for Mr. Crosland and it will be possible then to foreshadow a timetable
19     to take us into November; and if we reach that point, to get us to the
20     point of commencing Defence evidence.  It, therefore, seems that what we
21     must do now is adjourn with a view to commencing again on Monday with the
22     evidence of the next witness.  We will do that.
23             We now adjourn.
