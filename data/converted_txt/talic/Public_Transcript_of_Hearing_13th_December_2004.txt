 1                          Monday, 13 December 2004
 2                          [Pre-Trial Conference]
 3                          [Open session]
 4                          [The respondent not present in court]
 5                          --- Upon commencing at 3.02 p.m.
 6            JUDGE KWON:  Good afternoon.  Would the Registrar please call the
 7    case.
 8            THE REGISTRAR:  Case number IT-99-36-R77, the Prosecutor versus
 9    Radoslav Brdjanin concerning allegations against Milka Maglov.
10            JUDGE KWON:  Thank you.  Would both parties make their
11    appearances, please.
12            MS. HOLLIS:  Good afternoon, Your Honours.  My name is Brenda J.
13    Hollis.  I appear as Amicus Curiae Prosecutor by direction of the Trial
14    Chamber originally seized of this matter.  I was appointed Amicus Curiae
15    Prosecutor by decision dated 30 October, 2004 -- 2003.  Excuse me.
16            JUDGE KWON:  Thank you, Ms. Hollis.  For the Defence, please.
17            MR. COOPER:  May it please Your Honours, my name is J. Cooper.  I
18    appear as pro bono counsel for Mrs. Maglov.  I'm joined by Professor Peter
19    Murphy, co-counsel, and Virginia Lindsay who is our senior legal
20    consultant.
21            JUDGE KWON:  Thank you.  This is a Pre-Trial Conference being held
22    pursuant to Rule 73 bis to discuss various matters prior to the
23    commencement of trial.  I shall briefly lay out the procedural history of
24    the case.
25            This case arose out of an alleged contempt by Ms. Milka Maglov in
 1    her capacity as a member of the Defence team for Mr. Radoslav Brdjanin,
 2    and the case was initially assigned to the Trial Chamber II, the Chamber
 3    hearing that trial against Mr. Brdjanin.
 4            In the same Chamber, a contempt trial against Ms. Maglov commenced
 5    on 16th February of 2004, and upon completion of the Prosecution's case
 6    after four days' hearing, the proceedings were adjourned to allow the
 7    Trial Chamber to determine a 98 bis motion.  Thereafter, the proceedings
 8    was scheduled to resume on 20th of July, 2004.  However, a continuance
 9    based upon medical reasons was granted.  In the meantime, following the
10    issuance of the judgement in the case against Mr. Brdjanin and the
11    completion of the mandate of the two ad litem Judges, the composition of
12    the Trial Chamber was dissolved and, thereafter, on 22nd of September, the
13    present President assigned this case to this Chamber.
14            Having briefly said the procedural history of the case, I now turn
15    to the issues in relation to this Pre-Trial Conference.
16            The first and foremost important issue will be whether this trial
17    should be a trial de novo or it should be a continuation of the prior
18    proceedings.  This Chamber is of the view that the continuation of the
19    prior proceedings would only be possible when both parties - the Defence
20    in particular - would agree to it.  However, it has been brought to the
21    Chamber's attention by the Senior Legal Officer, who held the 65 ter
22    Conference, that the Defence is opposed to the continuation of prior
23    proceedings.  In addition, on the part of the Amicus Curiae Prosecutor,
24    we've also been informed that should there be a trial de novo, there would
25    be some difficulty on the part of the Prosecutor in calling the witnesses
 1    again for testimony.
 2            So shall we hear from the parties on this matter first.  Who
 3    should begin first?
 4            Yes, Mrs. Hollis.
 5            MS. HOLLIS:  Thank you, Your Honour.  Your Honour, I would like to
 6    begin on the assumption that the proceedings would be de novo proceedings.
 7    Although I believe there is an argument to be made for a continuation, I
 8    believe that in an abundance of caution, de novo proceedings would be the
 9    better approach.
10            Given that there were to be de novo proceedings, then I as the
11    Amicus Curiae Prosecutor would request the Chamber in fact not to proceed
12    with this as a criminal matter.  I would request that this Trial Chamber
13    would instead vacate the orders of the prior Trial Chamber which moved
14    this matter forward as a criminal matter.  And I believe, Your Honour,
15    that those orders were dated initially the 15th of April, 2003, where the
16    Trial Chamber ordered that they determined that this matter would proceed
17    on two original charges as a criminal matter.  Then on the 6th of
18    February, 2004, the original Trial Chamber approved the amended
19    allegations to move forward as a criminal matter.
20            Were I the Prosecutor in the normal course of proceedings before
21    this Tribunal, I would at this point be making a motion pursuant to Rule
22    of Procedure And Evidence 51(A)(iii) and Rule 73(A) that the indictment be
23    withdrawn.  Since these charges are before you not in the normal procedure
24    before this Tribunal, I am making the request instead that you vacate the
25    two prior orders and that this matter be dealt with administratively.
 1            JUDGE KWON:  Ms. Hollis, if you could elaborate a bit more on the
 2    reasons why this should not be continued on the basis of criminal
 3    procedures.
 4            MS. HOLLIS:  Thank you, Your Honour.  Your Honour, in a proceeding
 5    de novo, the Prosecution of course would be required to put its case on
 6    once again.  The three primary fact witnesses to these allegations were
 7    extremely reluctant to come forward during the last hearing.  In
 8    particular, the alleged victim referred to as "Witness" was extremely
 9    concerned about coming forward both for security reasons and for privacy
10    reasons.  This witness had been victimised and traumatised by events
11    during the conflict in Bosnia, had encountered precarious security
12    conditions after the conflict in Bosnia, and had been traumatised by
13    events that occurred that surround these allegations regardless of how
14    respondent's might be characterised.  This person was extremely reluctant
15    to come forward, and at the conclusion of testimony indicated a wish that
16    there be no further involvement on the part of this witness in any of
17    these proceedings.
18            In addition to that, two other important fact witnesses were also
19    reluctant.  One of them, again for security reasons, dealing with a fear
20    that both personal and professional adverse consequences could flow from
21    that person's appearance here; and the third person was reluctant because
22    of treatment that person had endured during the conflict and because of
23    very strongly felt privacy concerns about coming forward.
24            Given these positions and the fact that despite these grave
25    concerns all three persons came forward and testified, I would suggest to
 1    you that it would be contrary to the interests of justice to require so
 2    reluctant a group of witness who have already come forward to once again
 3    come forward despite their concerns.  And, Your Honours, this is the basis
 4    upon which I would ask you to take the action that I have requested.
 5            JUDGE KWON:  Thank you, Ms. Hollis.
 6            What would you say to that on the part of the Defence?
 7            MR. COOPER:  Your Honours, we're very grateful to Ms. Hollis for
 8    her submissions, and we very respectfully support her submissions.
 9            We do agree that it would be appropriate for any trial to operate
10    de novo, and we say that bearing in mind our acquaintanceship with the
11    proceedings thus far.  In earlier pleadings, which I don't rehearse here,
12    the Defence for Ms. Maglov had set out very extensively what they
13    considered to have been shortcomings in those proceedings.  Ms. Maglov
14    herself was not represented by counsel, and she conducted
15    cross-examination herself.
16            In our submission, there could be no substitute for this Trial
17    Chamber proceeding with this matter de novo, there being no substitute for
18    the Trial Chamber itself being able to see the witnesses, to see their
19    demeanour, and to raise its own questions as well as having the assistance
20    of questions raised on Mrs. Maglov's behalf by counsel.
21            So for those reasons, in relation to the first point, we
22    respectfully do agree that the only fair, fully fair procedure to operate
23    now and henceforth, if a trial were to proceed, would be a trial de novo.
24            Having said that, we hear Ms. Hollis's submissions in relation to
25    the witness and indeed all of the witnesses.  Those are plainly matters in
 1    which the Amicus Prosecutor must look with great care and indeed so must
 2    the Trial Chamber.  Those are matters about which I myself don't wish to
 3    say anything further.
 4            We support the application which is effectively being made to this
 5    Trial Chamber.
 6            JUDGE KWON:  Thank you, Mr. Cooper.
 7            Ms. Hollis, your submission to withdraw the indictment actually
 8    means that a request to vacate the order on the part of the Trial Chamber,
 9    and if you could help me, what order it is to be vacated.  Was it -- is it
10    the order of the 6th of February of this year, or which order the
11    commencement of the contempt trial?
12            MS. HOLLIS:  Thank you, Your Honour.  In my submission, there
13    would really be two orders that were involved.  The order which originally
14    moved this matter forward as a criminal matter, and I believe as I
15    indicated the date of that order was 15 April 2003.  That was the order
16    that moved this matter forward on the two original allegations.
17            I believe secondarily the order of 6 February 2004 would also be
18    vacated, and that was the order, I believe, that approved the requested
19    amendments to those allegations and moved the proceeding forward based on
20    those amended allegations.  So in my submission, it would be necessary to
21    vacate both those orders.
22            JUDGE KWON:  Thank you, Ms. Hollis.  The Chamber will consult on
23    the matter.
24                          [Trial Chamber confers]
25            JUDGE KWON:  Judge El Mahdi will put some questions to Ms. Hollis.
 1            JUDGE EL MAHDI:  Thank you, Mr. President.
 2             [Interpretation] Mrs. Hollis, I listened with great interest to
 3    your explanations about the reasons why the witness, who is also a victim,
 4    is reluctant to come and testify, but I would like you to clarify
 5    something for me.  I would like to have your impression.  Did you feel
 6    that his position not to come back to testify before the honourable
 7    Tribunal, did you feel that it was because he had relived or thought about
 8    the fear he experienced during -- with the visit that -- when Mrs. Maglov
 9    went to visit him?  So in other words, did you think that he thought back,
10    he thought of all these -- the fear he experienced at that time, the
11    intimidation?  Because at the time, if I understand correctly, at the
12    specific time he didn't feel that it was a matter of intimidation, but
13    only afterwards, when he thought about it later on.  That's when, later.
14    That's when he came to the conclusion that maybe there was an attempt at
15    intimidation by Mrs. Maglov.  So I'd like to have your personal opinion
16    about that matter, please.
17            MS. HOLLIS:  Yes, Your Honour.  For this explanation, could we go
18    into closed session?
19            JUDGE KWON:  Yes, private session.

15                          [Open session]
16            JUDGE KWON:  Yes.  We are now in public session.
17            The Amicus Curiae Prosecutor has made an oral motion to this Trial
18    Chamber to vacate the order of Trial Chamber II, dated 15th of April,
19    2003, and 6th of February, 2004, which ordered to prosecute Ms. Maglov for
20    the contempt charges.
21            In view of the submission by the Amicus Curiae Prosecutor and lack
22    of opposition from the Defence or, rather, its consent, and considering
23    the procedural history of this case and all circumstances surrounding this
24    case, the Chamber hereby grants the motion and vacates the orders.
25            These proceedings are hereby terminated, and this hearing is now
 1    adjourned.
 2            MS. HOLLIS:  Your Honour.
 3            JUDGE KWON:  Yes, Ms. Hollis.
 4            MS. HOLLIS:  Thank you, Your Honour.  May it please the Court, we
 5    appreciate your timely ruling on this request.  At the same time, I would
 6    like for the record, at least, to deal with two other issues that arise
 7    from the conduct in question here, and I have spoken with the Defence
 8    about these issues.
 9            JUDGE KWON:  Yes, please.
10            MS. HOLLIS:  Thank you, Your Honour.
11            Your Honour, the first issue is what further action might be taken
12    administratively regarding this matter, and in that regard, Your Honour,
13    and I believe in substance, the Defence would not oppose this, the first
14    thing the Prosecution would ask, would like to put on the record is that
15    the Registry consider with great care any future applications by this
16    respondent to appear before this Tribunal in any capacity, taking into
17    consideration the circumstances surrounding her removal as co-counsel in
18    the Brdjanin case as well as the circumstances surrounding these
19    allegations.  That would be the first submission.
20            The second submission is that in view of the Amicus Prosecutor,
21    these events raise grave concerns regarding an essential matter, and
22    perhaps there is need for guidance or clarification by some appropriate
23    body at the ICTY regarding this matter, and that is to say regardless of
24    how one were to characterise the conduct of the respondent, it appears to
25    the Amicus Prosecutor that her conduct brings into question what guidance
 1    exists or what standard should exist to guide both parties to these
 2    proceedings or any proceedings as to how they can initiate contact with
 3    persons who may have relevant information in a way that would protect that
 4    person's security and privacy concerns.
 5            In that regard in relation to this matter, the Prosecution would
 6    suggest that one thing, one obligation the parties would have would be to
 7    familiarise themselves with materials they had been given, which might
 8    make them aware of persons, circumstances and potential security and
 9    privacy concerns.  In particular, I speak to the disclosure materials that
10    had been made available to the Defence, and according to evidence in the
11    trial had been made available to this respondent, but for a variety
12    reasons, materials which this respondent had not fully familiarised
13    herself.
14            So an issue would be what fundamental procedures should the
15    parties take that their contacts with people do not jeopardise those
16    people or traumatise them, and I suggest this is a matter that should be
17    considered by the appropriate body within the Tribunal and that input from
18    both the Office of the Prosecutor and the Defence bar as well as the
19    Victim and Witnesses Unit should be sought and that some sort of guidance
20    should be provided so that perhaps we could avoid this kind of conduct in
21    the future.
22            Thank you, Your Honour.
23            JUDGE KWON:  Thank you, Ms. Hollis.
24            Yes, Mr. Cooper.
25            MR. COOPER:  Your Honours, might I address you in relation to
 1    those matters that have been raised.  In essence, Ms. Hollis makes two
 2    submissions to Your Honours.  One is a matter of practice and the second
 3    is a matter of policy.
 4            As to the matter of practice, may I say that we have no objection
 5    at all to the form of words that has been proposed by Ms. Hollis in
 6    relation to any future application by our client to come onto the Rule 45
 7    list of counsel.  Plainly any application will be looked at with the
 8    Registrar with great care, and this will be no different.
 9            In that context, I should say that pursuant to this Trial
10    Chamber's order, these proceedings will have resolved without an
11    adjudication on the facts, and we make clear that Mrs. Maglov has at no
12    stage made any admission, and she makes no admission of criminal conduct
13    on her part whatsoever.
14            The other matter that I should raise in relation to practice is
15    that it is right that Mrs. Maglov has taken every possible opportunity
16    within these proceedings to express in the clearest terms her own regret
17    if her conduct had inadvertently caused discomfort for Witness W, and on
18    her behalf, I have no hesitation at all in these proceedings of repeating
19    the regret that she herself has expressed.
20            As to policy matters, I'm going to ask Professor Murphy to address
21    those.
22            MR. MURPHY:  Your Honours, as regards Ms. Hollis's submissions
23    about the need for clarity and guidance, we would wish to be associated
24    with those observations.
25            This case has caused a great deal of anxiety not just for
 1    Mrs. Maglov but I think it right to say for the Defence bar as a whole,
 2    because it raised the question of what Defence counsel could or could not
 3    properly do in order to approach and interview a witness.
 4            Your Honours will be aware that there are two decisions of the
 5    Appeals Chamber in which it is suggested that it is the duty of Defence
 6    counsel to approach and interview witnesses as may be necessary without
 7    any restrictions.  However, clearly that has to be read in the light of
 8    the matters affecting privacy to which Ms. Hollis has alluded, and we
 9    feel, Your Honour, that this may be an appropriate matter for the Rules
10    Committee at some point to look at with the input of the Office of the
11    Prosecutor, the Defence bar, and Chambers, of course, and also the Victims
12    and Witness Unit.  So to that extent, Your Honour, both we and Mrs. Maglov
13    support the -- any efforts that may be made to bring some clarity and
14    guidance to that situation.
15            JUDGE KWON:  Thank you.  Thank you for that timely and appropriate
16    observation from both parties.  Those having been put on record, I don't
17    think there's anything we can deal with right now.
18            So this hearing is now adjourned.
