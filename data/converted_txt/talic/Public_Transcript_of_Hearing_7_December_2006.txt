24    criminal plan or purpose, may be comprised of members none of whom
25    physically carry out crimes but who use other people to do so.  This means
 1    that a JCE may exist entirely at a leadership level.  If such persons have
 2    agreed amongst themselves on a common criminal plan and carried out
 3    through others, they can be liable under JCE.  There's no need to even
 4    inquire into whether the physical perpetrators agreed to or even knew of
 5    their criminal plan.
 6            One important goal of international criminal law is to punish
 7    those most responsible for crimes and to accurately reflect their role in
 8    terms of the law.  Requiring that physical perpetrators must be in the JCE
 9    does not reflect how international crimes typically occur by a multitude
10    of people orchestrated or coordinated by those who are the most
11    responsible at a senior -- a hierarchically senior level.
12            Those on the higher level who have agreed on the criminal goal,
13    they don't carry out the crimes themselves.  Instead, they implement their
14    plan by using the means at their disposal; the army, the police units,
15    civilian authorities, paramilitaries and the like.  And those high-level
16    people will usually be physically remote from the actual perpetration of
17    the crimes.
18            While the physical perpetrators may often agree with the purpose
19    of the high-level perpetrators, the high-level people, the reality is that
20    very often the high-level perpetrators make the decisions and then use the
21    physical perpetrators as a way to implement those decisions.  And
22    moreover, in other cases high-level perpetrators may in fact use physical
23    perpetrators who do not even necessarily adopt the criminal purpose, or it
24    can't be proved that they adopted the criminal purpose, yet through their
25    combined actions the high-level accused exactly achieve their criminal
 1    goal.
 2            Ordering, planning, instigating, Article 7(3) liability, these may
 3    apply, but they don't always capture the true situation and the true
 4    culpability of the high-level offenders.
 5            On the other hand, using aiding and abetting to characterise these
 6    high-level accused is inappropriate.  Not only does this mode of liability
 7    incur a lesser degree of culpability in this Tribunal, it denies full
 8    responsibility for commission.
 9            In our submission, it's contrary to the objects and purposes of
10    international criminal law that commission liability should pivot or
11    depend on whether physical perpetrators were in the JCE and that the
12    liability of senior leaders should depend on what is or is not in the mind
13    of soldiers or policemen on the ground.  The focus must be on the mind and
14    the acts of the senior leaders, and the doctrine should apply when these
15    JCE members further the common criminal plan by using the others as tools
16    to carry out their crimes.

18    the culpability principle by placing criminal responsibility squarely on
19    the shoulders of those who are or should be the most responsible.
