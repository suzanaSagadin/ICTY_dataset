17     Do you get translation?

19             JUDGE LATTANZI: [Interpretation] Thank you.  Could you please
20     give us your name, full name, for the transcript.
21             THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] My name is Ljubisa Petkovic, born
22     on the 29th of November, 1952, in a town called Deronje, Odzaci
23     municipality, Republic of Serbia.
24             JUDGE LATTANZI: [Interpretation] Thank you.  Your family was
25     informed that you were transferred to the UN Detention Unit?
 1             THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] Yes.  My wife was there when I
 2     surrendered.
 3             JUDGE LATTANZI: [Interpretation] Thank you.
 4             Does the counsel want to make any comments regarding this job as
 5     assigned counsel for Mr. Petkovic?
 6             MR. GUY-SMITH:  At this time I can inform the Court of the
 7     following:  I had a very limited conversation with Mr. Petkovic yesterday
 8     afternoon.  It was unfortunately truncated because there was an incident
 9     at the prison totally unrelated to anything involving anyone at the
10     United Nations Detention Unit, whereby we were not allowed entry into the
11     prison for over an hour, which unfortunately limited our time because the
12     Registrar had to speak to Mr. Petkovic before I had the opportunity to
13     discuss matters with him.  However, we did have a modicum of success in
14     that conversation, and we are able at this point in time to proceed with
15     limited issues as they relate to the order in lieu of indictment which
16     has been rendered against him and some issues concerning potential
17     provisional release as well as potential disclosure issues and anything
18     else that the Chamber may wish to have me address.
19             With regard to his presence here, I think the Chamber understands
20     as Mr. Petkovic has just indicated that he attempted to surrender to the
21     authorities for purposes of dealing with these particular charges, but I
22     will -- I'm guided -- I'm guided by Your Honour as to how you wish to
23     proceed.  I'm more than happy to discuss whatever Your Honour wishes to
24     discuss.
25             JUDGE LATTANZI: [Interpretation] Mr. Petkovic, do you have
 1     anything to add regarding your counsel, your representation?
 2             THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] I have no complaints whatsoever.
 3             JUDGE LATTANZI: [Interpretation] Thank you.  We can then move on.
 4             My name is Judge Flavia Lattanzi.  I was appointed for this
 5     initial appearance by an order issued by the Chamber in charge of this
 6     case dated May 28, 2008.  This Trial Chamber is also made up of Judge
 7     Jean-Claude Antonetti, Presiding Judge, and Judge Frederik Harhoff.
 8             Mr. Ljubisa Petkovic, born on November 29, 1952, in Deronje in
 9     Serbia, is accused of having deliberately obstructed justice by refusing
10     to abide by the subpoena that had been served to him, subpoena as a
11     Chamber witness, served to him on May 13 2008.  This subpoena had been
12     issued on April 7, 2008, in the first place, was served to Mr. Petkovic
13     on April 18, 2008, pursuant to Rule 97(A)(iii) [as interpreted].  In
14     exercising its power, the Tribunal can convict for contempt any person
15     wilfully and knowingly interfering the administration of justice,
16     including any person who voluntarily does not abide by a subpoena and
17     refuses to appear as a witness summoned by a Chamber.
18             MR. GUY-SMITH:  Excuse me, Your Honour I'm reviewing the
19     transcript as it is being produced.  I believe that there is a
20     translation error on page 3, line 13, with regard to the Rule.
21             JUDGE LATTANZI: [Interpretation] Yes, you're absolutely right.
22     This is Rule 77, not 97.  Rule 77.
23             Mr. Petkovic, were you served the order in lieu of indictment in
24     a language that you understand?
25             THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] Yes, I was.
 1             JUDGE LATTANZI: [Interpretation] And have you understood the
 2     content of this document?
 3             THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] Yes, I have understood the content
 4     on page 4 of the subpoena under item 4 of the Serbian version, case
 5     number 3/29322 ter.  By your leave, I can read this.  I will not be
 6     mentioning the name mentioned in the document.  Under item 4 it says that
 7     the authorities of the Republic of Serbia need to inform immediately the
 8     International Tribunal about the passport details.  I don't want to
 9     mention the name of this person.  This does not pertain to me.  I don't
10     know if this person is going to testify here or not.  A mistake was made
11     in these details, and I do not wish to mention the name.
12             MR. GUY-SMITH:  Your Honour, if I might, I think we -- I think we
13     might be dealing with two separate documents at this time.  I believe --
14             JUDGE LATTANZI: [Interpretation] Yes.  I was -- I wanted to tell
15     Mr. Petkovic that I was talking about the Order in Lieu of an Indictment,
16     and I think you were talking about another document.  What document are
17     you talking about?  Maybe the subpoena, the summons.
18             THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] Dated the 7th of April, 2008.
19             MR. GUY-SMITH:  If I might be of some assistance here, Your
20     Honour, with regard to the question that you asked Mr. Petkovic.  If I
21     might be of some assistance, I can inform Your Honour that yesterday
22     evening I specifically spoke to Mr. Petkovic about the document that's
23     entitled Order in Lieu of an Indictment for Contempt Against Ljubisa
24     Petkovic, and I was informed that he had that document in his own
25     language.  I don't know if that's of any assistance or not, but perhaps
 1     that would be of some assistance if not to you to Mr. Petkovic so that we
 2     can focus on the document that the Court is inquiring about which
 3     obviously are the charges against him.
 4             JUDGE LATTANZI: [Interpretation] Yes.  I believe that
 5     Mr. Petkovic was talking about the subpoena.  So this -- everything that
 6     deals with this other person, I think we will set it aside for a moment
 7     and we will deal with it later on when we sit with a full Bench.
 8             So let's come back to the order in lieu of an indictment.  You
 9     obtained it in your own language, and you said that you understood the
10     content of this document.  Is this true?
11             THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] Yes.  I received this document two
12     days ago -- or perhaps it was yesterday morning before my plane took off.
13     Not earlier than that.
14             JUDGE LATTANZI: [Interpretation] Very well.  During this hearing
15     regarding this order in lieu of an indictment for contempt, you are
16     entitled to have of this document read in full.  Do you want us to read
17     this order in full or would you rather waive this right?
18             THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] I have understood it, and there is
19     no need for you to read it again.
20             JUDGE LATTANZI: [Interpretation] Thank you, Mr. Petkovic.  I will
21     now explain what -- how we're going to proceed.  Pursuant to Rule
22     62(A)(ii) in -- in the Rules, you could either plead guilty or not guilty
23     today, or you could also decide to postpone your plea for 30 days.  Your
24     counsel must have explained this to you.  Did you talk with your counsel
25     about the plea, entering a plea?
 1             THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] Yes.
 2             JUDGE LATTANZI: [Interpretation] Everything is perfectly clear in
 3     your mind?
 4             THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] Yes, I can.
 5             JUDGE LATTANZI: [Interpretation] Therefore, you decide to enter a
 6     plea today, guilty or not guilty, or would you rather postpone it for
 7     some time, 30 days -- up to 30 days?  It's up to you to decide what you
 8     want to do.
 9             THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] I wish to plead today.
10             JUDGE LATTANZI: [Interpretation] Mr. Petkovic, could you please
11     stand up then.
12             Mr. Petkovic, you are accused of contempt of court under Rule
13     77(A)(iii) of the Rules.  What is your plea, guilty or not guilty?
14             THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] I plead not guilty.
15             JUDGE LATTANZI: [Interpretation] Very well.  Then, Mr. Petkovic,
16     a date for trial will be set later on by the Registry.  You may sit down.
17             THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] Thank you.
18             JUDGE LATTANZI: [Interpretation] I would like to tell you that we
19     will -- this procedure will be as short as possible, and I would like
20     your counsel to tell us how much time you need.
21             Mr. Guy-Smith, how much time do you need to prepare for the
22     defence of your client?
23             MR. GUY-SMITH:  Well, before I can quite frankly intelligently
24     address that particular question, Your Honour, there's information that I
25     need to have that I don't have at this time.  There are a series of
 1     filings which I understand are confidential that I need receipt of.
 2     There are some reports and orders that are in fact contained in the Order
 3     in Lieu of the Indictment that I need to have copies of.  I'm not sure
 4     whether all of these documents are in English at this time.  If they're
 5     not, I would need them translated.
 6             Apart from that, from what I understand because I am yet unclear
 7     about precisely who was involved with regard to the service of the
 8     summons.  I understand there is some history behind the relationship of
 9     this particular witness in the court.  All of those documents I do not
10     have either in terms of any other prior contact that existed between the
11     parties.  I believe there was some other correspondence or motions that
12     were filed that were potentially confidential.  And with regard to
13     anything concerning Mr. Petkovic that has been previously or continues to
14     be confidential, I would ask that all of those matters that the
15     confidentiality be lifted or in the alternative if the Chamber feels it
16     appropriate to maintain confidentiality that I be supplied with all of
17     that documentation so that I'm in a position to intelligently make a
18     determination of what the contours are of this particular case.
19             I'm somewhat in an awkward position.  I would normally be asking
20     this question obviously of my opponent or the Prosecutor, and I would not
21     be directing this particular kind of request to the Chamber, but since
22     understand it's the Chamber that's taken upon itself in these
23     proceedings, I'm making that request, and I mean no disrespect
24     whatsoever.
25             Above and beyond, above and beyond that particular documentation
 1     from the limited conversation I've had with Mr. Petkovic, I believe that
 2     there are potentially a number of witnesses who are germane specifically
 3     to the issue before the Court.  Whether or not that is accurate, meaning
 4     that the Chamber would deem those witnesses to be relevant to the
 5     gravamen of the charges or whether or not the Chamber would deem those
 6     witnesses to be relevant to other issues assuming that there was a
 7     finding of culpability is another matter that I need to investigate.
 8             The reason I'm mentioning all of this is because I don't want to
 9     irresponsibly make a determination of how long it would take.  I can't
10     see that I can get this all done realistically based upon other
11     commitments that I have coupled with what I understand the case to be,
12     which will include the necessity of a potential investigator and a
13     translator in going to the area for a moment at least.  I don't believe
14     that I could responsibly perform my duties to Mr. Petkovic in anything
15     less than 45 days.
16             What I think might be appropriate is that we have an expedited
17     Status Conference in a very short period of time at which point I
18     could -- I could at that point give you a report of what I believe is a
19     realistic trial date, because I am in agreement with -- with you that
20     once this matter does go to trial or in the event it does go to trial
21     that it is a relatively brief proceeding, but there is -- that is bit of
22     preparation that needs to be done beforehand, that might be the most
23     responsive way of dealing with it.  And by a short Status Conference, I
24     mean something perhaps within the next two or three weeks.  That would be
25     my suggestion at the moment, and I know I haven't answered your question
 1     directly which is how much time do I need, but I'm trying to do the best
 2     I can within the context of the information I have at the present time.
 3             JUDGE LATTANZI: [Interpretation] In that case, we will look into
 4     this matter at a later stage.
 5             Could we now move into private session, please.

 1     need as well as conditions, however, I'm not positive whether or not I'm

 8     would appreciate it.
