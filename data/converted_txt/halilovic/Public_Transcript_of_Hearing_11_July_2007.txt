23            Let me now turn to a discussion about the reason to know.
24    Mr. Guenael Mettraux in his submissions postulated a legal test for when a
25    superior is obliged to take action to prevent, and it really involves the
 1    question of when is a superior on notice of the risk of future criminal
 2    conduct, such as to put him or to crystallise the duty to act to prevent
 3    the crimes.
 4            The Prosecution's position is that the Hadzihasanovic Trial
 5    Chamber's formulation of the risk threshold is correct.  They say it is a
 6    real and reasonable or real and reasonably foreseeable risk of crimes
 7    which triggers the superior's duty to take steps.  Our submission is that
 8    the risk need not be as high as substantial likelihood, as put forward by
 9    Mr. Mettraux, which has been the risk standard adopted for 7(1) modes of
10    liability.  We say that a different threshold is justified by the special
11    nature of superior responsibility, that the position held by a superior
12    and the unique degree of control that a superior exercises over the
13    actions of the perpetrator makes it appropriate for a superior to act in
14    the face of any real or reasonably foreseeable risk.  And this standard is
15    consistent with Appeals Chamber case law on superior responsibility as --
16    including the Bagilishema and Celebici appeals.  I refer you to Celebici
17    at 238 and Bagilishema at paragraph 28.  I also refer you to the Krnojelac
18    Appeals Chamber decision at 155, 160, 169, 170, and 178.
19            The reason we say that the superior should be held to this
20    standard is that requiring proof that a superior had notice of a certainty
21    or a near certainty of future crimes would significantly undermine the
22    superior responsibility doctrine.  It would permit a superior to sit back,
23    do nothing, and wait until an obviously risky situation escalates into an
24    actual or imminent commission of crimes.  Requiring a superior to act in
25    the face of a real and reasonable risk puts the onus on a superior to
 1    intervene early, in order to supervise and to take the reasonable and
 2    necessary measures to control and prevent crime.  The superiors have a
 3    responsibility of enforcing the law of armed conflict as the prevention of
 4    wartime atrocities depends on the conscientious supervision of
 5    subordinates.  It is our submission that to require such a high standard
 6    as put forward by counsel for Mr. Halilovic would be inappropriate.
 7    Superiors must be required to prevent the realisation of a real and
 8    reasonable risk of crimes.
