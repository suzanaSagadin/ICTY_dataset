14    Intelligence of the Bratunac Brigade.  He was present in the Bratunac
15    Brigade zone of responsibility during the attack on the Srebrenica enclave

 9            General allegations:
10            At all times relevant to this indictment, a state of armed
11    conflict existed in the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina.
12            At all relevant times, the accused was required to abide by the
13    laws and customs governing the conduct of war.
14            All acts and omissions charged as crimes against humanity were
15    part of a widespread or systematic attack directed against the Bosnian
16    Muslim civilian population of Srebrenica and its surroundings.
17            Factual background:
18            On 12 May 1992, Momcilo Krajisnik, President of the RS National
19    Assembly, executed the following "Decision of Strategic Objectives of the
20    Serbian People in Bosnia and Herzegovina"; this Decision was published in
21    the Official Gazette of the Republika Srpska on 26 November 1993:
22            "The strategic objectives or priorities of the Serbian people in
23    Bosnia and Herzegovina are to:
24            1. Establish state borders separating the Serbian people from the
25    other two ethnic communities.
 1            2. Set up a corridor between Semberija and Krajina.
 2            3. Establish a corridor in the Drina river valley, that is,
 3    eliminate the Drina as border separating Serbia States.
 4            4. Establish a border on the Una and Neretva Rivers.
 5            5. Divide the city of Sarajevo into Serbia and Bosnian Muslim
 6    parts and establish effective State authorities in both parts.
 7            6. Ensure access to the sea for Republika Srpska."
 8            After armed conflict erupted in the Republic of Bosnia and
 9    Herzegovina, in the spring of 1992, Bosnian Serb military and paramilitary
10    forces attacked and occupied cities, towns, and villages, including
11    Zvornik in the eastern part of the country and participated in ethnic
12    cleansing campaign which resulted in an exodus of Bosnian Muslim civilians
13    to enclaves in Srebrenica, Gorazde, and Zepa.
14            On 19 November 1992, General Ratko Mladic, the Commander of the
15    VRS Main Staff, issued Operational Directive 04.  This Directive, in part,
16    ordered the Drina Corps to "... inflict the heaviest possible losses on
17    the enemy, and force him to leave the Birac, Zepa, and Gorazde areas
18    together with the Bosnian Muslim population.  First, offer the able-bodied
19    and armed men to surrender, and if they refuse, destroy them."
20            On 16 April 1993, the Security Council of the United Nations,
21    acting pursuant to Chapter VII of its chapter, adopted Resolution 819, in
22    which it demanded that all parties to the conflict in the Republic of
23    Bosnia and Herzegovina treat Srebrenica and its surroundings as a "safe
24    area" which was to be free from any armed attack or any other hostile
25    act.
 1            On 4 July 1994, Lieutenant Colonel Slavko Ognjenovic, then
 2    Commander of the Bratunac Brigade, issued a report to all members of the
 3    Bratunac Brigade, stating in relevant part:  "We must continue to arm,
 4    train, discipline, and prepare the RS Army for the execution of this
 5    crucial task -- the expulsion of Muslims from the Srebrenica enclave.
 6    There will be no retreat when it comes to the Srebrenica enclave.  We must
 7    advance.  The enemy's life has to be made unbearable and their temporary
 8    stay in the enclave impossible so that they leave the enclave en masse as
 9    soon as possible, realising that they cannot survive there."
10            On 8 March 1995, the Supreme Command of the armed forces of the
11    Republika Srpska issued Operational Directive 07.  In this Directive,
12    Republika Srpska President Radovan Karadzic directed the VRS (specifically
13    the VRS Drina Corps) to "... complete the physical separation of the
14    Srebrenica and Zepa enclaves as soon as possible, preventing even
15    communication between individuals between the two enclaves.  By planned
16    and well-thought-out combat operations, create an unbearable situation of
17    total insecurity, with no hope of further survival or life for the
18    inhabitants of Srebrenica or Zepa."
19            On 2 July 1995, in the Drina Corps operational order for the
20    attack on the Srebrenica enclave, General Milenko Zivanovic ordered that
21    the attack of the enclave should result in "reducing the enclave to its
22    urban area."  The size of the enclave on 2 July 1995 was approximately 58
23    square kilometres and the urban area of the enclave was about 2 square
24    kilometres.  Large numbers of the Bosnian Muslim population of the enclave
25    lived outside the urban area of Srebrenica prior to 2 July 1995.
 1            On or about 6 July 1995, units of the Drina Corps shelled
 2    Srebrenica and attacked Dutch-manned United Nations observation posts
 3    which were located in the enclave.  The Drina Corps attack on the
 4    Srebrenica enclave, including the shelling, continued through 11 July
 5    1995, when forces from the Zvornik Brigade's Drina Wolves, the Bratunac
 6    Brigade, and 10th Sabotage Detachment and other units of the VRS entered
 7    Srebrenica.
 8            In the several days following this attack on Srebrenica, VRS
 9    forces captured, detained, summarily executed, and buried over 7.000
10    Bosnian Muslim men and boys from the Srebrenica enclave, and forcibly
11    transferred the Bosnian Muslim women and children of Srebrenica out of the
12    enclave.  The details of these events and the role of each accused therein
13    are presented in the paragraphs below.
14            Individual criminal responsibility:
15            Direct criminal responsibility:
16            Pursuant to Article 7(1) of the Statute of the Tribunal, Momir
17    Nikolic is individually responsible for genocide or complicity in
18    genocide, crime against humanity (murder, persecutions, forcible transfer,
19    and inhumane acts), and murder as a violation of the laws or customs of
20    war.  Momir Nikolic committed, planned, instigated, ordered, and otherwise
21    aided and abetted in the planning, preparation, and execution of these
22    charged crimes.  By using the word "committed" in this indictment, the
23    Prosecutor does not intend to suggest that the accused necessarily
24    physically and personally perpetrated any of the crimes charged.
25    "Committing" the crimes can be established by virtue of participation in
 1    the joint criminal enterprise.
 2            Joint criminal enterprise:
 3            Momir Nikolic, together with other VRS and MUP officers and units
 4    as identified in this indictment, was a member of and knowingly
 5    participated in a joint criminal enterprise, the common purpose of which
 6    was: to forcibly transfer the women and children from the Srebrenica
 7    enclave to Kladanj, on 12 July and 13 July 1995; and to capture, detain,
 8    and summarily execute by firing squad, bury, and rebury thousands of
 9    Bosnian Muslim men and boys aged 16 to 60 from the Srebrenica enclave from
10    12 July 1995 until and about 19 July 1995.  The last known primary burial
11    of Srebrenica victims occurred on or about 19 July 1995 in Glogova.  The
12    initial plan was to summarily execute more than 1.000 Bosnian Muslim men
13    and boys, aged 16 to 60, who were separated from the group of Bosnian
14    Muslims in Potocari on 12 and 13 July.  On 12 July, this plan was
15    broadened to include the summary execution of over 6.000 men and boys,
16    aged 16 to 60, who were captured from the column of Bosnian Muslim men
17    escaping the Srebrenica enclave on 12 of July through about 19 July 1995.
18    Most of these men and boys from the column were captured along the
19    Bratunac-Milici road on 13 July, 1995.  While the joint criminal
20    enterprise contemplated organised and systematic executions, it was
21    foreseeable to Momir Nikolic that opportunistic criminal acts, such as
22    those described in this indictment, would be carried out by VRS and MUP
23    forces during and after the joint criminal enterprise.  VRS and MUP forces
24    carried out such opportunistic criminal acts from 12 of July 1995 to about
25    1 November 1995.  The implementation of this joint criminal enterprise
 1    resulted in the summary execution of over 7.000 Bosnian Muslim men and
 2    boys from the Srebrenica enclave.
 3            Momir Nikolic possessed the criminal intent and state of mind

14            (a) killed members of the group by summary execution as described

17            Immediately after the fall of Srebrenica on 11 July 1995, senior
18    VRS officers including Ratko Mladic and Radislav Krstic surveyed the

21            Thousands of Bosnian Muslims from the enclave, including women,
22    children, and some men, fled to the UN compound in Potocari on 11 July

10    Nikolic, and with the DutchBat command.  At the first meeting, Ratko
11    Mladic intimidated and threatened the DutchBat commander.  The second
12    meeting was convened by Ratko Mladic, Radislav Krstic, Momir Nikolic, and
13    other members of the VRS at approximately 2300 hours on 11 July, and was
14    attended by members of the DutchBat command and representatives of the
15    Bosnian Muslim refugees in Potocari.  At this second meeting, Ratko Mladic
16    warned the Bosnian Muslim representatives that their people could either
17    "survive or disappear."  At the third meeting, convened at about 1000
18    hours on 12 July 1995 by Ratko Mladic, Radislav Krstic, and other VRS and
19    Bosnian Serb civilian representatives, and attended by DutchBat officers
20    and representatives of the Bosnian Muslim refugees, Ratko Mladic explained
21    that he would supervise the "evacuation" of refugees from Potocari and
22    that he wanted to see all military-aged Bosnian Muslim men so that they
23    could be screened as possible war criminals.  During the meetings at the
24    Hotel Fontana from the evening of 11 July to the early morning of 12 July
25    1995, the plan to transport the civilian refugee population from Potocari
 1    was developed.  By his presence in these meetings and his subsequent
