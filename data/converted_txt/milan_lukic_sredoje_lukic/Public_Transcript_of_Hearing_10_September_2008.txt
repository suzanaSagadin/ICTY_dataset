 2                           [Open session]
 3                           [The accused entered court]
 4                           --- Upon commencing at 2.43 p.m.
 5             JUDGE ROBINSON:  Just one or two preliminary matters before the
 6     witness is called.  The first has to do with the time for the
 7     Prosecution's case.
 8             Mr. Groome, the Trial Chamber is concerned about the time that
 9     you have requested, an hour and a half, this for a witness whose evidence
10     is to be confined to rebuttal.  I've looked at the history of the times
11     that you have requested, and there has been significant changes.  First
12     two hours in March, then in April three hours, then on the 9th of June
13     the Trial Chamber issued its order concerning alibi evidence of this
14     witness.  A time of three hours was set.  Then on the 17th of July, no
15     doubt in response to the realisation that this witness's evidence would
16     be confined to alibi testimony, the Prosecution indicated a half an hour
17     for this witness.  That was the final witness list.  But a couple days
18     ago on the list for this week, we see an hour and a half.
19             Now, this witness, as I have said, is to give evidence in
20     rebuttal of the alibi defence, and we don't see why an hour and a half
21     should be needed.  We intend to be very strict, because any evidence that
22     you lead the Defence will have a right to respond to it.  The evidence
23     must be confined to -- to alibi, and you will have to persuade us as to
24     why an hour and a half is needed.
25             Mr. Groome.
 1             MR. GROOME:  Yes, Your Honour.  The -- Mr. Vasiljevic testified
 2     for eight days, I believe in excess of 21 hours, 500 pages of transcript.
 3     In my attempt to deal with him most expeditiously, and I do apologise for
 4     the different time estimates, what I've done is the following, Your
 5     Honour:  I have prepared a sheet which has the excerpts that the
 6     Prosecution wishes to rely on.  It is but a small, small portion of the
 7     entire testimony, and, Your Honour, in the Vasiljevic judgement the Trial
 8     Chamber credited some of what Mr. Vasiljevic said and discredited some of
 9     what he said, and it seemed to me that you have the same task before you,
10     and it also seemed to me that one of the tools that Judges use in
11     assessing credibility is their impressions and their view of a witness as
12     that witness gives their evidence.  How long do they take to answer
13     questions, you know, and their general appearance.
14             So I have prepared the video excerpts.  I have put them all
15     together, and that takes precisely one hour and 28 minutes, and that is
16     all of the evidence that the Prosecution wishes to rely on.  It was my
17     belief that by allowing the Chamber to see Mr. Vasiljevic testify and
18     give his evidence related to the alibi in this case, the Chamber would be
19     the optimal position to ask question of Mr. Vasiljevic, as well as the
20     Defence would be in a good position to cross-examine him, and that would
21     be all that -- Your Honour, that I would be seeking to do.  I have but
22     two or three other questions to put to him.  I am relying on what he has
23     given in sworn evidence prior to today.
24             JUDGE ROBINSON:  So you're saying that the evidence consists
25     substantially of video excerpts.
 1             MR. GROOME:  Yes, Your Honour, almost entirely.
 2                           [Trial Chamber confers]
 3             JUDGE ROBINSON:  Mr. Groome, we are not inclined to watch videos
 4     for an hour and a half.  The witness is here.  If you have questions for
 5     him, then put the questions to him.  You should be selective in the
 6     videos that you wish to present to the Trial Chamber, but we don't see
 7     the need to watch an hour and a half or an hour and 20 minutes of videos
 8     with just two or three questions being put to the witness.  We don't need
 9     more than half an hour, 40 minutes, to observe a witness to be able to
10     assess his demeanour.
11             The Chamber will grant you one hour for the examination-in-chief.
12             MR. GROOME:  Yes, Your Honour.
13             THE INTERPRETER:  Microphone, please.
14             MR. GROOME:  Yes, Your Honour.  Before the witness is brought in
15     may I raise two other -- or two preliminary matters?

24     his testimony, about testifying, which could inflict damage on his family

 1     he be asked about them, and he asked that he be heard in private session.
 2             Now, since all the other -- because it's too late for any other
 3     protective measures.  Beforehand he did not discuss this with the
 4     Prosecution, nor was he informed that he would be a witness.  The first
 5     time he heard of that was when the Defence counsel came to visit him and
 6     conveyed that to him.  So he did not have an opportunity to discuss any
 7     possible protective measures or what he is insisting upon now.
 8             JUDGE ROBINSON:  So if I understand you correctly, you're seeking
 9     the protective measure of a private session.
10             Just a second.
11                           [Trial Chamber confers]
12             JUDGE ROBINSON:  Mr. Domazet, the Chamber, of course, would in
13     the circumstances grant the private session, but I listened to you very
14     carefully, and I noted that but for the time factor, Mr. Vasiljevic would
15     have sought greater protective measures, and I'd like to know what those
16     are, because the Chamber is sensitive to the -- the position that he
17     finds himself in, and if it is at all possible to make arrangements for
18     any additional measures we would consider doing so.
19             MR. DOMAZET [Interpretation] Your Honour, I think that this --
20     that a private session would suffice.  It's quite enough.  When I spoke
21     about previous requests, that was, well, with other protective measures,
22     a pseudonym and so on, but I think at this point in time that is no
23     longer necessary.  So if you grant a private session, that will be
24     enough.

 1     the private session, and we note that you have said that that is enough.
