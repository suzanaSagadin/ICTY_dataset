 5            JUDGE SCHOMBURG:  We have got the motion of the Office of the
 6    Prosecutor and already an answer of the Defence, from the side of the
 7    Defence counsel of Mr. Jokic.  First of all, you're the moving party.  Do
 8    you want to make any oral statements?
 9            MR. STOJANOVIC:  [Interpretation] Your Honour, we would only like
10    to repeat in a few sentences what we already mentioned in our motion.
11    After having heard what the Prosecution had to say, we would like to
12    repeat our proposal, namely, the Defence believes that all the
13    requirements from Rule 65 of the Rules of Procedure and Evidence have been
14    met, namely that Dragan Jokic be provisionally released.  We would just
15    like to note that Dragan Jokic responded to both calls by the OTP, as far
16    back as 1999 and also in April 2000.  He received the indictment at his
17    workplace.  He responded to the request made by the OTP.  He surrendered
18    to the representative of the Tribunal.  And he came to the detention unit
19    in August last year.
20            There is not a single reason for us to doubt that if you decide to
21    release him provisionally, Dragan Jokic will be a fugitive and prevent
22    proceedings against him from taking place.  He does not constitute a
23    threat, either to witnesses or to victims, and there is no reason for us
24    to have any doubts regarding his personal safety either.
25            I wish to thank the Office of the Prosecutor on this occasion for
 1    not having rejected our proposal.  We would like to promise that we are
 2    going to meet all the requirements made and everything that the Prosecutor
 3    called for in their response to our motion.  We have also obtained
 4    guarantees from the government of Republika Srpska and also the consent of
 5    the Dutch government, so I believe that we have met the formal
 6    prerequisites for the provisional release of Dragan Jokic.
 7            I would like to ask Ms. Cynthia Sinatra as co-counsel to say a few
 8    words about the legal practice of this Tribunal to date regarding
 9    provisional release, and this will be our joint proposal as it has been so
10    far.  Thank you, Your Honours.
11            MS. SINATRA:  May it please the Court, Your Honours, at this point
12    we would like to re-urge, of course, the original request for provisional
13    release because in there are illuminated a lot of the personal
14    circumstances of Mr. Jokic, but Mr. Jokic will be able to describe those
15    for you in a moment.  And we'd also like to thank the Prosecution.  We
16    appreciate the fact that they have no objections, that they have stated on
17    the record they don't believe that he is a threat of flight, that they
18    don't believe that there is any -- that he poses any danger to any
19    witnesses or victims or any other persons, and as such, under the
20    pre-trial provisional release, those are really the only conditions that
21    need to be met.  Now, I know that sometimes there is a confusion between
22    pre-trial and post-conviction provisional release.  Post-conviction
23    requires an exceptional circumstance or occasion but pre-trial release
24    only requires these conditions under Rule 65.
25            I would also like to say that every request that the Prosecution
 1    made in its motion, every condition that it has placed on there, that we
 2    have no objections to, that Mr. Jokic agrees with, and if it can be
 3    arranged with the government of Bosnia, who was guaranteed everything,
 4    that he will accept every condition and additional conditions that this
 5    Court might set upon him.
 6            I'd like for the -- this Trial Chamber to please refer to Exhibit
 7    A and Exhibit B of the motion that the Defence filed yesterday.  Of
 8    course, Exhibit A is the guarantee from the government of Bosnia and
 9    Herzegovina through the Republic of Srpska.  They do guarantee everything
10    that the Prosecution has asked for, and if the Court adds any additional
11    conditions, then they have been working freely and completely with this
12    Tribunal since the beginning of the Tribunal and they certainly will abide
13    by any conditions that -- I'm not speaking for the government.
14    Unfortunately, they have -- we can't find the representative at the
15    moment.  I believe he received your invitation but we have got our wires
16    mixed up some way.
17            I would like to also refer to -- by the way, we would like to
18    offer the guarantee of the Bosnian government and the letter from the
19    Dutch minister saying that they have no objections to his release into
20    evidence at this time, if they have not already been introduced into
21    evidence.  We are offering them, Your Honour.  Does the Court accept
22    them?
23            JUDGE SCHOMBURG:  I think we should go into details of guarantees
24    only at a later stage when it could become necessary.  I think there are
25    some other questions having priority.  We will come back to this.
 1            MS. SINATRA:  Okay.  And Your Honour also I would like to take
 2    this opportunity to discuss Dragan Jokic's provisional release as compared
 3    to Sefer Halilovic who Judge O-gon Kwon was on the Chamber of that one.
 4    He was released in December and he voluntarily surrendered, just as
 5    Mr. Jokic did.  Of course, the Court found that pre-trial detention is the
 6    most restrictive sanction and I believe the European Commission on Human
 7    Rights says that pre-trial detention is the exception, not the rule.  His
 8    trial will not start immediately, as Halilovic.  He will be able to
 9    support this -- with Halilovic, it was an ailing son, with Jokic he has a
10    16-year-old son with no other parent to take care of him in his home
11    town.
12            The government has guaranteed, the same guarantees that they have
13    with Mr. Halilovic for Mr. Jokic, and he's only charged -- he's charged in
14    the indictment in a joint criminal enterprise, no personal participation
15    in any of the crimes alleged, as far as the supporting material that we
16    have at the moment, and the government of Bosnia and Herzegovina has
17    always cooperated and will continue to cooperate.  At this point, Your
18    Honour, I believe that Mr. Stojanovic will complete this.  And if you have
19    any questions, I'm available to answer any questions that you might pose
20    for us.
21            JUDGE SCHOMBURG:  First of all, one question seems to be evident.
22    You provided us with guarantees from the Republika Srpska.  As you know,
23    the commitments against -- or vis-a-vis the United Nations and vis-a-vis
24    this Tribunal are only those of Bosnia-Herzegovina.  Did you try to get
25    any guarantees from Bosnia-Herzegovina?
 1            MS. SINATRA:  Your Honour, if I'm not mistaken - and I didn't deal
 2    directly with Mr. Ivanic or the representative that Mr. Stojanovic visited
 3    with - but this is a guarantee of the Republika Srpska, but it also is
 4    signed through the Prime Minister of the government of Bosnia and
 5    Herzegovina.  The representative coming is -- that was to appear is from
 6    the government of --
 7            Your Honour, I'm going to defer to Mr. Stojanovic who has a lot
 8    more information about that subject than I.  Thank you.
 9            MR. STOJANOVIC: [Interpretation] Your Honours, you have before you
10    the guarantee of the government of Republika Srpska.  This is the
11    guarantee that we obtained, as was customary practice until now, with
12    regard to motions for provisional release of accused who come from the
13    territory of Republika Srpska in Bosnia-Herzegovina.  I should like to
14    remind you of the following cases:  Simic, Zaric, Tadic, the Samac
15    indictment.
16            The guarantee of the government of Republika Srpska contains
17    everything that the Office of the Prosecutor requested in their motion
18    when they did not express their disapproval of -- for the provisional
19    release of Dragan Jokic.  We tried to compare this, and the answer that we
20    got was that practically everything that was written in the guarantee of
21    the government of Republika Srpska was also taken over by the Office of
22    the Prosecutor as a request, which, in their opinion, constitutes grounds
23    for them not to oppose provisional release.
24            I wish to reiterate that Dragan Jokic himself through his
25    behaviour, through his attitude both towards the Prosecutor and primarily
 1    towards the Tribunal, show that he is cooperative, that there is not a
 2    single reason why he would not come to trials or obstruct in any way the
 3    proceedings involved.  The guarantee confirms that the government of
 4    Republika Srpska will, precisely due to these personal characteristics of
 5    his, also support this, namely that he will carry out all his
 6    obligations.
 7            I am ready to answer all your questions that I am in a position to
 8    answer and that would be supportive of our motion for provisional
 9    release.  Therefore, that is our request to you, because we believe that
10    we do meet the requirements of Rule 65 of the Rules of Procedure and
11    Evidence.
12            JUDGE SCHOMBURG:  Under the Rule 65 you just quoted, it is
13    necessary that the Trial Chamber is satisfied that the accused will appear
14    for trial.  And as it was said in Hadzihasanovic, Alagic, Kubura, Almir,
15    and Jokic, in the former decision in another case, relevant were, first of
16    all, all the relevant circumstances.  And it was said in all the five
17    decisions that, of course, there is no automatic reaction when a person
18    surrenders that then in return, there will be provisional release.
19            Coming to this point, one indicia is of course the question
20    whether or not there are strong links to a family, to house, or whatever,
21    in the country.  And I read in your contribution that there are difficult
22    family solutions and financial situations.  Could you please go a little
23    bit into details of this aspect.
24            MR. STOJANOVIC: [Interpretation] Your Honours, in our submissions,
25    we did not want to sound pathetic at all.  We just presented the relevant

 5    his provisional release.  We do recognise that this is a decision for the
