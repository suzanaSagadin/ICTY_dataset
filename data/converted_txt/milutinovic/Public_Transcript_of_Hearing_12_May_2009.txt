21     evidence.
22             Ms. Hajrizi lived in Kosovska Mitrovica with her family.  She
23     describes the persecution faced by Kosovo Albanians and the threats and
24     harassment experienced by her family in particular prior to the armed
25     conflict in 1999.  Ms. Hajrizi's husband was Agim Hajrizi, a well-known
 1     advocate for the rights of Albanian workers.
 2             In the early morning hours of the 25th of March, 1999, two cars
 3     drove to the house where Ms. Hajrizi's family lived.  Six policemen
 4     wearing blue camouflage uniforms and black berets forced their way into
 5     the house.  They murdered Agim Hajrizi along with Ms. Hajrizi's oldest
 6     son Ilir and Mr. Hajrizi's mother.
 7             Ms. Hajrizi and her surviving children managed to hide and after
 8     the murders they moved to a house in the Tavnik neighbourhood of
 9     Mitrovica.  On the 28th of March, 1999, Serb forces began burning houses
10     and telling people to leave.  Ms. Hajrizi and her children joined a large
11     column of people fleeing to Zabare village.  After three days they were
12     told by the police there to go back to the Mitrovica.  The situation that
13     she describes in Mitrovica was terrible with shooting and burning
14     throughout the town.  They spent several days there before boarding buses
15     that Serb forces had arranged to transport Kosovo Albanians across the
16     border to Montenegro.
17             That is the conclusion of the summary.
