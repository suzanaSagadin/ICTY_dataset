 1            As the Registrar announced, the case on our agenda is prosecutor
 2    versus Miroslav Deronjic.
 3            In accordance with our scheduling order issued on 1 July 2005,
 4    today the Appeal's Chamber will deliver its Judgement on Sentencing Appeal
 5    in this case.
 6            Mr. Deronjic has appealed against the Sentencing Judgement
 7    rendered by Trial Chamber II of this International Tribunal on 30 March
 8    2004.  This case concerns events which took place in the village of
 9    Glogova in the Bratunac municipality in eastern Bosnia and Herzegovina in
10    May 1992.  Miroslav Deronjic was then president of the Bratunac Crisis
11    Staff and a member of the Serbian Democratic Party of Bosnia and
12    Herzegovina.  In the evening of 8 May 1992, he ordered an attack on the
13    village of Glogova as part of his participation in a joint criminal
14    enterprise, the objective of which was the permanent removal by force or
15    other means of Bosnian Muslim inhabitants from the village of Glogova
16    through the commission of the crime of persecutions.  The village of
17    Glogova was burned down in part and the Bosnian Muslim residents were
18    forcibly displaced.  As a result of this attack, 64 Muslim civilians from
19    the village were killed.  Bosnian Muslim homes, private property, and the
20    mosque were destroyed, and a substantial part of Glogova was razed.
21            On 29 September 2003, the parties entered a plea agreement based
22    on the second amended indictment and a separate factual basis.  At the
23    plea hearing held on 30 September 2003, the Appellant pleaded guilty to
24    the single charge of persecutions as set forth in the second amended
25    indictment.  In order to reconcile discrepancies that the Trial Chamber
 1    identified between the second amended indictment and the factual basis
 2    filed by the parties, the Trial Chamber invited the parties to provide
 3    further clarification.  During the sentencing hearing of 27 and 28 January
 4    2004, the Trial Chamber entered a finding of guilt in relation to the
 5    charge of persecutions, a crime against humanity, under Article 5(h) of
 6    the International Tribunal's Statute.  After a further review of the
 7    Appellant's testimony, given on 27 January 2004, the second amended
 8    indictment and the factual basis, the Trial Chamber found further material
 9    discrepancies and ordered a continuation of the sentencing hearing to be
10    held on 5 March 2004 in order to verify that the Appellant's guilty plea
11    could still fulfil the prerequisites of Rule 62 bis of the Rules.
12            The Appellant was held individually criminally responsible
13    pursuant to Rule 7(1) of the Statute -- to Article 7(1) of the Statute of
14    the International Tribunal for his substantial participation as a
15    co-perpetrator in a joint criminal enterprise.  The Trial Chamber
16    sentenced the Appellant to ten years of imprisonment, Judge Schomburg
17    dissenting with credit for time already served in detention.
18            Following the practice of the International Tribunal, I will not
19    read out the text of the Judgement except for the disposition.  Instead, I
20    will summarise the issues on this appeal and the findings of the Appeals
21    Chamber.  I emphasise that this summary is not part of the written
22    Judgement which is the only authoritative account of the Appeals Chamber's
23    rulings and reasons.  Copies of the written Judgement will be made
24    available to the parties and to the public at the conclusion of this
25    hearing.
 1            I will not elaborate on the standard of review on appeal and the
 2    relevant provisions on sentencing since I have already addressed that
 3    during my opening statement at the appeal hearing.
 4            The Appellant raises four grounds of appeal which I will address
 5    in turn.
 6            In his first ground of appeal, the Appellant contends that the
 7    Trial Chamber erred in law and in fact and abused its discretion in
 8    reaching conclusions and findings which are based upon evidence not
 9    specifically contained in the second amended indictment, the plea
10    agreement or the factual basis.  He refers to all these documents as the
11    "plea agreement package."  In support of this contention, the Appellant
12    draws the attention of the Appeals Chamber to various paragraphs of the
13    Sentencing Judgement which he claims are contradictory or contain errors
14    of law or fact.
15            The Appeals Chamber finds that the Trial Chamber appropriately and
16    necessarily looked beyond the plea agreement package to other evidence as
17    independent indicia in order to satisfy itself that there was a sufficient
18    factual basis for the guilty plea.  The Trial Chamber's approach was
19    consistent with Rule 62 bis of the Rules of Procedure and Evidence.  In
20    addition, the Appeals Chamber finds that the Trial Chamber did not err in
21    determining the Appellant's sentence by considering all relevant
22    information it had before it.
23            With respect to the specific errors of law and fact alleged by the
24    Appellant under his first ground of appeal, the Appeals Chamber recalls
25    that in general a Trial Chamber is not obliged to refer to every piece of
 1    evidence on the trial record in its judgement or to every submission made
 2    during the trial, if the evidence cited does not directly support the
 3    facts on which the Trial Chamber's challenged finding is based.  The
 4    determination as to whether the Trial Chamber made an error must be
 5    considered on a case-by-case basis and in light of all the evidence before
 6    it.  Moreover, the Appeals Chamber emphasis that it is for the Appellant
 7    to demonstrate that he was prejudiced by the error.
 8            The Appeals Chamber has carefully examined each allegation
 9    concerning the legal and factual errors alleged by the Appellant.  It
10    concludes that none of the Appellant's arguments under this ground of
11    appeal has any merit.  The Appellant has failed to show that the Trial
12    Chamber erred to the prejudice of the Appellant:  (1) in finding that he
13    accepted the arrival of volunteers and their involvement in the use of
14    force; (2) in concluding that the Appellant joined the mission to disarm
15    the population of Glogova by not only accepting it but also by
16    participating in it; (3) in making a remark about the temporary length of
17    the joint criminal enterprise; (4) in stating that the crime was
18    long-planned; (5) in its statements concerning the Appellant's mens rea;
19    (6) in various paragraphs that the Appellant claims insinuate his
20    responsibility for additional crimes not contained in the second amended
21    indictment; (7) in concluding that the Appellant abused his authority and
22    political power as president of the Crisis Staff in the Municipal Board to
23    commit the crimes he was charged with; (8) in making reference to the fact
24    that the Appellant's involvement during the attack on Glogova included
25    "planning"; (9) in concluding that the Appellant had a leading position
 1    in this operation; and, finally, (10) in reaching factual conclusions
 2    regarding the vulnerability of the inhabitants of Glogova.  The
 3    Appellant's first grounds of appeal is accordingly dismissed.
 4            In his second ground of appeal, the Appellant contends that the
 5    principle of lex mitior is applicable in his case and submits that the
 6    Trial Chamber erred in law and in fact in concluding that "the Tribunal,
 7    having primacy vis-a-vis national jurisdictions in the former Yugoslavia
 8    is not bound to apply a more lenient penalty, if any, under these
 9    jurisdictions."
10            The principle of lex mitior is understood to me that the more
11    lenient law has to be applied if the laws relevant to the offence have
12    been amended.  The Appeals Chamber reiterates its finding in the Dragan
13    Nikolic sentencing Appeal Judgement that the principle of lex mitior
14    applies to the Statute of the International Tribunal and therefore if ever
15    there is a change in the Statute regarding sentencing powers, the
16    International Tribunal would have to apply the version of the Statute
17    resulting in the less serious penalty.
18            With respect to the applicability of the principle of lex mitior
19    to the relationship between the law of the International Tribunal and the
20    law relevant for the national courts of the former Yugoslavia, the issue
21    is whether differing national criminal laws are relevant and applicable to
22    the law governing the sentencing consideration of the International
23    Tribunal.  The Appeals Chamber notes that the answer is to be found in the
24    principle of lex mitior itself and to this end, reiterates its finding in
25    the Dragan Nikolic's Sentencing Appeal Judgement where it found that "the

 1    principle of lex mitior is thus only applicable if a law that binds the
 2    International Tribunal is subsequently changed to a more favourable law by
 3    which the International Tribunal is also obliged to abide."
 4            Consequently, the International Tribunal is not bound by the law
 5    on sentencing practice in the former Yugoslavia.  The principle of lex
 6    mitior is not applicable in relation to those laws.  The second ground of
 7    appeal is therefore dismissed.
 8            The Appellant contends under his third ground of appeal that the
 9    Trial Chamber erred in law and in fact in its assessment of the
10    aggravating circumstances because they are either subsumed in the overall
11    gravity of the offence for which he was convicted or are incorporated as
12    constituent elements of the crime or are illustrative of the context of
13    the crime.  The Appeals Chamber notes that the Appellant's arguments in
14    this respect go beyond his notice of appeal and that he never sought
15    "variation of the grounds of appeal" pursuant to Rule 108 of the Rules, a
16    procedure he was required to follow if, upon further review, the initial
17    notice of appeal proved inadequate.  This procedural requirement was net
18    neither by the Appellant's proviso in his notice of appeal, in which he
19    reserved the right to raise any errors that might become apparent
20    subsequent to the full review and analysis of the entire record of the
21    proceedings and him being given a copy of the Sentencing Judgement in his
22    own language, nor by the inclusion of the relevant arguments in his appeal
23    brief.  Nonetheless the Appeals Chamber finds that under the circumstances
24    of the case, the Prosecution was not materially prejudiced by the
25    Appellant's failure to seek a variation of the notice of appeal pursuant
 1    to Rule 108 of the Rules, and that, therefore, pursuant to Rule 5 of the
 2    Rules, relief in the form of a refusal to hear the Appellant's arguments
 3    is not required.  In light of this lack of material prejudice and the
 4    potential importance of the arguments in question for the sentence of the
 5    Appellant, the Appeals Chamber decided to consider the Appellant's
 6    arguments concerning the Trial Chamber's treatment of aggravating factors
 7    notwithstanding the Appellant's failure to comply with the Rules.
 8            On the merits, the Appellant essentially alleges that the Trial
 9    Chamber impermissibly considered the factors in aggravation of his
10    sentence twice.  The Appeals Chamber considers that factors which a Trial
11    Chamber takes into account as aspect of the gravity of the crime cannot
12    additionally be taken into account as separate aggravating circumstances
13    and vice versa.  The Appeals Chamber acknowledges that, indeed, the Trial
14    Chamber did not expressly address the distinction between the gravity of
15    the offence and the aggravating circumstances in its Sentencing Judgement.
16    This is unfortunate, but it does not necessarily follow that the Trial
17    Chamber undertook impermissible double counting by treating matters
18    relevant to the gravity of the offence also as additional aggravating
19    circumstances.  The Appeals Chamber finds that the Sentencing Judgement
20    clearly shows that the Trial Chamber distinguished between aggravating
21    circumstances on the one hand and the gravity of the offence on the other,
22    albeit considering them under the same heading.  The Trial Chamber was
23    therefore cognizant of the fact that taking into account matters relevant
24    to the gravity of the offence also is aggravating circumstances for the
25    purposes of sentencing is not permissible.
 1            With respect to the Appellant's remaining arguments under his
 2    third ground of appeal concerning the aggravating circumstances considered
 3    by the Trial Chamber, namely:  (1), the large number of victims; (2), the
 4    fact that the attack on Glogova was meticulously planned; (3), the
 5    Appellant's abuse of authority; (4), the fact that the Appellant ordered
 6    the additional torching of houses; and (5), the exacerbated vulnerability
 7    and defencelessness of the victims; the Appeals Chamber concludes that the
 8    Trial Chamber was entitled to rely upon facts related to the context of
 9    the crime in imposing its sentence, and finds that the Appellant has
10    failed to demonstrate that the Trial Chamber engaged in the impermissible
11    double counting of aggravating factors.  The third ground of appeal is
12    accordingly dismissed.
13            Finally, the Appellant contends under his fourth ground of appeal
14    that the Trial Chamber erred in its assessment of the mitigating
15    circumstances.  The Appeals Chamber notes that it is again exercised its
16    discretion to address the merits of the Appellant's argument, even though
17    some arguments put forward in this ground of appeal impermissibly exceed
18    the scope of his notice of appeal.
19            On the merits, the Appellant submits that the reference by the
20    Trial Chamber to the "negative side effects of the guilty plea" shows that
21    it expressed concerns as to the truthfulness of his admissions.  The
22    Appeals Chamber notes that it cannot be inferred from the part of the
23    Sentencing Judgement referred to by the Appellant in support of his
24    argument that the Trial Chamber challenged the truthfulness of the
25    Appellant's statements.  The paragraph referred to by the Appellant is
 1    included in the chapeau of the section of the Sentencing Judgement
 2    addressing the law applicable to sentencing and contains no reference to
 3    the Appellant's case.  Furthermore, the Appeals Chamber notes that the
 4    Appellant does not make any reference to the Sentencing Judgement in
 5    support of his argument that the Trial Chamber challenged his statements
 6    as untrue.  As a result, the Appeals Chamber finds that the Appellant's
 7    argument is without merit.
 8            The Appellant also argues that in determining his sentence, the
 9    Trial Chamber only focused on deterrence and retribution and neglected to
10    afford sufficient prominence to the process of rehabilitation.  The
11    Appeals Chamber notes that in citing the Celebici Appeal Judgement, the
12    Trial Chamber correctly referred to deterrence and retribution as the main
13    purposes of sentencing and correctly considered rehabilitation as a
14    relevant factor that should not be given undue weight.  Thus the Trial
15    Chamber did consider rehabilitation as a sentencing principle and the fact
16    that they decided not to give it undue weight to such a factor was within
17    its discretion.
18            The Appellant further submits that the Trial Chamber erred by not
19    considering as mitigating circumstances some facts which, in his view,
20    showed that his character and behaviour amounted to exceptional
21    circumstances.  Having carefully examined each of these facts, the Appeals
22    Chamber concludes that the Trial Chamber committed no error.
23            The Appellant finally asserts that the Trial Chamber did not
24    address at all the personal and family circumstances of the Appellant as a
25    mitigating circumstance despite the fact that he offered substantial
 1    reasoning and presented material evidence to that effect.  The Appeals
 2    Chamber finds that the Trial Chamber in fact referred expressly to the
 3    Appellant's arguments regarding his family circumstances and this
 4    reference constitutes prima facie evidence that these circumstances were
 5    taken into account.
 6            The Appeals Chamber therefore concludes that the Appellant has
 7    failed to show that the Trial Chamber committed an error that occasioned a
 8    miscarriage of justice on this point or otherwise erred in its treatment
 9    of mitigating factors.  The fourth ground of appeal is accordingly
10    dismissed.

18    in open session;
19            Dismisses unanimously all the grounds of appeal filed by the
20    Appellant;
