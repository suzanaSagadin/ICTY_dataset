 8             This sitting of the Referral Bench of the International Tribunal
 9    has been convened for the delivery of its decision on a motion of the
10    Prosecutor for this case to be referred for trial to the authorities of
11    Bosnia and Herzegovina.
12             The Motion for referral was spotted by the Government of Bosnia
13    and Herzegovina.  The Government of Serbia and Montenegro, however,
14    requested this Bench to act on its own volition to refer this case instead
15    to the authorities of Serbia and Montenegro for trial.  The accused were
16    opposed to referral, preferring trial before this Tribunal, but submitted
17    that if referral was appropriate, the case should be referred to Serbia
18    and Montenegro.
19             We would note that the third member of the Bench which has heard
20    and decided this case, Judge O-Gon Kwon, is unable to be present this
21    afternoon.  Judge O-Gon Kwon is in full agreement with the reasons set out
22    in the written decision and with the orders which will be made.
23             It is not proposed to read out the full written decision of the
24    Bench at this hearing.  Instead, a summary of key points will be given.
25    It is emphasised that the summary is not part of the written decision,
 1    which is the only authoritative account of the Bench's reasons.  Copies of
 2    the written decision will be made available at the conclusion of this
 3    hearing.
 4             While this Tribunal is established by the United Nations with
 5    powers to try cases against persons responsible for serious violations of
 6    International Humanitarian Law committed in the territory of the former
 7    Yugoslavia since 1991, it has never had the exclusive power to do so.  The
 8    courts of the present nations in the territory of the former Yugoslavia
 9    have not been denied jurisdiction to try such cases.
10             By resolutions of the Security Council of the United Nations,
11    which are referred to in the written decision, this Tribunal has been
12    requested by the Security Council to concentrate on cases involving the
13    most senior leaders suspected of being most responsible for crimes with
14    the -- within the Tribunal's jurisdiction and to transfer cases involving
15    those who may not bear this level of responsibility to competent national
16    jurisdictions for trial.
17             Hence, the Motion of the Prosecutor to refer this case to Bosnia
18    and Herzegovina.
19             Rule 11 bis of this Tribunal's Rules allow referral for trial to
20    be ordered to the authorities of:
21             (1)  a state where the crime was committed; or
22             (2)  a state where the accused was arrested; or
23             (3)  a state which has jurisdiction and which is willing and
24    adequately prepared to accept the case.
25             The gravity of the alleged crimes and the level of responsibility
 1    of the accused must be considered, and, before ordering referral, this
 2    Bench must be satisfied that the accused will receive a fair trial and
 3    that the death penalty will not be imposed or carried out if the trial
 4    should result in a conviction.
 5             The charges.
 6             The four accused are charged with having committed crimes which
 7    arise in the context of the detainment of non-Serbs, principally Bosnian
 8    Muslims and Bosnian Croats, in camps at Omarska and Keraterm in Bosnia and
 9    Herzegovina, between May and August 1992.  It is alleged that the accused
10    Zeljko Mejakic was the Commander of the Omarska Camp at that time, Momcilo
11    Gruban was a guard shift commander at the Omarska Camp, and Dusan Fustar
12    at the Keraterm Camp, and that Dusko Knezevic held no official position
13    but was able to enter both of those camps when he wished.
14             Each of the accused is charged with five offences:  Persecution
15    as a crime against humanity; murder, as a crime against humanity; murder,
16    as a violation of the laws and customs of war; inhumane acts as a crime
17    against humanity and customs of war; cruel treatment, as a violation of
18    the laws and customs of war.
19             Each of the accused, except Dusko Knezevic, is alleged to bear
20    criminal responsibility pursuant to both Articles 7(1) and 7(3) of the
21    Statute of the International Tribunal, whereas only Article 7(1) is relied
22    on against Dusko Knezevic.
23             The gravity of charges and level of responsibility of the
24    accused.
25             For reasons set out in the written decision, the Referral Bench
 1    is satisfied that the crimes alleged against the accused are indeed grave.
 2    Nonetheless, when they are considered in the context of other cases which
 3    this Tribunal deals with, they are not among the "most serious."
 4             Even though Zeljko Mejakic is alleged to have been the Commander
 5    of the camp at Omarska, he cannot be considered to be among the "most
 6    senior leaders."  The other three are not "leaders" in any relevant sense.
 7             Accordingly, the Referral Bench is satisfied that neither the
 8    gravity of the alleged offences, nor the level of responsibility of the
 9    accused at the time precludes the referral of this case to the authorities
10    of a nation which meets the requirements of Rule 11 bis.
11             Referral to Bosnia and Herzegovina or Serbia and Montenegro.
12             The offences charged are alleged to have been committed in Bosnia
13    and Herzegovina against persons living in Bosnia and Herzegovina and by
14    the accused who were then living in Bosnia and Herzegovina.  At least
15    three of the accused were citizens of Bosnia and Herzegovina at the time
16    of the alleged offences, and two of them were transferred to this Tribunal
17    from Bosnia and Herzegovina.
18             By contrast, the nexus with Serbia and Montenegro is limited.
19    Nationality was the primary justification advanced by the Defence and
20    Serbia and Montenegro.  It does appear that while two of the accused are
21    citizens of Bosnia and Herzegovina by birth, they were also granted
22    citizenship of Serbia and Montenegro in July 2004 and January 2005
23    respectively, that is, after these accused were placed in custody by this
24    Tribunal.  A third was born in Bosnia and Herzegovina but it has not been
25    established that he has also been granted citizenship of Serbia and

 1    Montenegro.  Only the fourth appears to be a citizen of Serbia and
 2    Montenegro by birth.
 3             Both Bosnia and Herzegovina and Serbia and Montenegro affirm that
 4    they are willing and adequately prepared to accept the case for trial.
 5    However, given the strength of the connections of the case with Bosnia and
 6    Herzegovina, the Referral Bench has concentrated its attention on referral
 7    to that state on the basis that only if it was found that other
 8    considerations weighed significantly against such referral would the
 9    Referral Bench return to a detailed consideration of ordering referral
10    proprio motu to Serbia and Montenegro.  It has not proved necessary to do
11    so.
12             The law of Bosnia and Herzegovina.
13             If the case were referred to Bosnia and Herzegovina, the law in
14    force there provides that the trial will be conducted by the State Court.
15    It would be necessary for the State Court to determine the law applicable
16    to each of the alleged criminal acts of the accused.  This Referral Bench
17    has no authority to purport to determine those issues in any binding way.
18    Nevertheless, the Bench has considered the possible alternatives and has
19    been satisfied, for reasons set out in the written decision, that whatever
20    alternative is applied, there are appropriate provisions which permit the
21    prosecution of the accused for most, if not all, of the criminal acts
22    alleged in the present Indictment, and which provide for adequate
23    penalties in the event of convictions.
24             Fair trial.
25             The Referral Bench has given detailed consideration to the
 1    question whether there would be a fair trial if the case were referred to
 2    Bosnia and Herzegovina.  In addition to general fair trial considerations,
 3    the Referral Bench has dealt specifically in its written decision with
 4    each of the following concerns raised by the Defence:
 5             1.  The composition of the State Court;
 6             2.  The capacity to amend the present Indictment if referral is
 7    ordered;
 8             3.  The use of materials and judgement of this Tribunal in the
 9    State Court;
10             4.  Whether the accused could examine the witnesses against them,
11    whether they could obtain the attendance of Defence witnesses, and whether
12    there are adequate safe conduct and witness protection measures;
13             5.  The detention of the accused during trial;
14             6.  The availability to the accused of counsel of choice;
15             7.  Delay caused by a referral.
16             The Referral Bench notes, in particular, the changes that have
17    been made to the law of Bosnia and Herzegovina to ensure fair trial
18    guarantees generally comparable to those which apply in this Tribunal.
19    The War Crimes Chamber of the State Court has also been established to try
20    referred cases.  This is comprised of both international and national
21    judges.  The procedure for appointing the national judges directly
22    involves judicial and parliamentary representatives of Republika Srpska.
23             The Referral Bench is satisfied that the concerns raised by the
24    Defence are not well founded.  Our reasons are set out fully in the
25    Written decision.  In addition, the Rules of this Tribunal provide for the
 1    monitoring of a referred case, which provides a further assurance of a
 2    fair trial.
 3             Death penalty.
 4             The Referral Bench is satisfied that the death penalty has been
 5    abolished in Bosnia and Herzegovina.
 6             Conclusion.
 7             Having considered the matters raised by the submissions and in
 8    particular the gravity of the alleged criminal conduct of the accused and
 9    their level of responsibility, and also being satisfied on the information
10    presently available that the accused will receive a fair trial and that
11    the death penalty will not be imposed or carried out, the Referral Bench
12    concludes that referral of this case to the Authorities of Bosnia and
13    Herzegovina for trial should be ordered.
14             I therefore now read the disposition of the decision of the
15    Referral Bench.
16             For the foregoing reasons, the Referral Bench, pursuant to Rules
17    11 bis of the Rules, hereby grants the motion and orders that the case of
18    Prosecutor versus Zeljko Mejakic, Momcilo Gruban, Dusan Fustar, and Dusko
19    Knezevic be referred to the authorities of the State of Bosnia and
20    Herzegovina so that those authorities should forthwith refer the case to
21    the appropriate court, that is, the State Court, for trial within Bosnia
22    and Herzegovina.
23             Declares that the referral of this case shall not have the effect
24    of revoking the previous orders and decisions of the Tribunal in this
25    case.  It will be for the State Court or the competent national
 1    authorities of Bosnia and Herzegovina to determine whether different
 2    provisions should be made for the purposes of the trial of this case in
 3    Bosnia and Herzegovina.
 4             Orders the Registrar of the Tribunal to arrange for transport of
 5    each of the accused and their personal belongings within 30 days of this
 6    decision becoming final to Bosnia and Herzegovina in accordance with the
 7    procedures applicable to transfer of convicted persons to states for
 8    service of sentence.
 9             Orders the Prosecutor to hand over to the Prosecutor of Bosnia
10    and Herzegovina as soon as possible and no later than 30 days after this
11    decision in this case has become final the material supporting the
12    indictment against the accused and all other appropriate evidentiary
13    material.
14             Orders the Prosecutor to continue its efforts in cooperation with
15    the Organisation for Security and Cooperation in Europe or another
16    international organisation of notable standing to ensure the monitoring
17    and reporting on the proceedings of this case before the State Court of
18    Bosnia and Herzegovina.  If arrangements for monitoring and reporting
19    should prove ineffective, the Prosecutor should seek further direction
20    from the Referral Bench.
21             Further orders the Prosecutor to file an initial report to the
22    Referral Bench on the progress made by the Prosecutor of Bosnia and
23    Herzegovina in the prosecution of the accused six weeks after transfer of
24    the evidentiary material and thereafter every three months, including
25    information on the course of the proceedings of the State Court of Bosnia
 1    and Herzegovina after commencement of trial, such reports to comprise or
 2    to include any reports received by the Prosecutor from the international
 3    organisation monitoring or reporting on the proceedings.
 4             And finally, orders that the protective measures granted to
 5    victims and witnesses as set forth in the confidential annex are to remain
 6    in force and that requests for protective measures pending before this
 7    Tribunal should be resubmitted to the State Court of Bosnia and
 8    Herzegovina for determination.
 9             We would add, to avoid any uncertainty, that the accused Momcilo
10    Gruban should, of course, remain in custody until the Pre-Trial Chamber
11    has considered the effect of this decision on the issue of the
12    continuation of his pre-trial release.
13             This concludes this session of the Referral Bench.
