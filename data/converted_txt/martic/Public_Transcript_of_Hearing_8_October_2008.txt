 3             JUDGE POCAR:  Good.  Thank you.
 4             Now, as the Registrar announced, the case on our agenda today is
 5     the Prosecutor versus Milan Martic.  In accordance with the order
 6     scheduling the hearing issued on 29 September 2008, the Appeals Chamber
 7     will deliver its judgement today.
 8             Following the practice of the International Tribunal, I will not
 9     read out the text of the judgement, except for the disposition.  Instead,
10     I will summarise the issues on appeal and the findings of the
11     Appeals Chamber.  This summary is not part of the written judgement,
12     which is the only authoritative account of the Appeals Chamber's rulings
13     and reasons.  Of course, copies of the written judgement will be made
14     available to the parties at the conclusion of this hearing.
15             The events giving rise to this appeal took place between August
16     1991 and December 1995 in the Serbian Autonomous Region for Krajina, also
17     known as SAO Krajina, and the Republic of Serbian Krajina, also known as
18     RSK.  During the relevant period, Milan Martic held various positions
19     within the government of the SAO Krajina, which later evolved into the
20     RSK.  These positions included chief of police in Knin; secretary for
21     Internal Affairs of the SAO Krajina; deputy commander of the Territorial
22     Defence of the SAO Krajina; Minister of Defence of the SAO Krajina;
23     Minister of the Interior of the SAO Krajina and of the RSK; and from
24     January 25, 1994, onwards, president of the RSK.
25             The Trial Chamber found that Martic participated in a joint
 1     criminal enterprise, the common purpose of which was the establishment of
 2     an ethnically Serb territory through the displacement of the non-Serb
 3     population.  Martic was convicted of the following crimes:  persecution,
 4     as a crime against humanity; murder, as a crime against humanity; murder,
 5     as a violation of the laws or customs of war; imprisonment, as a crime
 6     against humanity; torture, as a crime against humanity; inhuman acts, as
 7     a crime against humanity; torture, as a violation of the laws or customs
 8     of war; cruel treatment, as a violation of the laws or customs of war;
 9     deportation, as a crime against humanity; forcible transfer, as a crime
10     against humanity; wanton destruction of villages or devastation not
11     justified when military necessity as a violation of the laws or customs
12     of war; destruction or willful damage done to institutions dedicated to
13     education or religion, as a violation of the laws or customs of war; and
14     plunder of public or private property, as a violation of the laws or
15     customs of war.
16             The Trial Chamber specifically convicted Martic for deportation,
17     forcible transfer and persecution, in part, as crimes falling within the
18     joint criminal enterprise.  For the other counts, its natural and
19     foreseeable consequences of the common purpose to create the
20     Serb-dominated territory.
21             The Trial Chamber further found that Martic ordered the shelling
22     of Zagreb on 2 and 3 May 1995.  It therefore held that he was
23     individually criminally responsible pursuant to Article 7(1) of the
24     Statute for ordering murder, as a crime against humanity; murder, as a
25     violation of the laws or customs of war; inhuman acts, as a crime against
 1     humanity; cruel treatment, as a violation of the laws or customs of war;
 2     and attacks on civilians, as a violation of the laws or customs of war.
 3             The Trial Chamber sentenced Martic to a single sentence of 35
 4     years of imprisonment.
 5             Milan Martic brought ten grounds of appeal against the trial
 6     judgement.  First, an alleged error of law, by not providing a reasoned
 7     judgement; second, alleged violations of his right to be tried by an
 8     impartial tribunal and to be presumed innocent; third, an alleged error
 9     of law regarding the evaluation of evidence; fourth, alleged errors of
10     law regarding the joint criminal enterprise; fifth, alleged errors of
11     fact in findings concerning the joint criminal enterprise; sixth, an
12     alleged error of law regarding the mode of commission of ordering;
13     seventh, alleged errors of fact concerning the shelling of Zagreb;
14     eighth, alleged errors of fact in making erroneous and insufficient
15     findings; ninth, alleged errors of law regarding sentencing; and,
16     finally, alleged errors of fact concerning sentencing.
17             Milan Martic sought an acquittal on all charges.  Alternatively,
18     he requested that he be given a new trial or that his sentence be
19     significantly reduced.
20             The Office of the Prosecutor identified one ground of appeal
21     against the trial judgement.  It argued that the Trial Chamber erred in
22     law in finding that Article 5 of the Statute does not encompass crimes
23     committed against persons hors de combat.  It requested that the
24     Appeal Chamber correct the legal error, revise the Trial Chamber's
25     factual findings relating to counts charges under article 5 of the
 1     statute, and adjust Martic's sentence accordingly.
 2             I will now briefly summarise the conclusions of the
 3     Appeals Chamber.
 4             In his first ground of appeal, Martic alleges that the Trial
 5     Chamber violated his right under Article 23(2) of the Statute to be
 6     provided with a reasoned opinion with regard to all the Trial Chamber's
 7     findings of guilt.  The Appeal Chamber dismisses this ground of appeal
 8     due to its vagueness.
 9             Under his second ground of appeal, Martic claims that the Trial
10     Chamber violated his right to be tried by an impartial tribunal and to be
11     presumed innocent.  Concerning Martic's allegation that the Trial Chamber
12     violated his rights to be presumed innocent and to remain silent in its
13     consideration of the evidence regarding his personality and voluntary
14     surrender, the Appeal Chamber notes that challenged sections of the Trial
15     Chamber judgement only deal with his sentence and fall within the
16     discretion of the Trial Chamber.  Other claims of Martic go to the
17     substance of the Trial Chamber's findings rather than to the presumption
18     of innocence.  The Appeal Chamber has, therefore, dealt with them in
19     other portions of the judgement.
20             Concerning his allegedly violated right to be tried before an
21     impartial tribunal, the Appeals Chamber finds that the conduct by Judges
22     at trial did not give rise to an appearance of bias.  The Appeal Chamber,
23     therefore, dismisses Martic's second ground of appeal in its entirety.
24             Under his third ground of appeal, Martic claims that the Trial
25     Chamber erred in law by inter alia applying to the Prosecution's evidence
 1     a standard of proof lower than the one required in international criminal
 2     law.  The Appeals Chamber finds that the Trial Chamber's reference to a
 3     high degree of probability in one of the footnotes to the section on
 4     standard of proof is confusing and not in accordance with the standard of
 5     proof of a criminal trial.  However, the Appeal Chamber is satisfied that
 6     with respect to the application of the standard, the Trial Chamber
 7     adopted the appropriate standard of proof beyond a reasonable doubt in
 8     its consideration of the evidence.  The Appeal Chamber therefore
 9     dismisses Martic's third ground of appeal in its entirety.
10             In his fourth ground of appeal, Martic alleges that the Trial
11     Chamber erred in law by failing to properly elaborate on the elements of
12     the law of joint criminal enterprise.  The Appeal Chamber finds that the
13     Trial Chamber correctly stated the law applicable to joint criminal
14     enterprise and duly described the requirements for a conviction pursuant
15     to that mode of liability.  The Appeal Chamber therefore dismisses
16     Martic's fourth ground of appeal in its entirety.
17             In his fifth ground of appeal, Martic alleges error of fact in
18     the Trial Chamber's findings relating to the general requirements of
19     Article 3 and 5 of the Statute, the existence of a joint criminal
20     enterprise, his participation in the joint criminal enterprise, and the
21     crimes committed in furtherance of the common criminal purpose of the
22     joint criminal enterprise.  In addition, Martic also argues under various
23     grounds of appeal that the Trial Chamber erred in failing to properly
24     establish a link between himself and the principal perpetrators of the
25     crimes.  In challenging the Trial Chamber's findings that the general
 1     requirements of Article 3 and 5 of the Statute have been established,
 2     Martic argues that the Trial Chamber erred in finding that a state of
 3     armed conflict existed in the relevant territories of Croatia and the
 4     Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina during the indictment period and that
 5     the crimes charged were committed in the context of that armed conflict.
 6     Further, Martic challenges the finding that there was a widespread and
 7     systematic attack against the Croat and non-Serb civilian population in
 8     those territories during the same period.  The Appeal Chamber finds that
 9     Martic merely suggests a different interpretation of the facts to that of
10     the Trial Chamber and failed to show that the Trial Chamber erred in its
11     findings.  Accordingly, the Appeal Chamber dismisses this sub-ground of
12     appeals.
13             With regard to Martic's challenges to the Trial Chamber's finding
14     on the clashes in and around the village of Kijevo on 26 August 1991 and
15     the Kijevo ultimatum, the Appeal Chamber finds that Martic has not shown
16     that no reasonable trier of fact could have reached that conclusion
17     beyond a reasonable doubt.  It therefore dismisses this sub-ground.
18             Martic argues that the Trial Chamber erred in not making findings
19     on the background and political objectives of the Serb leadership.  He
20     argues that had the Trial Chamber taken into account the preceding
21     historical context, it would have understood that Martic advocated for an
22     independent Serb state or at least a substantial degree of autonomy
23     within Croatia as a response to the aims of the Croatian authorities.  To
24     the extent the Martic argument is an attempt to plead that the acts for
25     which he was found responsible should not be considered criminal because
 1     they were a response to crimes committed against him and his people, this
 2     must be rejected.  The Appeal Chamber reiterates that this is not an
 3     available defence to serious violations of international humanitarian
 4     law.
 5             To the extent that Martic argues that the Trial Chamber erred in
 6     failing to take into account the relevant contextual factors, in
 7     particular the political objectives of the Serb leadership, the
 8     Appeal Chamber is not persuaded that the Trial Chamber erred in either
 9     respect.  The Trial Chamber considered that the political aims of the
10     Serb leadership "to unite Serb areas in Croatia and in Bosnia-Herzegovina
11     with Serbia in order to establish a unified territory," did not "amount
12     to a common purpose within the meaning of the law on joint criminal
13     enterprise, pursuant to Article 7(1) of the Statute."  The Trial Chamber,
14     rather, held that:
15             "Where the creation of such territories is intended to be
16     implemented through the commission of crimes within the Statute, this may
17     be sufficient to amount to a common joint criminal purpose."
18             The Trial Chamber, in other words, found that the objectives of
19     the Serb leadership were "implemented through widespread and systematic
20     armoured attacks on predominantly Croat and other non-Serb areas and
21     through the commission of acts of violence and intimidation," which
22     "necessitated the forcible removal of the non-Serb population from the
23     SAO Krajina and RSK territory."
24             Martic was not convicted for his political views or for the
25     political aims of the Serb leadership.  Rather, the Trial Chamber found
 1     that in pursuing political aims, Martic and the other political and
 2     military leaders committed serious crimes.
 3             With regard to Martic's submissions that no reasonable trier of
 4     fact could have concluded that he had deliberately refrained from
 5     intervening against perpetrators who committed crimes against the
 6     non-Serb population, the Appeal Chamber notes that the Trial Chamber did
 7     not convict Martic for his failure to intervene against the perpetrators
 8     of crimes against non-Serbs.  The Trial Chamber observed that:
 9             "The evidence includes only scarce reference to Milan Martic
10     acting to take measures to prevent or punish such crimes."  Language very
11     close to that of Article 7(3) of the Statute.
12             However, the Appeal Chamber takes into consideration that these
13     statements were made in order to establish that Martic had the requisite
14     mens rea to be held criminally responsible as a participant in the joint
15     criminal enterprise.
16             The Appeal Chamber is of the view that it was not for Martic to
17     bring evidence that he took action to punish the crimes committed against
18     non-Serbs.  Rather, it was for the Prosecution to prove that he failed to
19     do so.  In this respect, the Trial Chamber erred.  However, the
20     Appeal Chamber does not find that this error affected the reasonableness
21     of the Trial Chamber's overall conclusion regarding Martic's mens rea.
22     Accordingly, this sub-ground of appeal is dismissed.
23             The Appeal Chamber farther summarily dismisses Martic's
24     challenges to the Trial Chamber's findings regarding crimes committed
25     during several armoured clashes between Serb and Croatian forces in the
 1     SAO Krajina on the basis that they challenge factual findings on which a
 2     conviction does not rely; they misrepresent or ignore the Trial Chamber's
 3     finding; they include assertions that the Trial Chamber should have
 4     interpreted evidence in a particular manner, include assertions
 5     unsupported by any evidence, and include assertions that the Trial
 6     Chamber should or should not have relied on certain pieces of evidence.
 7             In relation to his conviction for crimes convicted in Benkovac,
 8     Martic submits that the Trial Chamber erred in making these findings
 9     because the evidence upon which the Trial Chamber relied was unsupported
10     and uncorroborated.  The Appeal Chamber notes that the Prosecution
11     acknowledged that these convictions should be reversed for reasons of
12     fairness, as they did not form part of the case against Martic.  The
13     Appeal Chamber therefore finds that the Trial Chamber erred in convicting
14     Martic for crimes committed in Benkovac and reverses Martic's conviction
15     for counts 8 and 9 in respect of the crimes perpetrated against Ivan
16     Atelj and Sime Cacic in Benkovac, and counts 7 and 9 in respect of the
17     crimes perpetrated against the three children detained in a kindergarten
18     in Benkovac.
19             In various sections of his appeal brief, Martic challenges the
20     findings of the Trial Chamber establishing a link between the principle
21     perpetrators of the criminal acts charged in the indictment and himself.
22     More specifically, Martic alleges that the Trial Chamber erred on various
23     occasions in reaching the conclusion that crimes were committed by forces
24     under his control or the control of another member of the joint criminal
25     enterprise.  The Appeal Chamber recalls that in joining, the
 1     Appeal Chamber held that members of a joint criminal enterprise can be
 2     held liable for crimes committed by principal perpetrators who were not
 3     members of the joint criminal enterprise, provided that it has been shown
 4     that the crimes can be imputed to at least one member of the joint
 5     criminal enterprise and that this member, when using a principal
 6     perpetrator, acted in accordance with the common plan.  The establishment
 7     of a link between the crimes in question and the member of the joint
 8     criminal enterprise is a matter to be assessed on a case-by-case basis.
 9             The Appeal Chamber has considered the Trial Chamber's finding on
10     the crimes for which Martic was convicted as a participant in a joint
11     criminal enterprise to determine whether there was a sufficient link
12     between the principal perpetrators of the crimes and Martic or another
13     member of the joint criminal enterprise.  The Trial Chamber considered
14     that the JNA, the police, and other Serb forces active on the territory
15     of the SAO Krajina and the RSK were structured hierarchically and closely
16     coordinated with each other.  Moreover, the Trial Chamber reached
17     findings on the plurality of members of the joint criminal enterprise and
18     the Martic contribution to the joint criminal enterprise.  Finally, the
19     Trial Chamber explicitly found that the objective for uniting Serb areas
20     was implemented "through widespread and systematic armoured attacks and
21     through the commission of acts of violence and intimidation."  While it
22     would have been preferable for the Trial Chamber to make an explicit
23     finding that members of the joint criminal enterprise, when using these
24     forces, were acting in accordance with the common plan, the
25     Appeals Chamber finds that this omission does not invalidate the trial
 1     judgement.  Such attacks could not have been implemented but with the
 2     participation of forces under the joint criminal enterprise members'
 3     control.
 4             Therefore, with respect of the crimes committed by the militia
 5     Krajina, the JNA, the TO, the MUP, or a combination thereof in Hrvatska
 6     Dubica, Cerovljani, Bacin, Burska [phoen], the JNA 9th Corps barracks in
 7     Knin, the hospital in Knin and Titova Korenica facility, the
 8     Appeal Chamber finds the Trial Chamber did not err in establishing the
 9     required the link between Martic and the perpetrators.
10             Similarly, the Appeal Chamber finds no error in the Trial
11     Chamber's finding that the sufficient link existed between the principle
12     perpetrators and Martic with regard to the crimes committed in Lipovaca
13     by Serb paramilitary forces, crimes committed in Skabrnja and Nadin and
14     Saborsko by a combination of JNA soldiers or TO soldiers, or rather,
15     units, and crimes of persecution and deportation committed by the JNA,
16     the TO, the militia Krajina, the MUP, the armoured forces of police
17     officers of the SAO Krajina, and the RSK.  The Appeal Chamber dismisses
18     this sub-ground of appeal in relation to these crimes.
19             However, the Appeals Chamber finds that the Trial Chamber erred
20     in finding that the required link existed between Martic and the
21     principal perpetrators with regard to crimes committed in Cerovljani by
22     armoured Serbs from Zivaja led by Nikola Begovic, crimes committed in
23     Vukovic and Poljanak by unidentified Serbs or soldiers, and crimes
24     committed in Vukovici by a combination of JNA soldiers or TO soldiers and
25     other units.  With regard to these crimes, the Appeal Chamber finds that
 1     the miscarriage of justice resulted and reverses Martic's convictions
 2     under counts 1, 3, 4, 12 and 13, insofar as they relate to these crimes.
 3             I add at this point that the Judge Schomberg repents a separate
 4     opinion on the matter of joint criminal enterprise.
 5             Coming to the sixth ground of appeal, Martic submits that the
 6     Trial Chamber erred in setting out the mental element of ordering a crime
 7     under Article 7(1) of the Statute in holding that indirect intent was
 8     sufficient for ordering.  The Appeals Chamber finds no error in the
 9     approach of the Trial Chamber and dismisses Martic's sixth ground of
10     appeal.
11             In his seventh ground of appeal, Martic claims that the Trial
12     Chamber erred when it found that he ordered the shelling of Zagreb on 2
13     and 3 May 1995.  The Appeal Chamber finds that Martic has not shown that
14     the Trial Chamber erred when it found that Martic himself admitted to
15     ordering the shelling of Zagreb and that circumstantial evidence also
16     supported the finding that he did, in fact, issue an order.
17     Consequently, the Trial Chamber's conclusion, in light of the totality of
18     the evidence, that it was proven beyond a reasonable doubt that Martic
19     ordered the shelling stands.  The Appeal Chamber dismisses Martic's
20     seventh ground of appeal.
21             In its eighth ground of appeal, Martic argues that the shelling
22     of Zagreb constituted a lawful reprisal.  He contends that the Trial
23     Chamber failed to consider the unlawful purpose and effects of
24     Operation Flash launched by Croat forces in breach of a ceasefire
25     agreement.  He argues that in doing so, the Trial Chamber disregarded the
 1     relevant evidence.  In the alternative, he argues that the shelling of
 2     Zagreb was a lawful military action undertaken in self-defence.  After
 3     reviewing the evidence, the Appeal Chamber finds that Martic has not
 4     demonstrated that the Trial Chamber erred when it found that the M-87
 5     Orkan, as used in the circumstances of the case, was an indiscriminate
 6     weapon, incapable of hitting specific targets.  Furthermore, the
 7     Appeal Chamber is satisfied that a reasonable Trial Chamber could have
 8     concluded beyond a reasonable doubt that Martic knew of the effects of
 9     the M-87 Orkan when he ordered the shelling of Zagreb.  This sub-ground
10     of appeal is accordingly dismissed.
11             As for Martic's alternative argument that the shelling of Zagreb
12     was a lawful military action conducted in self-defence, since Martic has
13     failed to show any error in the Trial Chamber's conclusion that he
14     deliberately targeted the civilian population of Zagreb, this submission
15     must fail.  In light of the fact that the prohibition against attacking
16     civilians is absolute, the Appeal Chamber fails to see how Martic's
17     argument, that the Serbs were not aggressors but, rather, were defending
18     themselves, could justify Martic's action in relation to the shelling of
19     Zagreb.  For these reasons and on the basis of the full reasoning in the
20     judgement, the Appeal Chamber dismisses Martic's eighth ground of appeal.
21             In its sole ground of appeal, the Prosecution submits that the
22     Trial Chamber erred in finding that people hors de combat could not be
23     victims of crimes against humanity.  In support of this, the Prosecution
24     argues that the definition of "civilians" when used in the context of
25     crimes against humanity should not be limited to its definition under
 1     international humanitarian law but could also include other categories of
 2     people.  While the Appeal Chamber considers that certain terms have been
 3     defined differently in international humanitarian law and international
 4     criminal law, the fundamental character of the notion of "civilian" in
 5     international humanitarian law and in international criminal law weighs
 6     against giving them different definitions under Article 3 and Article 5
 7     of the Statute.  The Appeal Chamber therefore finds that the definition
 8     of "civilian" contained in Article 50 of Additional Protocol 1 reflects
 9     the definition of "civilian" for the purposes of Article 5 of the Statute
10     and does not include people hors de combat.  That said, the
11     Appeal Chamber notes that the relevance of the civilian population in
12     Article 5 of the Statute is to the requirement that there be a widespread
13     or systematic attack against the civilian population.  There is nothing
14     in the text of Article 5 of the Statute or previous authorities of the
15     Appeals Chamber that requires individual victims of crimes against
16     humanity to be civilians.  A person hors de combat may thus be the victim
17     of an act amounting to a crime against humanity, provided that all the
18     other necessary conditions are met, including that the crimes occur as
19     part of a widespread systematic attack against a civilian population.
20             The Appeals Chamber finds that the Trial Chamber erred in law in
21     this respect, and had it not done so, it would have entered the
22     convictions under Article 5 for murder, count 3; torture, count 6;
23     inhuman acts, count 7; and persecution, count 1, for acts against victims
24     who were hors de combat at the time of the commission of the offence.
25             The Appeal Chamber finds that all elements of these offences have
 1     been met in relation to these victims and enters convictions for these
 2     crimes, with the exception of the conviction for crimes committed in
 3     Benkovac, which the Appeal Chamber reversed.
 4             Turning to sentencing:  In his ninth ground of appeal, Martic
 5     contends that the Trial Chamber erred when it held that Article 24 of the
 6     Statute and Rule 101 of the Rules, which contain general factors that the
 7     Trial Chamber is to take into account when determining sentence, do not
 8     impose binding obligations.  He farther challenges the comparisons of
 9     this case to that of Babic in the determination of his sentence.  The
10     Appeal Chamber rejects these alleged errors.
11             In his 10th ground of appeal, Martic alleges that the Trial
12     Chamber erred in fact and in law in determining the gravity of the
13     offence as well as the aggravating and mitigating factors.  These errors,
14     he claims, invalidate the trial judgement on the occasion and miscarriage
15     of justice.  The Appeal Chamber disagrees with Martic's claims that the
16     Trial Chamber examined the gravity of offences that he did not commit.
17     Since he does not put forward any argument to substantiate his claim,
18     this sub-ground of appeal is dismissed.
19             The Appeal Chamber also dismisses the challenges by Martic as to
20     how the Trial Chamber interpreted and applied the aggravating and
21     mitigating factors in the circumstances of this case.
22             Concerning the impact of the Appeal Chamber findings on
23     sentencing, the Appeal Chamber notes that it has allowed a number of
24     Martic's sub-grounds of appeal and that his fifth ground of appeal, as
25     well as the Prosecutor's sole ground of appeal.  In light of this, the
 1     Appeal Chamber has given consideration to whether an adjustment in
 2     Martic's sentence is appropriate.
 3             The new convictions under Article 5 of the Statute serve to
 4     capture the full culpability of the accused.  However, the Appeal Chamber
 5     finds that this conviction relate to underlying conduct already taken
 6     into account by the Trial Chamber when entering convictions under
 7     Article 3.  Given that the material acts underlying the convictions are
 8     the same, they do not warrant an increase in sentence.
 9             In relation to the convictions reversed on appeal, the
10     Appeal Chamber considered that this reversal has minimal impact on
11     Martic's overall culpability in light of the remaining crimes for which
12     he was convicted and the impact they had on the victims.
13             In view of the foregoing, the Appeal Chamber affirms the sentence
14     imposed by the Trial Chamber.
15             I will now read out the disposition of the appeal judgement.
16             Mr. Martic, will you please rise.
17             This is the disposition:
18             Pursuant to Article 5 of the Statute and Rules 117 and 118 of the
19     Rules, noting the respective written submissions of the parties and the
20     arguments they presented at the appeal hearing on 25 and 26 June, 2008,
21     sitting in open session, allows Milan Martic's fifth ground of appeal in
22     relation to the crimes committed in Benkovac, counts 7, 8 and 9, in part;
23     Cerovljani, counts 1, 12 and 13, in part; Vukovici, counts 1, 3, 4, and
24     12 in part; and Poljanak, counts 1, 3, 4, and 12, in part, as well as in
25     relation to count 10 in part, dismisses all other grounds of appeal
 1     submitted by Milan Martic; allows the Prosecution's alternative ground of
 2     appeal; affirms Milan Martic's sentence of 35 years of imprisonment,
 3     subject to credit being given under Rule 101(C) of the Rules for the
 4     period already spent in detention; orders, in accordance with Rule 103(C)
 5     and Rule 107 of the Rules, that Milan Martic is to remain in the custody
 6     of the Tribunal pending the finalisation of arrangements for his transfer
 7     to the state where his sentence will be served.
 8             Judge Schomberg represents a separate opinion.
 9             You may now sit down.
10             May I ask the Registrar to deliver a copy of the judgement to the
11     parties.
