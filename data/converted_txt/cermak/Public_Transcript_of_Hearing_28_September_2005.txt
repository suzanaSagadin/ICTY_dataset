23            The accused are on pre-trial release, provisional release, there
24    is no need for their appearance this morning.  Now, this I believe is the
25    fifth of the Status Conferences.  It's called because of the requirement
 1    to have regular Status Conferences of this type.  There was yesterday a
 2    Rule 65 ter meeting, and I've received a brief report on that from the
 3    Senior Legal Officer who conducted the conference.  Could I indicate to
 4    the parties that I believe very shortly all Judges who are dealing with
 5    the matter will have completed their consideration of the motion that is
 6    outstanding on the amendment of the indictment and that it is my
 7    expectation that the decision in that issue is imminent.  Now, we come to
 8    matters of disclosure.  Perhaps Mr. Whiting, you might indicate the
 9    current state of the Prosecutor's position about that.
10            MR. WHITING:  Yes, Your Honour.  Of course, disclosure under
11    66(i)(i) has long been completed.  With respect to 66(A)(ii), the
12    Prosecution has disclosed all statements which fall under that Rule to the
13    Defence, both in English and in B/C/S.  So that that -- as of this time,
14    disclosure is complete under that rule.  And of course, as the Rule
15    provides, to the extent that the Prosecution identifies additional
16    witnesses that it intends to call at trial, it will disclose those
17    statements in both languages promptly, immediately.
18            I should say on this point that as Your Honour is no doubt aware,
19    yesterday there was a question from Defence counsel about certain
20    instances where the Prosecution disclosed videotapes of interviews of
21    witnesses without a transcript, and there was a question from Defence
22    counsel about that, whether a transcript was going to be prepared.  The
23    Prosecution has looked into that matter and has also spoken with Defence
24    counsel.  I believe we've resolved the matter.  But for the satisfaction
25    of the Court, what we have determined is that there are approximately
 1    eight or so interviews that were taken in 2005.  The tape of the interview
 2    which contains of course both English and B/C/S has been disclosed and can
 3    be reviewed by Defence counsel.  Transcripts have been requested for those
 4    tapes; however, they're not yet completed.  And as soon as they are
 5    complete, they will be provided to Defence counsel.  And I believe that
 6    that is - and Defence counsel can correct me if I'm wrong - but I believe
 7    that's satisfactory to Defence counsel.
 8            Moving through the other disclosure obligations.  With respect to
 9    Rule 70, the Rule 70 disclosure has been completed with one exception.
10    And the exception has to do with a group of documents that -- for which
11    the Prosecution requested Rule 70 clearance from the United Nations
12    headquarters last spring, but learned that in fact the United Nations
13    headquarters did not have the authority to give the clearance.  Even
14    though the documents had been sent to the headquarters, it -- the
15    clearance has to come from the country of the person who wrote the
16    documents.  And in this case it's the United Kingdom.  A request has been
17    made to the foreign office in London.  I have followed-up on that request
18    and I anticipate that that matter will be resolved within the next month.
19            Rule 68 disclosure is ongoing.  As I told the Senior Legal Officer
20    yesterday, we have reviewed all of the materials directly related to this
21    investigation to determine what, if any, material might fall under Rule
22    68.  And all that material has been disclosed.  And as the -- as the
23    investigate -- as there is additional investigation or other documents are
24    reviewed from other cases and Rule 68 material or potential Rule 68
25    material is identified, that material will of course be disclosed.  And

 1            MR. WHITING:  Well, I -- there's been a little -- just a little
 2    bit of progress but I think there's hope for more.
 3            JUDGE PARKER:  There's certainly room for more.
 4            MR. WHITING:  There's certainly room for more.  The Prosecution --
 5    just for the record, the Prosecution sent a list of proposed agreed facts
 6    to the Defence on August 1st, and the Defence has responded on September
 7    15th.  And because there's two accused and one Prosecution, there is in
 8    fact very little overlap.  I think it amounts to perhaps only three agreed
 9    facts out of 154 that all three parties agree to.  However, individually
10    each of the Defence counsel have agreed to more facts, and more
11    importantly have proposed modifications to some of the facts which the
12    Prosecution still has to consider.  We have not had time yet to evaluate
13    their proposals and look at them.  But there's certainly room for progress
14    there.  And further, I think that certainly for the Defence counsel and to
15    some extent for the Prosecution, the imminent decision about the amendment
16    to the indictment may create further possibilities for agreed facts.  I
17    think Defence counsel have been reluctant to take final positions without
18    knowing what the final indictment would be.  So we're hopeful that further
19    discussions will lead to further agreed -- additional agreed facts.  And
20    at the moment the ball is more or less in our court to evaluate the
21    responses of the Defence and respond.
22            JUDGE PARKER:  Yes.  Now, that's all in respect of facts which you
23    have proposed.  Has there been any proposal for the Prosecution to agreed
24    facts?

 1    proposed modifications to ours.  So those might be considered proposals
 2    from the Defence, but otherwise, no, not to my knowledge.
 3            JUDGE PARKER:  Thank you.

 5    and Mr. Separovic would like to see the decision on the indictment before
 6    you reach any final decisions about agreed facts.  I can understand that

 8    extensive consideration of agreement, both discussing it with
 9    Mr. Separovic and then negotiating perhaps with Mr. Whiting.  Is there

11            MR. PRODANOVIC: [Interpretation] Thank you, Your Honour.  Of

17    don't know whether the problem is in translation or whether the OTP has a
