 2             JUDGE ROBINSON:  Today the Appeals Chamber convenes in accordance
 3     with the Scheduling Order issued on the 17th of November, 2014, pursuant
 4     to Rule 117(D) of the Tribunal's Rules of Procedure and Evidence to
 5     deliver its judgement in the case of Prosecutor versus Popovic, Beara,
 6     Nikolic, Miletic, and Pandurevic.  Let me inform the parties that Judge
 7     Fausto Pocar is unable to sit during this hearing, and therefore the
 8     hearing takes place pursuant to Rule 15 bis (A) of the Rules of Procedure
 9     and Evidence.
10             Following the practice of the Tribunal, I am not going to read
11     out the text of the judgement except for the disposition.  Instead, I
12     will summarise the essential issues on appeal and the central findings of
13     the Appeals Chamber.  I emphasise that this oral summary does not
14     constitute any part of official and authoritative judgement of the
15     Appeals Chamber.  This will be distributed in writing to the parties at
16     the close of this hearing.  In view of the length of this summary, a
17     break or two may be taken during this reading.
18             I begin first by giving the background of the case.  The events
19     giving rise to this case took place in July 1995 in and around Srebrenica
20     and Zepa in the Podrinje region, Eastern Bosnia and Herzegovina.  The
21     Trial Chamber found that these events followed an intense military
22     assault by the Bosnian Serb forces, or BSF, on the United Nations
23     protected areas of Srebrenica and Zepa in July 1995.  Bosnian Muslims
24     fled Srebrenica to the nearby town of Potocari, where the women,
25     children, and the elderly were loaded on to packed buses and transported
 1     away from their homes in Eastern Bosnia.  Thousands of males were
 2     detained in horrific conditions and subsequently summarily executed.  In
 3     Zepa, a series of military attacks also led to the removal of the entire
 4     Bosnian Muslim population, either by transport or by flight.
 5             The Trial Chamber found that a joint criminal enterprise existed
 6     to murder the able-bodied Bosnian Muslim men from Srebrenica in July 1995
 7     or the JCE to murder, and that a joint criminal enterprise existed to
 8     forcibly remove the Bosnian Muslim population from Srebrenica and Zepa in
 9     1995 or JCE to forcibly remove.
10             Now, during the relevant period Mr. Popovic was the chief of
11     security of the Army of the Republika Srpska, or the VRS, Drina Corps.
12     Mr. Beara was the chief of the VRS Main Staff's administration for
13     security.  Mr. Nikolic was the chief of security in the 1st Light
14     Infantry Zvornik Brigade of the Drina Corps.  Mr. Miletic was the chief
15     of the Main Staff's administration for operations and the training.  And
16     Mr. Pandurevic was the commander of the Zvornik Brigade.  The
17     Trial Chamber determined that Messrs. Popovic, Beara, and Nikolic were
18     participants in the JCE to murder and Mr. Miletic was a participant in
19     the JCE to forcibly remove.
20             The Trial Chamber convicted Mr. Popovic and Mr. Beara of
21     committing, through the JCE to murder, genocide, murder as a violation of
22     the laws or customs of war, as well as extermination and persecution as
23     crimes against humanity.  Mr. Popovic and Mr. Beara were both sentenced
24     to life imprisonment.
25             With respect to Mr. Nikolic, the Trial Chamber convicted him of
 1     committing, through the JCE to murder, extermination and persecution as
 2     crimes against humanity, and murder as a violation of the laws or customs
 3     of war.  He was also convicted of aiding and abetting genocide.
 4     Mr. Nikolic was sentenced to 35 years of imprisonment.
 5             The Trial Chamber convicted Mr. Miletic of committing, through
 6     the JCE to forcibly remove, murder, persecution, and forcible transfer as
 7     crimes against humanity.  He was sentenced to 19 years of imprisonment.
 8             With respect to Mr. Pandurevic, the Trial Chamber convicted him
 9     of aiding and abetting murder as a violation of the laws or customs of
10     war, as well as murder, persecution, and forcible transfer as crimes
11     against humanity.  He was also convicted of murder as a crime against
12     humanity and as a violation of the laws or customs of war pursuant to
13     command responsibility.  He was sentenced to 13 years' imprisonment.
14             I come now to the appeals.
15             Following the rendering of the trial judgement on the 10th of
16     June, 2010, the Prosecution, Messrs. Popovic, Beara, Nikolic, Miletic,
17     and Pandurevic appealed the trial judgement.  The Appeals Chamber heard
18     oral submissions of the parties from the 2nd to the 6th of December,
19     2013.
20             I now outline the contentions of the parties, the appellants and
21     the Prosecution.  And I deal first with alleged errors concerning the
22     indictment.  Messrs. Popovic and Miletic advance arguments contending
23     that the Trial Chamber erred in law by convicting them either on the
24     basis of crimes not charged in the indictment or on the basis of
25     allegations not clearly pleaded in the indictment.  The Appeals Chamber
 1     finds that Messrs. Popovic and Miletic have failed to demonstrate that
 2     the Trial Chamber erred.
 3             The Trial Chamber found Mr. Pandurevic guilty of having aided and
 4     abetted the murder of the ten wounded Bosnian Muslim prisoners from the
 5     Milici Hospital, or Milici prisoners, by omission through a failure to
 6     discharge a legal duty.  He argues that, as the Prosecution neither
 7     pleaded nor gave any indication throughout the trial that he was charged
 8     with aiding and abetting by omission through a failure to discharge a
 9     legal duty, the Trial Chamber erred in convicting him for this crime.
10     The Appeals Chamber recalls that the indictment must be read as a whole
11     and finds, Judge Niang dissenting, that it contains allegations that
12     provided notice to Mr. Pandurevic of the material facts underlying the
13     charge that he aided and abetted the murder of the Milici prisoners by
14     omission.  The Appeals Chamber, Judge Niang dissenting, therefore
15     dismisses the relevant part of Mr. Pandurevic's appeal.
16             I turn now to alleged errors relating to the admissibility and
17     weight of the evidence.
18             Messrs. Popovic, Beara, Nikolic, and Miletic present several
19     challenges to the admission of evidence by the Trial Chamber as well as
20     its assessment of weighing of evidence.  Specifically they challenge the
21     Trial Chamber's decisions not to admit certain evidence, admission of
22     statements made pursuant to Rule 92 quater of the Rules, use of untested
23     and uncorroborated evidence, and admission of intercepts and other
24     documentary evidence.  The Appeals Chamber finds no error in the
25     Trial Chamber's exercise of its broad discretion, it dismisses all
 1     challenges considered in this section of the appeal judgement.
 2             I turn now to alleged errors concerning witness credibility.
 3             Messrs. Popovic, Beara, Nikolic, and Miletic present challenges
 4     concerning the overall credibility of witnesses who testified in this
 5     case.  In particular, they challenge the Trial Chamber's assessment of
 6     the credibility of witnesses PW-168, Momir Nikolic, PW-101, and
 7     Srecko Acimovic.  Having considered the parties' submissions, the Appeals
 8     Chamber finds that the appellants have not shown that the Trial Chamber
 9     erred and dismisses all challenges regarding the overall credibility of
10     witnesses.
11             I turn now to alleged errors in relation to the evidence
12     regarding the number of deceased.
13             Mr. Popovic contends that the Trial Chamber erred in its findings
14     on the number of persons executed at specific execution sites.
15     Messrs. Popovic, Beara, and Nikolic all challenge the Trial Chamber's
16     findings on the total number of persons executed based on forensic and
17     demographic evidence.  This Chamber dismisses all challenges to the total
18     number of deceased.
19             I now deal with other evidentiary matters.
20             On the issue of alibi, Mr. Popovic submits that the Trial Chamber
21     erred in its consideration of his alibis for the evening of 14 July 1995
22     and 23rd July 1995.  Specifically, he argues that the Trial Chamber
23     failed to consider all the evidence on the record in rejecting his alibi
24     that he was at the forward command post in Krivace on 14 July 1995 and
25     therefore could not have participated in the killings at Orahovac.
 1     Likewise, he argues that the Trial Chamber erred in dismissing his alibi
 2     for the 23rd July, 1995, by disregarding evidence that he was in a
 3     meeting at the time of the killings in Bisina.  This Chamber finds that
 4     Mr. Popovic has not shown that the Trial Chamber disregarded evidence
 5     concerning his alibis or demonstrated that any error by the Trial Chamber
 6     resulted in a miscarriage of justice.  It therefore dismisses his
 7     arguments.
 8             Mr. Beara also contests the Trial Chamber's consideration of his
 9     alibi for the 13th and 14th July 1995 and its finding that he was
10     actively engaged in murder operations in Bratunac and Zvornik.  He argues
11     that the Trial Chamber erroneously assessed and disregarded the Defence
12     evidence and inappropriately shifted the burden of proof to the Defence
13     by requiring it to prove his alibi beyond reasonable doubt.  After
14     considering Mr. Beara's arguments, the Appeals Chamber dismisses all of
15     his challenges.
16             Messrs. Nikolic and Beara also present challenges to the
17     Trial Chamber's assessment of expert evidence.  Mr. Nikolic argues that
18     the Trial Chamber disregarded the evidence of a military expert, while
19     Mr. Beara disputes the Trial Chamber's approach to identification
20     evidence.  Further, Mr. Miletic contests the Trial Chamber's reliance on
21     intercept evidence and contends that it arrived at erroneous conclusions.
22     Having considered the parties' submissions, the Appeals Chamber finds
23     that the appellants have not shown that the Trial Chamber erred and
24     dismisses all challenges regarding expert evidence, identification
25     evidence, and intercept evidence.
 1             I now address the appellants' and the Prosecution's challenges to
 2     the Trial Chamber's findings on the crimes perpetrated against the
 3     Bosnian Muslims of Eastern Bosnia.
 4             First, genocide.  The Trial Chamber concluded that members of the
 5     BSF committed genocide against the Muslims of Eastern Bosnia, which
 6     constituted a substantial component of Bosnian Muslims as a group.  The
 7     Trial Chamber was satisfied beyond a reasonable doubt that
 8     Messrs. Popovic and Beara committed genocide through their participation
 9     in the JCE to murder with genocidal intent.  The Trial Chamber found that
10     Mr. Nikolic did not have genocidal intent but it concluded that he aided
11     and abetted genocide.
12             Mr. Beara argues that the Trial Chamber erred in finding that the
13     targeted group of Bosnian Muslims was a substantial part of the entire
14     group.  He contends in particular that the Trial Chamber ignored the
15     numeric size of the targeted group, wrongly assessed the strategic
16     importance of the Srebrenica enclave, and widened the scope of the
17     targeted group as defined by the indictment.  This Chamber dismisses all
18     of Mr. Beara's challenges and finds that he fails to show an error on the
19     part of the Trial Chamber.
20             Mr. Nikolic argues that the Trial Chamber erred in failing to
21     identify state policy as an essential element of genocide.  The Appeals
22     Chamber finds that the question of whether the existence of state policy
23     is required for genocide has already been considered by the Tribunal, and
24     as a policy is not a legal requirement, then it follows that state policy
25     is not a legal requirement of genocide.  The Appeals Chamber also finds
 1     that Mr. Nikolic has not established the existence of cogent reasons to
 2     depart from this jurisprudence or that the Trial Chamber erred.
 3             Messrs. Popovic, Beara, and Nikolic also challenge, to varying
 4     degrees, the Trial Chamber's findings of the genocidal intent of the BSF.
 5     Notably, Mr. Nikolic argues that the acts of killing and infliction of
 6     serious bodily and mental harm against the Muslims of Eastern Bosnia were
 7     not perpetrated with genocidal intent by submitting that the
 8     Trial Chamber failed to consider that no genocidal acts were perpetrated
 9     against the Bosnian Muslims of Zepa, that the column of 10.000 Bosnian
10     Muslims was allowed to pass through the defence lines of the
11     Zvornik Brigade, and that numerous prisoner exchanges occurred in mid- to
12     late July 1995.  Mr. Nikolic also argues that the Trial Chamber ignored
13     significant recent precedents, establishing that the killing of a group
14     of men while forcibly transferring the remainder of the population does
15     not evince genocidal intent.  This Chamber considers that the
16     Trial Chamber found that the Muslims of Eastern Bosnia, including the
17     inhabitants of Zepa, were found to be victims of the genocidal enterprise
18     and it can see no error in the Trial Chamber's consideration of the
19     opening of the corridor to allow the column to pass through or the
20     exchange of prisoners.  The Appeals Chamber also considers that there was
21     no obligation on the Trial Chamber to explicitly consider the authorities
22     relied upon by Mr. Nikolic and that he fails to show an error.  In sum,
23     the Appeals Chamber dismisses the appellant's arguments concerning the
24     genocidal intent of the BSF.
25             I now address the challenges to the appellants' liability for
 1     genocide.
 2             Mr. Popovic disputes the Trial Chamber's finding that he
 3     possessed genocidal intent and, among other things, argues that it
 4     disregarded evidence as well as analysed his use of a derogatory term out
 5     of context.  Mr. Beara argues that the Trial Chamber erred in finding
 6     that he had genocidal intent as it reached its conclusion on his
 7     knowledge of the murder operation solely on the basis of his position in
 8     the VRS, erroneously drew inferences from certain exhibits and evidence,
 9     and failed to take into account the legitimate military aims of attacks
10     on the Srebrenica enclave.
11             Mr. Beara also argues that the Trial Chamber erred in convicting
12     him for genocide after acquitting him of forcible transfer as genocidal
13     intent in the present case can only be inferred from the combined intent
14     to murder the Bosnian Muslim men and forcibly transfer the women,
15     children, and the elderly.  Having considered the arguments made by
16     Messrs. Popovic and Beara, as well as the Prosecution, this Chamber finds
17     that the appellants have not shown an error by the Trial Chamber.  It
18     dismisses their arguments.
19             The Prosecution presents various challenges to the
20     Trial Chamber's finding that Mr. Nikolic lacked genocidal intent.  It
21     argues that the Trial Chamber failed to apply nine accepted factors for
22     inferring genocidal intent, relied on irrelevant factual and legal
23     considerations, and erred in fact as no reasonable Trial Chamber could
24     have concluded that Mr. Nikolic lacked genocidal intent.  This Chamber
25     finds that although the Trial Chamber did not enter contradictory
 1     findings regarding Mr. Nikolic's involvement in the movement of prisoners
 2     from Bratunac to Zvornik and whether he was directly implicated in the
 3     killings at the Branjevo Military Farm, these contradictory findings do
 4     not occasion a miscarriage of justice, in light of the Trial Chamber's
 5     overall reasoning.  Further, the Appeals Chamber considers that the
 6     Trial Chamber erred in considering as a factor negating genocidal intent
 7     the fact that the Milici prisoners remained alive when in Mr. Nikolic's
 8     custody because of the lack of evidence or findings on his role in the
 9     matter.  This error, however, does not occasion a miscarriage of justice,
10     considering the wide range of evidence the Trial Chamber relied on to
11     conclude on Mr. Nikolic's mens rea.  The Appeals Chamber is not convinced
12     by the remainder of the Prosecution's arguments and, Judge Niang
13     dissenting, dismisses its challenges regarding Mr. Nikolic's mens rea for
14     genocide.
15             I turn now to the parties' submissions regarding conspiracy to
16     commit genocide.
17             The Trial Chamber found Messrs. Popovic and Beara criminally
18     responsible for conspiracy to commit genocide but declined to enter
19     convictions against them, concluding that the full criminality of the
20     accused is accounted for by a conviction for genocide.
21             The Prosecution submits the Trial Chamber erred in law.  As
22     conspiracy to commit genocide and genocide are distinct crimes, the
23     Appeals Chamber, Judge Niang dissenting, finds that it was necessary to
24     enter convictions against Messrs. Popovic and Beara for conspiracy to
25     commit genocide in order to reflect their full culpability.  By failing
 1     to do so, the Trial Chamber erred in law.  Furthermore, the Appeals
 2     Chamber discerns no error in the Trial Chamber's underlying factual
 3     findings and dismisses Messrs. Popovic's and Beara's arguments to the
 4     contrary.
 5             The Appeals Chamber, Judge Niang dissenting, therefore grants the
 6     Prosecution's ground of appeal 6 and, Judge Pocar dissenting, enters
 7     convictions against Messrs. Popovic and Beara for conspiracy to commit
 8     genocide.
 9             Next, crimes against humanity.
10             And I deal first with the parties' submissions.  The
11     Trial Chamber found beyond a reasonable doubt that there was a widespread
12     and systematic attack directed against the Bosnian Muslim civilian
13     population of Srebrenica and Zepa commencing with the issuance of
14     Directive 7.  The Chamber found that the attack included the following
15     components:  The strangulation of the enclaves through restrictions on
16     humanitarian supplies, the gradual weakening and disabling of the
17     United Nations Protection Force, or UNPROFOR; and a military assault on
18     the enclaves culminating in the removal of thousands of people from
19     Srebrenica and Zepa.  In addition, the Trial Chamber found that the
20     military assault, on its own, constituted a widespread and systematic
21     attack against the civilian population.  Messrs. Beara, Nikolic, and
22     Miletic were found responsible for certain crimes against humanity and
23     present challenges to the related findings of the Trial Chamber.
24             Mr. Beara's main contentions are that the Trial Chamber erred in
25     finding that:  1, the actions taken against the military-aged Bosnian
 1     Muslim men in Potocari and the column of Bosnian Muslim men fleeing
 2     towards Tuzla formed part of a widespread and systematic attack against a
 3     civilian population; 2, he satisfied the knowledge requirement for
 4     commission of a crime against humanity; 3, he possessed the mens rea for
 5     extermination; and 4, he had the specific discriminatory intent required
 6     for the crime of persecution.  After considering the submissions made,
 7     the Appeals Chamber finds that Mr. Beara has failed to demonstrate that
 8     the Trial Chamber erred and dismisses his contentions in their entirety.
 9             Mr. Nikolic challenges the Trial Chamber's factual finding that
10     his acts were clearly tied to the widespread and systematic attack on
11     Srebrenica and that he knew that this was the case.  He also argues that
12     the Trial Chamber erred in concluding that he had the requisite mens rea
13     for persecution.  Having considered the parties' arguments, the Appeals
14     Chamber finds that Mr. Nikolic has failed to demonstrate that the
15     Trial Chamber erred and therefore dismisses his arguments.
16             May I now turn to the challenges presented by Mr. Miletic.  This
17     Chamber finds that he has failed to demonstrate that the Trial Chamber
18     erred in linking Directive 7 to the attacks on the Srebrenica and Zepa
19     enclaves; in finding that all VRS military activity around the enclaves
20     constituted an attack on the civilian population without distinguishing
21     legitimate military actions; and in finding that a plan to restrict
22     humanitarian aid and the resupply of UNPROFOR constituted part of the
23     attack on the civilian population and that his acts were part of this
24     plan.  The Appeals Chamber also finds no merit in Mr. Miletic's argument
25     that the Trial Chamber erred in finding that he knew of the widespread
 1     and systematic attack against the civilian population and that he knew
 2     that his actions comprised part of the attack.
 3             Regarding Mr. Miletic's challenges to the Trial Chamber's
 4     findings on persecution, this Chamber first dismisses his submissions
 5     that the Trial Chamber erred in concluding that he had discriminatory
 6     intent, that the JCE to forcibly remove was partly implemented by acts of
 7     cruel and inhumane treatment, and that he intended those acts.
 8     Mr. Miletic also alleged errors in relation to the underlying act of
 9     terrorising civilians.  He submits that the Trial Chamber failed to
10     establish that he had the specific intent required for persecution
11     through terrorising civilians as it had to establish the intent to commit
12     the underlying act and the intent to discriminate.  The Appeals Chamber
13     first notes that a Trial Chamber does not need to establish the elements
14     of the underlying acts, including the mens rea.  All that is required is
15     establishing that the underlying act was deliberately carried out with
16     discriminatory intent.  Mr. Miletic has failed to show that the
17     Trial Chamber erred in establishing his discriminatory intent for
18     persecution through terrorising civilians, that he played a role in
19     disseminating terror, and that he intended to do so.  Mr. Miletic made
20     further submissions disputing the Trial Chamber's consideration of
21     shelling and sniping directed at the civilian populations of Srebrenica
22     in the months preceding the fall of the enclave as part of the actus reus
23     of terrorising civilians.  This Chamber considers, inter alia, that the
24     Trial Chamber established, with sufficient specificity, the numerous
25     incidents of shelling and sniping of the civilian population of
 1     Srebrenica in the months preceding the attack on the enclave and that
 2     these incidents of shelling and sniping were of sufficient gravity to
 3     constitute an underlying act of persecution.  This Chamber therefore
 4     dismisses Mr. Miletic's challenges to the Trial Chamber's findings on the
 5     underlying act of terrorising civilians.
 6             Mr. Miletic also disputes the Trial Chamber's findings on
 7     forcible transfer as a crime against humanity.  The Appeals Chamber finds
 8     that Mr. Miletic has failed to demonstrate that the Trial Chamber erred
 9     in finding that the actions directed against the civilian component of
10     the column constituted a crime against humanity.  The Trial Chamber found
11     that able-bodied Bosnian Muslim men fled Zepa and crossed the Drina River
12     into Serbia.  The Trial Chamber concluded that this movement across the
13     Drina River amounted to forcible transfer and had the required nexus with
14     the widespread and systematic attack on the civilian population.  The
15     Appeals Chamber notes that the Trial Chamber made no finding that the men
16     who crossed the Drina River included any civilians, and considered that,
17     in light of the facts of the case, no reasonable trier of fact could have
18     reached as the only reasonable inference the conclusion that the nexus
19     requirement for crimes against humanity had been established.  This
20     Chamber therefore finds that the Trial Chamber erred in this regard.  It
21     grants Mr. Miletic's appeal in relevant part.  His convictions for
22     persecution and forcible transfer as crimes against humanity in relation
23     to the men who crossed the Drina River are therefore reversed and the
24     impact of this reversal, if any, will be addressed below.
25             I turn now to Mr. Beara's challenges regarding murder as a
 1     violation of the laws or customs of war.
 2             I will now address these challenges.
 3             War crimes.  Mr. Beara submits that the Trial Chamber erred in
 4     finding him guilty of murder of Bosnian Muslim men from Potocari and
 5     members of the column of men heading for Tuzla.  Notably, he argues that
 6     the Trial Chamber erred by listing persons as victims who were in fact
 7     taking an active part in hostilities at the time they were killed.  The
 8     Trial Chamber was satisfied that since the Bosnian Muslim men from the
 9     column or who had been separated from their families at Potocari had been
10     killed after their surrender or capture and during the period of their
11     detention, they were not taking an active part in hostilities at the time
12     the crimes were committed.  The Appeals Chamber finds that Mr. Beara has
13     not shown that the Trial Chamber erred and dismisses his arguments.  The
14     Appeals Chamber also dismisses Mr. Beara's arguments that he did not know
15     that the victims were not taking an active part in hostilities when the
16     murders were committed and that he did not possess the required mens rea.
17             I now address the individual criminal responsibility of the
18     appellants and I deal first with joint criminal enterprise to murder.
19             The Trial Chamber found that the plan to murder Bosnian Muslim
20     men already existed on the 12th July 1995 and that the separation of the
21     Bosnian Muslim men that started later that day marked the commencement of
22     the implementation of the plan to murder.  The Trial Chamber also found
23     that the plan to murder subsequently expanded to include the males
24     captured from the column on the 13th July 1995.  Messrs. Popovic, Beara,
25     and Nikolic presented various challenges to these and related findings on
 1     the existence and implementation of the plan to murder.  This Chamber
 2     dismisses all of the appellants' arguments concerning this aspect of the
 3     trial judgement.  In particular, it rejects the submissions of
 4     Messrs. Popovic and Beara concerning the Trial Chamber's reliance on
 5     Witness Momir Nikolic's evidence, alleged errors by the Trial Chamber
 6     concerning the separation process, and the Trial Chamber's consideration
 7     of the detention conditions in Potocari as further evidence of the plan
 8     to murder.  Messrs. Popovic, Beara, and Nikolic all dispute the
 9     Trial Chamber's findings regarding the extent and the expansion of the
10     plan to murder.  Having examined the submissions of the parties, this
11     Chamber finds that the appellants have failed to show that no reasonable
12     trier of fact could have concluded as the Trial Chamber did and dismisses
13     their arguments accordingly.
14             The Trial Chamber found that the BSF killed several thousand
15     Bosnian Muslim men in locations across the Bratunac and Zvornik areas.
16     It also concluded that it did not have evidence in respect of each
17     killing site to determine whether the physical perpetrators of these
18     killings were themselves members of the JCE but considered whether each
19     killing formed part of the common purpose even when the crimes were
20     committed by persons outside of the JCE or by unknown members of the JCE.
21     Mr. Beara challenges the findings regarding the scope of the JCE to
22     murder.  This Chamber dismisses Mr. Beara's contentions that the killings
23     at the Kravica warehouse and the Cerska Valley were not committed in
24     furtherance of the JCE to murder.
25             Regarding the killings at the Jadar River, the Appeals Chamber
 1     finds that the Trial Chamber erred in fact by finding that the
 2     Bratunac Brigade was involved in these killings and thus the link between
 3     the killings and the JCE to murder is no longer readily apparent from the
 4     reasoning of the Trial Chamber.  However, this error does not result in a
 5     miscarriage of justice, as the Appeals Chamber is satisfied that in light
 6     of the close co-operation of the VRS and MUP forces in the lead-up to the
 7     killings at the Jadar River and in the implementation of the common
 8     purpose of the murder plan, a reasonable trier of fact could have found a
 9     link to at least one member of the JCE.
10             Mr. Beara also argues that the killings of six men at Trnovo were
11     not a part of the common purpose of the JCE to murder as the Skorpions

16     dissenting, thus dismisses all of Mr. Pandurevic's arguments related to
17     command responsibility.
18             The Prosecution argues that the Trial Chamber erred by failing to
19     adjudicate or provide a reasoned opinion regarding Mr. Pandurevic's
20     command responsibility for failing to prevent the Zvornik Brigade
21     members' participation from noon on 15 July through 16 July 1995, in
22     persecution through cruel and inhumane treatment, and requests the
23     Appeals Chamber to convict Mr. Pandurevic for these crimes.  The
24     Appeals Chamber finds that the Trial Chamber failed to enter any specific
25     findings and therefore failed to give a reasoned opinion on this issue
 1     and will therefore review the relevant factual findings of the
 2     Trial Chamber and, when necessary, the evidence in the trial record.
 3     Having done so, the Appeals Chamber, Judge Niang dissenting, finds beyond
 4     a reasonable doubt that Mr. Pandurevic failed to take the necessary and
 5     reasonable measures to prevent his subordinates from participating in
 6     persecution through cruel and inhumane treatment from noon on 15 July to
 7     16 July 1995, as required to discharge his duty as a commander.
 8     Therefore, the Appeals Chamber, Judge Niang dissenting, grants the
 9     Prosecution's appeal in this regard, reverses Mr. Pandurevic's acquittal,
10     and, Judge Pocar dissenting, enters a new conviction against
11     Mr. Pandurevic.
12             The Trial Chamber found that the Prosecution did not prove beyond
13     a reasonable doubt that Mr. Pandurevic failed to take the necessary and
14     reasonable measures to punish his subordinates as required of a
15     commander.  The Prosecution argues that the Trial Chamber erred in its
16     application of the necessary and reasonable measures to punish standard
17     which, if correctly applied, would result in convicting Mr. Pandurevic
18     for failing to punish the crimes committed by his subordinates from 13 to
19     17 July 1995.  The Appeals Chamber finds that the Trial Chamber's finding
20     rests on two implicit legal findings.  The first is that a measure that
21     would ordinarily be considered necessary and reasonable, such as
22     reporting crimes committed by a superior's subordinates to a competent
23     authority or up the chain of command, may in certain circumstances be
24     unreasonable if the evidence indicates that such a competent authority
25     may be subject to interference by the persons or bodies responsible for
 1     ordering or planning the crimes.  However, international law requires
 2     commanders to take some action to punish their subordinates for
 3     committing crimes, even in these circumstances.  The Appeals Chamber
 4     therefore finds that the Trial Chamber erred in this regard.
 5             The second implicit legal finding is that measures that would
 6     normally not be sufficient to fulfil the duty to punish might in certain
 7     circumstances be considered as the only necessary and reasonable measures
 8     available.  This Chamber recalls that a superior's duty to punish the
 9     perpetrators of a crime includes, if the superior has no power to
10     sanction, an obligation to report them to the competent authorities,
11     which would only suffice to fulfil the duty if such a report is likely to
12     trigger an investigation or initiate disciplinary or criminal
13     proceedings.  This Chamber finds that the Trial Chamber erred by failing
14     to consider if the measures Mr. Pandurevic took were in fact capable of
15     contributing to an investigation or punishment of those who committed the
16     crimes.
17             Having identified two implicit errors of law in the
18     Trial Chamber's reasoning, the Appeals Chamber, Judge Niang dissenting,
19     finds that Mr. Pandurevic failed to take the necessary and reasonable
20     measures to punish his subordinates as required to discharge his duty as
21     a commander.  Specifically, the Appeals Chamber finds that these measures
22     included reporting the crimes of his subordinates:  1, directly to the
23     military prosecutor; 2, to Mr. Pandurevic's higher-ranking officer,
24     Radislav Krstic; and 3, to the MUP so that they could investigate or pass
25     it on to the competent authority.  The Appeals Chamber, Judge Niang
 1     dissenting, therefore grants in part the Prosecution's appeal in this
 2     respect and finds that Mr. Pandurevic -- I read that again - and finds
 3     Mr. Pandurevic responsible as a superior for the crimes of his
 4     subordinates between 13 and 16 July 1995.
 5             However, as the Appeals Chamber, Judge Niang dissenting, has
 6     found Mr. Pandurevic responsible for aiding and abetting extermination as
 7     a crime against humanity, murder as a violation of the laws or customs of
 8     war, and persecution through murder as a crime against humanity for the
 9     killing of Bosnian Muslim prisoners from noon on 15 July to 16 July 1995,
10     and the Appeals Chamber has consequently entered new convictions against
11     Mr. Pandurevic for these crimes, it will not enter a conviction based on
12     command responsibility for the very same crimes.
13             The Appeals Chamber, Judge Pocar dissenting, therefore enters new
14     convictions against Mr. Pandurevic for failing to punish the crimes
15     perpetrated by his subordinates between 13 July 1995 and noon on 15 July
16     1995, for extermination as a crime against humanity, murder as a
17     violation of the laws or customs of war, and persecution as a crime
18     against humanity through murder.  This Chamber, Judge Pocar dissenting,
19     further enters a new conviction against Mr. Pandurevic for failure to
20     punish persecution as a crime against humanity through cruel and inhumane
21     treatment as perpetrated by his subordinates from 13 July to 16 July
22     1995.  The impact of these findings, if any, will be addressed below.
23             Now, that concludes the summary of the Appeals Chamber's findings
24     regarding the individual criminal responsibility of the appellants.  I
25     now address a few miscellaneous grounds of appeal.
 1             Mr. Miletic contends that the Trial Chamber violated his right to
 2     a fair trial by not issuing a decision on a motion he filed and that it
 3     also violated his right to obtain a public judgement as it only made a
 4     redacted version of the trial judgement available to the public.  This
 5     Chamber finds that although the Trial Chamber erred in not clearly
 6     deciding the motion, Mr. Miletic has not demonstrated that the
 7     Trial Chamber violated his right to a fair trial or his right to obtain a
 8     public judgement.  It dismisses his relevant grounds of appeal.
 9             And I turn now to the contentions of the appellants and the
10     Prosecution regarding sentencing.
11             The appellants and the Prosecution presented various challenges
12     to the factors considered by the Trial Chamber in the exercise of its
13     sentencing discretion, alleging errors concerning the sentencing goals,
14     the gravity of crimes and involvement of the appellants, aggravating
15     circumstances, mitigating factors, and the sentencing practice in the
16     former Yugoslavia as well as to the sentences given by the Tribunal in
17     similar cases.  Having considered the parties' arguments carefully, the
18     Appeals Chamber finds that the Trial Chamber failed to properly exercise
19     its discretion in its discussion on Mr. Nikolic's sentence when arriving
20     at the conclusion that there is some evidence that Mr. Nikolic was
21     disturbed by what he was asked to do.  However, this Chamber considers
22     that this error did not result in a miscarriage of justice.  Furthermore,
23     it finds merit in Mr. Miletic's submission that the Trial Chamber erred
24     in law by considering his use of his authority within the VRS Main Staff
25     as an aggravating circumstance, thus effectively double-counting it, as
 1     it was already considered in the findings on the gravity of his crime.
 2     Consequently, it is unnecessary to consider whether the Trial Chamber
 3     erred in concluding that Mr. Miletic abused his authority.  His relevant
 4     ground of appeal is therefore granted in part and I will address the
 5     impact of this error, if any, below.
 6             This Chamber dismisses all other grounds of appeal raised by the
 7     appellants and the Prosecution relating to sentencing.
 8             I now address the impact of the Appeals Chamber's findings on
 9     sentencing.  In this context, the Appeals Chamber recalls, in addition to
10     its findings on Mr. Miletic's appeal against sentencing, it has reversed
11     certain convictions in relation to each appellant.  The Appeals Chamber
12     has also entered convictions in relation to Messrs. Popovic, Beara,
13     Miletic, and Pandurevic.  The Appeals Chamber considers that, in light of
14     the circumstances of this case, as well as the gravity of the crimes for
15     which each appellant is responsible, the sentences for Messrs. Popovic,
16     Beara, Nikolic, and Pandurevic are not impacted, while a limited
17     reduction is warranted for Mr. Miletic.
