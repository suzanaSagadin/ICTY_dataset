 5            JUDGE ROBINSON:  I begin this morning's proceedings with the
 6    Chamber's ruling on the submission that Prosecution witnesses should not
 7    be allowed to make an in-court identification of the accused Sikirica.
 8    This submission was based on Articles 20(1), 21(2) as well as Rules 89 A
 9    to D and 95.
10            These provisions guarantee the accused the right to fair trial
11    and, while allowing a Chamber to admit any evidence of probative value,
12    they also empower a Chamber to exclude evidence if its probative value is
13    substantially outweighed by the need to ensure a fair trial.  They also
14    require the exclusion of the admission of evidence which is obtained by
15    methods that cast doubt on its reliability or if its submission would
16    seriously damage the integrity of the proceedings.
17            The essence of the submission is that it would be unfair to allow
18    in-court identification of the accused since it was inevitable that a
19    witness who had not identified the accused before the trial would pick him
20    out in court.  We were referred to the unfavourable view that common law
21    courts took of in-court identification and to other methods of
22    identification, such as a photo spread, that were open to the
23    Prosecution.
24            The Prosecutor, on the other hand, argued that it would be wrong
25    to adopt a decision that would bar the admission of evidence of all
 1    witnesses who identified the accused in court.  Rather, the Prosecutor
 2    argued that the evidence of each witness should be considered on its own
 3    merits.  The Prosecutor also advanced arguments as to why, in the
 4    circumstances of this case, in-court identification should be allowed.
 5    However, in light of the view that the Chamber takes as to how this matter
 6    should be disposed of, it is not necessary to outline those arguments.
 7            The Chamber holds that it would be wrong to make a decision whose
 8    effect would be to exclude evidence of all witnesses which identify the
 9    accused in court.  The Chamber will consider the admission of such
10    evidence on an individual basis, and in arriving at its decision, will
11    take into account the provisions of the Statute including Articles 20, 21,
12    as well as Rules 89 and 95, and all the relevant factors such as whether
13    the witness knew the accused prior to the camp incidents and the length of
14    time which the witness spent in the camp along with the accused,
15    Sikirica.  That's the ruling.
