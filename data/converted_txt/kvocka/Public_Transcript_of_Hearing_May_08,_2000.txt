         14       A.   Every day.  Every day.
         15       Q.   Could you describe for the Court the kind of
         16  bruising or injuries you observed on these detainees?
         17       A.   People were beaten with sticks, with boots,
         18  with rifle butts.  It depended how frequently.  Over
         19  their bodies, chest, back, arms, head, and so on.
         20       Q.   As a result of this, what did you observe on
         21  their bodies as a result of these beatings, what kinds
         22  of injuries or bruising?
         23       A.   They were black and blue, and there were
         24  wounds and bruises.
         25       Q.   Now, you began to see such bruising and such
          1  injuries how soon after you had been brought to the
          2  camp?
          3       A.   From the very first day.
          4       Q.   These people that you saw with these bruises
          5  and these injuries, when, if ever, did you see these
          6  persons given any kind of medical care for this
          7  bruising and injuries?
          8       A.   I did not see anyone giving that kind of
          9  assistance.
         10       Q.   During the period that you were detained in
         11  Omarska, how often, if ever, did you hear sounds as of
         12  someone in pain?
         13       A.   Every day.
         14       Q.   What did you hear?
         15       A.   We heard moans.  "Ah, oh, what am I doing
         16  here?  What have I done to deserve this?" and that sort
         17  of thing.
         18       Q.   How soon after you arrived in the camp did
         19  you begin to hear these kinds of sounds?
         20       A.   Those sounds and screams of pain could be
         21  heard from the first day.  And when the interrogations
         22  started upstairs, when people were taken upstairs and
         23  when this was to happen, people fled from one end of
         24  the room to the other to avoid it.
         25       Q.   You say that when interrogations began you
          1  could hear it from upstairs.  Upstairs in what
          2  building?
          3       A.   The same building in which I was detained,
          4  the building of the restaurant.
          5       Q.   Now, you indicated that you arrived at
          6  Omarska on the 30th of May.  When was the first time
          7  you received food after your arrival at Omarska?
          8       A.   After two days, I think.  Two or three after
          9  our arrival.
         10       Q.   Now, after that time, after your second or
         11  third day, how often did you get food?
         12       A.   We got food every day, but it was not
         13  possible for everyone to get a meal.  Either there was
         14  water missing or bread or food, until the next time a
         15  meal was cooked.
         16       Q.   And what was the quality of the food that you
         17  received?
         18       A.   It was terrible.
         19       Q.   What type of food did you receive?
         20       A.   There was beans and cabbage, but it was of
         21  very poor quality.  You would get a small quantity of
         22  beans.  You get it hot.  You have to swallow it up in a
         23  minute, and there's not really anything to eat.  You
         24  get one-eighth of a loaf of bread every day, sometimes
         25  once in two days.  Sometimes there was electricity,
          1  sometimes there wasn't, so no cooking could be done.
          2  Bread could not be baked.
          3       Q.   Did you yourself ever observe detainees being
          4  physically abused as they would go to or from their
          5  meal?
          6       A.   Yes.
          7       Q.   How often did you observe this?
          8       A.   Whenever we went to get lunch, you'd get a
          9  blow.  For instance, we would go in groups of 30, and
         10  then we would have three minutes to get there, to eat,
         11  and to come back, and to get a blow with a rifle butt,
         12  a kick.  It depended.  This is what I personally
         13  experience.
         14       Q.   And based on your observation and experience,
         15  on what shifts did this kind of abuse occur?
         16       A.   Well, one couldn't say that it was one
         17  particular shift that was better than another in its
         18  treatment.  In fact, there was one shift they said, of
         19  which Milojica was the leader, was a good shift.  Mujo
         20  would say, "Come on, go out.  Go out."  But I couldn't
         21  be bothered sometimes.  I didn't leave.
         22            Then I went out, and as soon as I went out,
         23  then somebody said, "You must all go to the pista."
         24  And we sat down there, and then Simo Kevic came.  He
         25  said something to the guards.  I don't know what.
          1  Muharem Murselovic was in front of me.  He hit him with
          2  a stick on the back.  When he saw me, so he said,
          3  "Look, (redacted) is there too."  Then he started
          4  beating (redacted).  And then I said after that,
          5  "There won't be any good shifts any more."  So I hid.
          6  I avoided going out altogether.
          7       Q.   Now, you mentioned that you were told or that
          8  people said that one shift, of which Milojica was the
          9  leader, was a good shift.  Milojica who?
         10       A.   Milojica, Krle.  Krle.  That's, I think, what
         11  they called him.  Yes, Kos.  Kos.  Milojica Kos.  That
         12  was his shift.
         13       Q.   Milojica Kos, Krle, did you know him before
         14  you went to the camp?
         15       A.   No.
         16       Q.   During the time that you were in the camp,

         18       A.   Only in passing, as he passed by.
         19       Q.   How was it that you came to know and
         20  recognise him in passing?
         21       A.   He often wore --

         12       Q.   And how much room did you have to move about

         25  was sick.  So he said, "Put him outside."  So I spent

          2  back inside.
          3       Q.   While you were in the garage, how were you

          5       A.   We would get water through the small window,

          7       A.   People from amongst us who were detained in
