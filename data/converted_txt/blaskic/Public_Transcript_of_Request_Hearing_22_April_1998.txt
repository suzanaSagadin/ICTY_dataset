         12            In this regard, it is important to restate
         13  briefly what we are dealing with here is a binding
         14  order issued under Article 29(2), rather than merely a
         15  request for cooperation under Article 29(1).  The scope
         16  of requests which may be the subject of a binding order
         17  under Article 29(2) was articulated by the Appeals
         18  Chamber in its original 29th October 1997 judgment.
         19            In that judgment, the Appeals Chamber
         20  specifically considered "whether binding orders for the
         21  production of documents addressed to State can be broad
         22  in scope or whether they must instead be specific".
         23            The Chamber made clear that the
         24  United States-style discovery requests, seeking broad
         25  categories of unspecified documents that may or may not
          1  contain relevant evidence, are not appropriate for
          2  requests or orders for the production of documents
          3  under Article 29(2) of the Tribunal's Statute.
          4            Instead the Appeals Chamber established a
          5  series of threshold requirements that must be met by
          6  any Prosecutorial request for documents to be issued to
          7  a State in the form of a binding order.
          8            These requirements are as follows:
          9            (1) the request must "identify specific
         10  documents and not broad categories."  That is to say,
         11  the documents must both be "identified as far as
         12  possible and in addition be limited in number".
         13            As a general rule, the Appeals Chamber made
         14  clear that documents must be identified by title, date
         15  and author or other particulars.
         16            As a concession to Defence counsel, and as an
         17  exception to this general rule, which I just
         18  articulated, the Appeals Chamber also stated that if
         19  a particular document cannot be identified by title,
         20  date and author or other particulars, the party may
         21  omit such details so long as it explains why that is
         22  the case and nevertheless identifies the "specific
         23  documents in question in some appropriate manner".
         24            I believe here it is important to note that
         25  the Trial Chamber may allow the omission, in our view,
          1  of course, of the details of title, date and author
          2  only if (1) it is satisfied that the requesting party
          3  is acting in good faith, and "has no means of providing
          4  those particulars".
          5            Moreover, even if the Trial Chamber
          6  determines that the details of title, date and author
          7  for a particular document may be omitted from the
          8  request, the requesting party still must identify
          9  specific documents, and not broad categories of
         10  documents as the Prosecution has done here.
         11            We believe effectively, enough detail must be
         12  provided so that the document or documents sought can
         13  be identified, i.e., separated from all the other
         14  documents that may be in the government archives.
         15            Any request that asks for categories of "any
         16  and all" documents - as do most of the Prosecutor's
         17  request in the order issued on January 30th, on its
         18  face, do not meet these requirements set forth by the
         19  Appellate Chamber.

         21  it is inherently incredible that almost all of the
         22  Prosecutor's categorical requests would fall within

         24  the Appellate Chamber.
