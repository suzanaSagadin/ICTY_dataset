 8    Article 18 of the Statute of the Tribunal and rule 77 of the Rules of
 9    Procedure and Evidence of the Tribunal, charges Marijan Krizic with
10    contempt of the Tribunal, punishable under the Tribunal's inherent power,
11    Rule 77(A), Rule 77(A)(ii), Rule 77(A)(iv) of the Rules of Procedure and
12    Evidence of the Tribunal.
13            The accused Marijan Krizic.  At all times relevant to this
14    indictment, Krizic was editor-in-chief of Hrvatsko Slovo.
15            Charges.  Count 1, contempt of the Tribunal.
16            On 16 March 1998, a protected witness testified before the
17    Tribunal in the case IT-95-14-A, the Prosecutor versus Tihomir Blaskic.
18    This testimony was conducted in closed session pursuant to oral orders of
19    the Trial Chamber and a written decision of the Trial Chamber that the
20    witness would testify in closed session and using a pseudonym.
21            On 26 November 2004, the Zagreb-based weekly newspaper Hrvatsko
22    Slovo, published by HKZ-Hrvatsko Slovo d.o.o., printed excerpts of the
23    testimony of the witness and revealed his identity.  Hrvatsko Slovo
24    indicated that the issue of 26 November 2004 was the first of ten issues
25    which would feature such excerpts.
 1            This publication contravened three existing Court orders:
 2            a)  The decision of the Trial Chamber I on the requests of the
 3    Prosecutor of 12 and 14 May 1997 in respect of the protection of
 4    witnesses, dated 6 June 1997;
 5            b)  The oral order for closed session issued by the Court on the
 6    first day of the protected witness's testimony before the ICTY.
 7    References to that order are recorded daily on the transcripts of his
 8    four-day testimony;
 9            And c)  The order of the Trial Chamber's Order for the immediate
10    cessation of protective measures for witnesses dated 1 December 2000.
11            From 26 November to 17 December 2004, the publisher of Hrvatsko
12    Slovo was Stjepan Seselj, and the editor-in-chief was Krizic.
13            The 26 November 2004 issue of Hrvatsko Slovo acknowledges that
14    published extracts were taken from transcripts of testimony delivered in
15    non-public proceedings before the Tribunal.  The published extracts
16    include an oral order that the testimony be held in closed session.
17            On 1 December 2004, the Prosecutor filed before the Duty Judge an
18    Urgent Motion for an Order for the Immediate Cessation of Violations of
19    Protective Measures.  In her Motion, the Prosecutor requested the Duty
20    Judge to issue an Order against HKZ-Hrvatsko Slovo d.o.o., Seselj, and
21    Domagoj Margetic (erroneously believing that he was still the
22    editor-in-chief) to cease publication and to desist from further
23    publication of statements or testimonies of the witness concerned, or of
24    any protected witness.
25            On 2 December 2004, the Duty Judge granted the Prosecutor's motion
 1    and ordered HKZ-Hrvatsko Slovo d.o.o. and all its employees, Seselj and
 2    Margetic, to cease publication in Hrvatsko Slovo and in any other
 3    publications, and to desist from the further publication of statements or
 4    testimonies of the witness concerned, or of any protected witnesses.  The
 5    Republic of Croatia was directed to serve the Cease and Desist Order on
 6    HKZ-Hrvatsko Slovo d.o.o., Seselj and Margetic.
 7            On 3 December 2004, Hrvatsko Slovo again published excerpts of the
 8    closed-session testimony of the protected witness.
 9            On 3 December 2004, upon receipt of the Cease and Desist Order,
10    Stjepan Seselj dispatched a letter to the Ministry of Justice, Department
11    of Cooperation with International Criminal Courts, indicating that
12    Hrvatsko Slovo would comply with the Cease and Desist Order.
13            In its 10 December 2004 edition, Hrvatsko Slovo published the
14    Cease and Desist Order, side by side with an article identifying the
15    protected witness again in direct violation of the court order.
16            In its 17 December 2004 edition, Hrvatsko Slovo again published an
17    article identifying the protected witness again in direct violation of the
18    court order.
19            Krizic knowingly and wilfully interfered with the administration
20    of justice by disclosing:
21            a) the identity of a protected witness;
22            b) the extracts of closed-session testimony; and
23            c) the fact that the protected witness had testified in non-public
24    proceedings before the Tribunal.
