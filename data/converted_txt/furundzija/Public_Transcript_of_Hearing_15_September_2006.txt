21            JUDGE VAZ: [Interpretation] Mr. Cooper, of course you are
22    perfectly entitled to file motions, including based on Rule 115.  You've
23    just mentioned it.  But at this stage of the proceedings, we don't have
24    any authorisation to give you any time limits to set.  Once you have these
25    documents, you will be able to file any motion you like, and the Chamber
 1    will then rule on these motions.
 2            Are there any other issues the parties would like to raise?
 3            MR. COOPER:  Your Honour, just as I'm on my feet.  Following on
 4    from Your Honour's observation.  This is a very practical question for the
 5    appellant in this case.  We have had, in this case, I'm afraid, a slow
 6    drip feed of additional information, which causes -- which raises
 7    challenges for the appellant's representatives.
 8            Each new piece of information needs to be considered in the
 9    context of the entire appeal, and we then have to consider an appropriate
10    method of getting that material to the Appeals Chamber in the most
11    appropriate and the briefest form.  And the -- as I described it in, at
12    one of my earlier pleadings, we haphazard approach to disclosure in this
13    case has caused a lot of difficulty first for the appellant legal team,
14    and I regret also for the Appeals Chamber who has had to deal with a
15    multiplicity of pleadings on matters that might have been sorted out at a
16    much earlier stage in these proceedings.
17            So I'm grateful for Your Honour's observation.  We of course will
18    present any further information in the most convenient format.  But our
19    practical issue is this.  Where we have incoming information which ought
20    to be included in our submissions, is the Defence required to present a
21    fresh pleading with each new piece of information, further multiplying the
22    amount of paper going backwards and forwards?  Or is it more sensible to
23    produce a more consolidated pleading, firstly, in relation to the material
24    which might supplement our brief, namely, the material which is newly
25    disclosed from the trial record, and secondly, in relation to new material
 1    which is coming to us each day which has never been in the trial record,
 2    but which is relevant to the appeal and could be the basis for a Rule 115
 3    motion.
 4            So the reason that I raised the question of the liberty to apply
 5    is that for exactly the reason that the Appeals Chamber does not want to
 6    rule prematurely on our motion to supplement our brief.  Similarly, we
 7    don't want to, so to speak, pepper the Appeals Chamber with a series of
 8    motions which may be unnecessary, each one containing a small amount of
 9    additional information which has just come to us, whether from the
10    Prosecution, whether from another source, or whether pursuant to Your
11    Honour's judgement.
12            So it seemed to us that it would be helpful to set some sort of
13    framework and some sort of timetable for additional submissions to be made
14    in the light of the new material that Your Honour has ordered, and which
15    has already emerged from the Blaskic review proceedings and which may
16    emerge in the future.  And it seemed to us that that would be a sensible
17    course, because it would assist the Appeals Chamber to be able to rule
18    definitively on the questions that are raised by the Defence.
19            Now, whether that is a matter capable of resolution today, I
20    suspect it wouldn't be.  But it certainly is a matter with which -- it's
21    certainly a matter which has been raised through the normal channels, on a
22    number of occasions.  And the Defence will -- or the appellant, through
23    his legal team, will very much wish to continue the discussion through the
24    normal channels as to how best we can assist the Appeals Chamber.
25            That's the point that I wanted to raise.  I'm obliged.
