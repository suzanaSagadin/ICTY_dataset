 8     Ranko Cesic.

11     you understand.

16             MR. HARMON:  Good afternoon, Mr. President, Your Honours, 
17     Counsel. My name is Mark Harmon.  To my right is Mr. Vladimir 
18     Tochilovsky, Mr. Thomas Hannis, and the case manager, Susan Grogan.

20             And for the Defence? 
21             MR. BAKRAC: [Interpretation] Good afternoon, Your Honour.  Good 
22     afternoon to everyone in the courtroom.  Representing Ranko Cesic am I,  
23     Mihajlo Bakrac, attorney-at-law from Belgrade. 
24             JUDGE ORIE:  Thank you, Mr. Bakrac.
25             We are sitting today to deliver the sentence judgement of Ranko 
 1     Cesic.  What now follows is only a summary of the written judgement and 
 2     forms no part of it.  The written judgement will be made available to the 
 3     parties and to the public at the end of this hearing.
 4             We will now briefly set out the context and the facts of the 
 5     case, as well as the factors the Trial Chamber considered in imposing the 
 6     sentence.
 7             Ranko Cesic was born on the 5th of September, 1964 in Bosnia and 
 8     Herzegovina.  He lived in the Brcko municipality before and during the 
 9     war and moved to Belgrade towards the end of 1996.
10             Ranko Cesic was first indicted together with Goran Jelisic in a 
11     joint indictment confirmed by Judge Vohrah on the 21st of July, 1995.  
12     Goran Jelisic was tried and convicted on the 14th of December, 1999.  An 
13     amended indictment, pertaining only to Ranko Cesic, was filed on the 26th 
14     of November, 2002.
15             The indictment is comprised of 12 counts.  Six counts charge 
16     Ranko Cesic with crimes against humanity.  Five of these charge murder 
17     and one charges rape.  Six other counts, referring to the same events, 
18     charge Cesic with violations of the laws or customs of war.  Five of 
19     these charge murder and one charges humiliating and degrading treatment. 
20             Ranko Cesic was arrested in Belgrade by the authorities of the 
21     Federal Republic of Yugoslavia on the 25th of May, 2002 and was 
22     transferred to the United Nations Detention Unit at The Hague on the 17th 
23     of June, 2002. 
24             At his Initial Appearance three days later, Ranko Cesic pleaded 
25     not guilty to all counts of the indictment.  However, on the 7th of 
 1     October, 2003, before the beginning of the trial, Cesic and the 
 2     Prosecutor entered into a plea agreement.  Ranko Cesic admitted his guilt 
 3     on all 12 counts and agreed to testify in other proceedings before the 
 4     Tribunal.  A written Factual Basis described the crimes and Cesic's 
 5     participation in them and was annexed to the plea agreement.  Cesic 
 6     subsequently pleaded guilty to the 12 counts of the indictment before the 
 7     Trial Chamber on October the 8th, 2003.  The Trial Chamber accepted the 
 8     guilty plea after having ensured that it was made voluntarily, was 
 9     informed and unequivocal, and after verifying that there was a sufficient 
10     factual basis for the crimes and for the accused's participation in them.  
11     A sentencing hearing was then held on the 27th of November, 2003. 
12             The acts for which Ranko Cesic is sentenced today took place in 
13     the Brcko municipality located in the north-east of Bosnia and 
14     Herzegovina, in May 1992.  At this time, Cesic was a member of the  
15     Bosnian-Serb Territorial Defence in Brcko.  On the 15th of May, 1992, he  
16     became a member of the Intervention platoon of the Bosnian Serb Police  
17     Reserve Corps at the Brcko police station.  His exact role and the rank 
18     within this corps is not known. 
19             According to the agreed Factual Basis, Ranko Cesic committed five 
20     criminal acts between the 5th of May and the 14th of May, and one 
21     criminal act sometime between the 14th of May and the 6th of June, 1992.  
22     These acts took place while the Serb forces in Bosnia, from the 30th of 
23     April onwards, engaged in the takeover of the municipality of Brcko.  
24     During this operation, the Serb forces forcibly expelled and transferred 
25     the Muslim and Croat residents of Brcko to collection centres.  These 
 1     collection centres included the Luka detention facility and the Brcko 
 2     Partizan Sports Hall.  Ranko Cesic's criminal acts were committed against 
 3     persons detained at these two collection centres. 
 4             Cesic has been convicted of the murder of Sakib Becirevic and of 
 5     four other men at the Brcko Partizan Sports Hall on or about the 5th of 
 6     May, 1992.  These murders constituted a violation of the laws or customs 
 7     of war and a crime against humanity. 
 8             Cesic has also been convicted of the murder of a detainee at Luka 
 9     camp named "Sejdo," on approximately the 11th of May, 1992.  This murder 
10     constituted a violation of the laws or customs of war and a crime against 
11     humanity. 
12             Thirdly, Cesic has been convicted of the murder of a Muslim 
13     policeman named Mirsad detained at Luka camp, on approximately the 11th 
14     of May, 1992.  The victim, who was detained in a hangar together with the 
15     other detainees, was first ordered by Ranko Cesic to say goodbye and 
16     shake hands with the other detainees.  He was then taken out by Ranko 
17     Cesic and other Serb policemen, who beat him and killed him.  This murder  
18     constituted a violation of the laws or the customs of war and a crime 
19     against humanity. 
20             Ranko Cesic has also been convicted of humiliating and degrading 
21     treatment as a violation of the laws or customs of war and of rape as a 
22     crime against humanity for having forced, at gunpoint, two Muslim 
23     brothers detained at Luka camp to perform fellatio on each other.  This 
24     act was committed around the 11th of May, 1992. 
25             The fifth criminal act Cesic has been convicted of is the murder 
 1     of a Muslim detainee at Luka Camp named Nihad Jasarevic.  This man was  
 2     beaten to death with clubs by Cesic and another Serb policeman.  The  
 3     murder constituted a violation of the laws or customs of war and a crime 
 4     against humanity.
 5             Finally, Ranko Cesic has been convicted of the murder of at least 
 6     two detainees at Luka Camp.  Ranko Cesic admitted that sometime between 
 7     the 14th of May and the 6th of June, 1992 he took four detainees out of 
 8     the Luka Camp's office building, led them to the paved road in front of 
 9     the main hangar building and, with the assistance of two guards, shot and 
10     killed at least two of them.  These murders constitute a violation of the 
11     laws or customs of war and a crime against humanity. 
12             In total, Ranko Cesic has admitted to killing ten individuals, 
13     two of whom died as a result of beatings, and to having forced two 
14     brothers to perform a sexual act on each other.  He committed all these 
15     crimes within a period of 10 to 32 days.
16             The Trial Chamber has considered the purposes of punishment in 
17     light of the mandate of the Tribunal.  In accordance with the  
18     jurisprudence, retribution, deterrence, and rehabilitation have been  
19     considered as relevant purposes of punishment for international crimes.
20             Turning to the sentencing factors, the Trial Chamber first 
21     considered the gravity of the crimes, with reference to the particular 
22     circumstances of the case, as well as to the form and degree of 
23     participation of Ranko Cesic in these crimes. 
24             Cesic is convicted for having personally committed ten murders, 
25     constituting five crimes against humanity and five violations of the laws

 1     or customs of war, and for one sexual assault, constituting a crime  
 2     against humanity (rape) and a violation of the laws or customs of war 
 3     (humiliating and degrading treatment).  These crimes are particularly 
 4     serious in terms of the protected interests violated: The life as well as 
 5     the physical and moral integrity of the victims.  The crime of murder is 
 6     inherently serious and a conviction for multiple murders of course 
 7     further adds to the seriousness of the crime.  In relation to the sexual 
 8     assault, the fact that the victims were brothers, that they were forced 
 9     to act at gunpoint and that they were watched by others, means that this 
10     offence was particularly serious.
11             The Prosecution submitted that, in assessing the gravity of the 
12     crimes, the Trial Chamber should also take into consideration the impact 
13     the crimes had on the victims' relatives and friends.  The Appeals 
14     Chamber has said that such impact may be taken into account when 
15     determining the appropriate sentence.  The Trial Chamber considers that 
16     the impact of the crimes on the victims' relatives and friends is among 
17     the factors that have been already taken into account when evaluating the 
18     inherent gravity of these crimes.  For example, it is among the factors 
19     taken into account in concluding that murder is a particularly serious 
20     offence.
21             The Prosecution invoked as aggravating circumstances the 
22     vulnerability of the victims and their civilian status, the purposefully  
23     cruel conduct of Ranko Cesic, the humiliating character and unusual 
24     depravity of the sexual assault, the fact that Cesic abused his position 
25     of authority and the recurrence of his criminal conduct over a period of 
 1     at least ten days.  The Defence claimed that these were not aggravating 
 2     factors in this case. 
 3             The Trial Chamber takes into account that the victims were all 
 4     detainees placed under the oversight of Bosnian-Serb soldiers and 
 5     policemen, and were thus particularly vulnerable.  Cesic's depravity and 
 6     cruelty, as shown by his beating of some of his victims and his forcing a 
 7     detainee to farewell other detainees before being taken out and executed, 
 8     were also considered aggravating factors. 
 9             As for the incontestable recurrence of Ranko Cesic's criminal 
10     behaviour, the number of crimes committed by him within this short period 
11     of time has already been taken into account in evaluating the gravity of  
12     the crimes, and hence should not also be considered as an aggravating  
13     factor.  The contention that Cesic abused his position of authority and  
14     that this should count in aggravation, is also rejected by the Trial 
15     Chamber.  Ranko Cesic's exact position in the military or police 
16     hierarchy is unclear but he was undoubtedly of low rank.
17             Ranko Cesic's admission of guilt, his substantial cooperation 
18     with the Prosecution, and his expression of remorse are the factors which 
19     the Trial Chamber has taken into consideration in mitigation.  The 
20     Defence raised a number of personal circumstances in mitigation, namely 
21     the fact that Mr. Cesic was brought up by a single parent, that he's 
22     currently married, that his low income would prove that he did not 
23     personally gain from the conflict, or that he was only 27 years old when 
24     he committed the crimes.  The Trial Chamber has not found that these 
25     circumstances merit mitigation of punishment.  The Trial Chamber has also 
 1     rejected the submission that Cesic's guilt is lessened by the fact that 
 2     he merely executed orders when committing the crimes.  Good character was 
 3     also not established. 
 4             A guilty plea helps establish the truth and may aid the process 
 5     of reconciliation in the Brcko municipality.  More particularly, it may 
 6     provide a sense of relief to the surviving victims and the victims' 
 7     relatives and friends.  It also saves the witnesses from the possible 
 8     trauma of reliving the events when testifying in court.  The guilty plea 
 9     was entered some 16 months after Cesic's Initial Appearance but 
10     nevertheless still before the commencement of trial, thereby saving time, 
11     effort, and resources.  For all these reasons, the Trial Chamber finds 
12     that Cesic's guilty plea is an important mitigating factor. 
13             As for Cesic's cooperation with the Prosecutor, the Trial 
14     Chamber, in the absence of any information to the contrary, relies on the 
15     Prosecutor's declaration that Cesic provided a full and complete 
16     interview to the Prosecutor concerning his knowledge of war crimes and 
17     other violations of international humanitarian law in and around Brcko 
18     during the armed conflict in Bosnia and Herzegovina.  The Trial Chamber 
19     also takes into account Cesic's commitment to testify in other cases if 
20     called by the Prosecutor and concludes that his cooperation with the 
21     Prosecutor is substantial. 
22             Ranko Cesic expressed remorse for his criminal acts at the 
23     sentencing hearing of the 26th of November, 2003.  The Trial Chamber 
24     believes that his expression of remorse was sincere.
25             The Trial Chamber has carefully reviewed the statements and 
 1     documents adduced in evidence by both parties in respect of the Defence's 
 2     submissions regarding Ranko Cesic's good character.  The Trial Chamber 
 3     finds that the evidence submitted by the Prosecution does not 
 4     successfully  rebut the evidence adduced by the Defence in support of its 
 5     claim of good character.  On the other hand, while the evidence presented 
 6     by the Defence, along with that concerning Ranko Cesic's behaviour at the 
 7     United Nations Detention Unit, shows that Ranko Cesic is capable of 
 8     benevolent and good behaviour, the Trial Chamber finds that the evidence 
 9     presented demonstrated at the same time the unpredictabilty of his 
10     behaviour.  On the balance of probabilities, the Trial Chamber cannot 
11     conclude that Ranko Cesic is of a genuine good character, so as to allow 
12     this good character to be taken into consideration as a mitigating 
13     factor. 
14             Finally, the Defence referred to a statement given by Ranko Cesic 
15     to the Prosecution in September 2003 in which he stated that he merely 
16     pulled the trigger but that others took aim.  According to the Defence, 
17     this statement establishes that Ranko Cesic acted pursuant to orders and 
18     that he would have been killed if he had failed to execute these orders. 
19     On the balance of probabilities, the Trial Chamber finds that the 
20     statement does not establish that Ranko Cesic acted under duress or 
21     pursuant to orders. 
22             The Prosecution has recommended a sentence in the range of 13 to 
23     18 years and has agreed not to appeal any sentence imposed within this 
24     recommended range.  The Defence requested a sentence of 13 years and 
25     agreed not to appeal the sentence unless it is above the range 
 1     recommended by the Prosecution.  The Trial Chamber is not bound by any 
 2     agreement between the parties on the sentence.
 3             Mr. Cesic, would you please rise.
 4                           [The accused stand up]
 5             JUDGE ORIE:  The Trial Chamber has carefully reviewed the Factual 
 6     Basis and the evidence presented by the parties.  The sentence you 
 7     receive should be proportional to the gravity of the crimes you 
 8     committed.  In addition to the gravity of the crimes you committed, your 
 9     victims were all detainees placed under the oversight of you and other 
10     Bosnian-Serb soldiers or policemen:  You knew that your victims were 
11     particularly vulnerable.  These considerations constitute, in the 
12     Chamber's view, aggravating circumstances.  The Trial Chamber finds that 
13     your guilty plea prior to the commencement of the trial, your substantial 
14     cooperation with the Prosecutor and your sincere expression of remorse 
15     are mitigating circumstances.  Finally, in determining the sentence the 
16     Trial Chamber has referred to the general sentencing practice of the 
17     courts of the former Yugoslavia.
18             The Trial Chamber hereby sentences you, Ranko Cesic, to a term of 
19     18 years of imprisonment.  You are entitled to 657 days credit for the 
20     time you served up to and including the day of this Judgement.
21             Please be seated. 
22                           [The accused sits down]
23             JUDGE ORIE:  This concludes this session in which the Chamber 
24     delivered the sentencing judgement of Ranko Cesic. 
25                           --- Whereupon the hearing adjourned at 3.26 p.m.
