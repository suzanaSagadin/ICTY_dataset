22             Then, finally, I'd like to deliver a further guidance in relation

24             There has been an out-of-court meeting last week between the
25     parties and Chamber's staff discussing in which form the per diem
 1     documents should be submitted.  Also last week, the Chamber informally
 2     received a memo from the Prosecution on its understanding of how bar
 3     table documents should be submitted to the Chamber.  And in response to
 4     that, I would like to give the following guidance on behalf of the
 5     Chamber:  First, if the parties want to tender documents, they should
 6     preferably do so during examinations of witnesses who may give meaningful
 7     context to the documents.
 8             Second, if there is no witness who could contextualise the
 9     document, the parties can bar table that document by creating a
10     spreadsheet.  That could be one document or more related documents.  That
11     spreadsheet should, first of all, describe the document.  It should
12     indicate relevance and should point to specific portions if the documents
13     are lengthy documents.  And they should leave one free column for the
14     other party for objections or comments.  If it would, therefore, be a
15     Prosecution motion -- or a Prosecution spreadsheet, then they should
16     leave one column open for the Defence.  Or for the two Defence teams, I
17     would say two columns.
18             Now, after that chart is completed, which means that it includes
19     any comments or any objections by the Defence if they wish to do so, then
20     that chart should be filed.  And the Chamber will then subsequently
21     decide on admission of those bar table documents.
22             Third, if the parties have a number of similar documents, they
23     are not required to ask a witness the same questions for each document
24     but could do so for two or three documents as examples and then bar table
25     the rest.  In such a scenario, the relevance and the contextualisation is
 1     sufficiently clear and no spreadsheet would need to be filed.
 2             Having given this guidance, I do understand that the parties have
 3     agreed to file a spreadsheet for the per diem lists.  So in this
 4     instance, we will await the filing of the spreadsheet.
 5             And this concludes the Chamber's guidance.
