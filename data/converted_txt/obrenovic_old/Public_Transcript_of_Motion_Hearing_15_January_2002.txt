12            JUDGE SCHOMBURG:  Please be seated.  We want to proceed.  After
13    intensive deliberations, the Trial Chamber has decided.  The Trial Chamber
14    grants the motion of the Office of the Prosecutor and orders as follows:
15    The indictments Prosecutor versus Obrenovic, Prosecutor versus Blagojevic,
16    and Prosecutor versus Jokic, be jointly charged and tried; second, the
17    Registry designate one unified case number to the joint case forthwith;
18    the Prosecution file a joint indictment immediately, and no later than
19    seven days from the date of this decision.
20            This Trial Chamber was seized of a motion filed on 11 September
21    2001 by the Office of the Prosecutor for an order that the accused
22    Obrenovic, Blagojevic, and Jokic, who were currently charged in separate
23    indictments, be jointly tried in one unified indictment.
24            Having looked into the merits of this motion and taking care of
25    the arguments provided by the Defence counsel, the Chamber came to the
 1    following conclusion:  In determining this motion, the Trial Chamber
 2    relies upon the allegations as contained in the Prosecution's draft
 3    proposed amended indictment of 14 December 2001.  One of the core issues,
 4    the common core issues, is the alleged murder of the Muslim male
 5    population of the Srebrenica enclave, and alleged crimes occurred
 6    basically from the 12th of July to the 16th of July, and resulting in the
 7    murder of about 7.500 men and boys systematically killed in this area.
 8            The first issue for our consideration was whether the crimes
 9    alleged against each accused were committed in the course of the same
10    transaction.  By reliance upon allegations pleaded in the draft proposed
11    amended indictment, it may be considered that there are a number of
12    factors that can lead the Trial Chamber to find that there was a common
13    scheme, strategy, or plan, and the accused are charged with crimes
14    committed during the course of it.
15            The offences with which the accused are charged all occurred over
16    the same narrow period of time, as mentioned, from 11, 12 July to 17 July
17    1995.  The accused were all military commanders of the RS and the victims
18    of the alleged offences were all Bosnian Muslims from the Srebrenica
19    area.
20            The Prosecution pleads its case on the basis that there was a
21    joint criminal enterprise with the same common purpose and design, namely,
22    the forcible transfer to Kladanj of Bosnian Muslim women and children from
23    the Srebrenica enclave, and the capture, detention, and summary execution
24    of thousands of Muslim men and boys from the Srebrenica enclave, and the
25    accused all participated in that common enterprise, of course, and, there
 1    was no doubt, with different contributions.
 2            As the Trial Chamber is satisfied that it is legally possible to
 3    join the accused under Rule 48, it must exercise its discretion whether or
 4    not to join the crimes.  From the allegations contained in the draft
 5    proposed amended indictment, factors favourable to joinder are, of
 6    course:  There are a large number of overlapping issues in all three
 7    cases, and if the three separate trials would be held, this would entail
 8    civilian and expert witnesses travelling to The Hague on more than one
 9    occasion, and it was outlined very clearly this morning that it would mean
10    to hear three times the same 30 to 40 victims in all the three cases, and
11    this is, of course, not in the spirit of economic use of manpower in this
12    Tribunal.  The Court time would also be taken up in going over the same
13    issues in these different trials.  And we, on a prima facie basis, we
14    didn't have any reasonable doubts that it would indeed mean three times a
