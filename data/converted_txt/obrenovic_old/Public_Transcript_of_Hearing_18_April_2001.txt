 1                          Wednesday, 18th April 2001
 2                          [Open session]
 3                          [Initial Appearance]
 4                          [The accused entered court]
 5                          --- Upon commencing at 3.01 p.m.
 6            JUDGE HUNT:  Call the case, please.
 7            THE REGISTRAR:  Yes, Your Honour.  This is the case number
 8    IT-01-43-I, the Prosecutor versus Dragan Obrenovic.
 9            JUDGE HUNT:  Appearances.  For the Prosecution?  Mr. McCloskey.
10            MR. McCLOSKEY:  Yes, Mr. President.  Good afternoon.  Good
11    afternoon counsel, Colonel Obrenovic.
12            My name is Peter McCloskey.  Appearing with me today is Dean
13    Manning, the lead investigator; Mark Vlasic, a legal assistant; and Janet
14    Stewart, the case manager.  Thank you.
15            JUDGE HUNT:  Thank you.  For the accused?
16            MR. NIKOLIC: [Interpretation] Good afternoon, Mr. President.  My
17    name is Zarko Nikolic.  I'm an attorney at law firm Yugoslavia, and the
18    registrar has appointed me Defence counsel for Mr. Obrenovic at his
19    initial appearance today.
20            JUDGE HUNT:  And is it only for the initial appearance?
21            MR. NIKOLIC: [Interpretation] Yes, sir.
22            JUDGE HUNT:  Mr. Obrenovic, can you hear the proceedings in a
23    language which you understand?
24            THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] Good afternoon, Your Honour.  Yes, I
25    do hear the proceedings in a language I understand.
 1            JUDGE HUNT:  Can you give us your full name?
 2            THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] I am Dragan Obrenovic.
 3            JUDGE HUNT:  And what is the date upon which you believe you were
 4    born?
 5            THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] The 12th of April, 1963.
 6            JUDGE HUNT:  And what is the address at which you resided before
 7    you were arrested?
 8            THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] Street of Sveti Sava, 5C, Zvornik.
 9            JUDGE HUNT:  Thank you, sir.  You may sit down for a moment.
10            Mr. Nikolic, it's the common practice in the Tribunal these days
11    for the accused to waive his right to have the indictment read out in full
12    before entering a plea.  Have you been able to discuss this with your
13    client?
14            MR. NIKOLIC: [Interpretation] Yes, Your Honour.  I have discussed
15    this with Mr. Obrenovic, and he stated the following, that he received the
16    indictment, he understood it, and there is no need for it to be read out
17    in its entirety today.  He agrees that only the counts that he has been
18    charged with be read to him today so that he could enter his plea.
19            JUDGE HUNT:  Does he understand that he has to plead within 30
20    days, but he may enter a plea today if he wishes to do so?
21            MR. NIKOLIC: [Interpretation] Yes, Your Honour.  Mr. Obrenovic is
22    prepared to enter his plea with regard to the counts in the indictment
23    today.
24            JUDGE HUNT:  Thank you.
25            Well, Mr. Obrenovic, would you stand up, please, sir.  In answer
 1    to Count 1 in which you are charged with complicity in genocide, do you
 2    plead guilty or not guilty?
 3            THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] Your Honour, not guilty.
 4            JUDGE HUNT:  In Count 2, you are charged with extermination as a
 5    crime against humanity.  Do you plead guilty or not guilty?
 6            THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] Your Honour, not guilty.
 7            JUDGE HUNT:  In Count 3, you are charged with murder as a crime
 8    against humanity.  Do you plead guilty or not guilty?
 9            THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] Your Honour, I'm not guilty.
10            JUDGE HUNT:  In Count 4, you are charged with murder as a
11    violation of the laws or customs of war.  Do you plead guilty or not
12    guilty?
13            THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] Your Honour, not guilty.
14            JUDGE HUNT:  And for the fifth count, you are charged with
15    persecution on political, racial, and religious grounds as a crime against
16    humanity.  Do you plead guilty or not guilty?
17            THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] Your Honour, not guilty.
18            JUDGE HUNT:  Sit down, please.
19            Now, Mr. McCloskey, are you able to tell me in what form the
20    supporting material was at the time that it was presented to the
21    confirming judge?
22            MR. McCLOSKEY:  I don't know exactly what you mean by "form."
23            JUDGE HUNT:  Was it in a redacted form or was it unredacted?
24            MR. McCLOSKEY:  Unredacted, fully.
25            JUDGE HUNT:  Is there going to be any problem in relation to
 1    protective measures before you comply with the rather stringent
 2    requirements of Rule 66(A)(1)?
 3            MR. McCLOSKEY:  No, Your Honour.  We -- in a similar trial, we had
 4    no problems, though I think that was largely because of the understanding
 5    of Defence counsel and the Prosecution that worked well together, and we
 6    don't need any -- if we can get that same understanding, we shouldn't need
 7    any special protective measures.
 8            JUDGE HUNT:  Then are you in a position to serve that material
 9    within the time limited?
10            MR. McCLOSKEY:  Yes, Mr. President.  I had a chance to speak with
11    Mr. Nikolic today, as well as immediately before coming into court, and he
12    would prefer not to receive anything until Colonel Obrenovic has chosen a
13    lawyer, in which case we will be able to provide that material which we
14    have ready to provide him when we know who is representing the accused.
15            JUDGE HUNT:  That's a sensible solution.  You will, of course,
16    keep an eye on the clock, as it were.  Now, may I make a suggestion so
17    that you will be ready for it when it comes up later?  Obviously a lot of
18    the evidence in this case will be the same as it was against General
19    Krstic.
20            MR. McCLOSKEY:  That's correct.
21            JUDGE HUNT:  The provisions of Rule 92 bis (D), transcripts of
22    proceedings -- transcript of evidence in other proceedings may be tendered
23    provided it does not go to an act or the conduct of the accused.  No doubt
24    that will be taken advantage of by the Prosecution, and perhaps also by
25    the accused.  When it's going to be tendered, it would be very helpful for
 1    the Trial Chamber to have a short summary of what the Prosecution asserts
 2    is established by that evidence so -- and with references to the
 3    transcript itself.  It saves an awful lot of reading, because when you do
 4    get hundreds of pages of transcript from some other trial, it's not
 5    exactly an easy task to follow, if I may say so, with respect to counsel
 6    who conduct those examinations, just what it is which is sought to be
 7    proved.  So it would help the Trial Chamber very much if we have a very
 8    short statement of what the Prosecution says that transcript will prove,
 9    with a reference to the page and the lines in that transcript.  It will,
10    of course, take a little time, but it will save everybody an enormous
11    amount of time at the trial.
12            MR. McCLOSKEY:  Yes.  We are in a position -- as you might
13    imagine, we have summaries for all -- most all the witnesses that will be
14    used in this case, and it's just a matter of adapting them to the
15    transcripts and the lines, so that shouldn't be a problem, especially when
16    we learn what kind of length and what kind of detail you prefer.
17            JUDGE HUNT:  Well, a little bit more than we get in those brief
18    summaries in the pre-trial brief.  It's just that having done this now in
19    two trials where we've had long transcripts, either you spend all night
20    reading it to keep up or you have somebody else do that exercise for you,
21    and it seems to me very much preferable that we get somebody else to do it
22    for us.
23            MR. McCLOSKEY:  We are in a position to be able to do that.
24            JUDGE HUNT:  Thank you very much.  Now, is there any other matter
25    that you wish to raise at this stage?
 1            MR. McCLOSKEY:  No, Mr. President.
 2            JUDGE HUNT:  How about you, Mr. Nikolic?  Do you want to raise any
 3    other matter at this stage?
 4            MR. NIKOLIC: [Interpretation] Your Honour, I just wish to notify
 5    you of the following, that I have suggested to Mr. Obrenovic that he
 6    engage a lawyer as soon as possible to act as his Defence counsel, due to
 7    the procedure that will follow.  Thank you.
 8            JUDGE HUNT:  That is very important, if I may emphasise that for
 9    the sake of your client, that he does have counsel retained - that's
10    permanent counsel - as soon as possible, because it is only in that way
11    that the preparation for the trial can get underway.  Assuming that the
12    United Nations produces these ad litem Judges later this year, there is
13    every chance that this case will get a hearing sometime early next year,
14    so there should be no delays at any stage.
15            With that, I will adjourn.
16                          --- Whereupon the Initial Appearance adjourned at
17                          3.13 p.m.
