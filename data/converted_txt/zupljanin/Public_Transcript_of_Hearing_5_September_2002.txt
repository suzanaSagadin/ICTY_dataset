12       Q.   Mr. Filipovic, I want to pick up the story where you left off
13    yesterday.  You told us how you were beaten outside the police station and
14    that you were then taken inside the police station.  What happened to you
15    when you were inside the police station?
16       A.   They didn't take me inside because if they had taken me inside, I
17    would have gone in on any own two feet.  They carried me inside in an
18    unconscious condition.  I came to in the office of the interrogator
19    Todo Gajic.  From then on, that is, when -- from when I came -- regained
20    consciousness until half past 5.00 on that same day, as far as I was able
21    to count and recollect, I lost consciousness seven to eight times.
22            They beat me, but the interrogator Todo Gajic himself never beat
23    me because he knew me personally.  And in his presence I was not beaten.
24    But he first asked me, "Muhis, where is Omer?"  And I said, "Omer has gone
25    to Krasulje together with Vinko to secure Duca's body being taken out."
 1    And he said to me, "I told you not to engage in politics.  You're a got
 2    professional.  It would have been better for you not to get involved in
 3    politics."  That is the only thing that the interrogator Todo Gajic would
 4    say to me.  Then he would get up and walk out and then people who I didn't
 5    know who were not from Kljuc came in wearing the uniforms of the Serbian
 6    police, camouflage blue uniforms.  They would beat me up.  And when they
 7    left, then Todo Gajic would come back in.
 8            I think it -- on the third occasion they ordered me to strip, and
 9    all I had on me was my slip.  They took everything else off.  And then
10    they beat me on my naked body with truncheons, their boots.  I think it
11    was the fifth time.  One of them - I don't know his name - but I know that
12    he used to drive a van in Sumarstvo in Kljuc.  His nickname was Lizija.
13    And he would put a knife to my neck.
14            And as I was lying in the middle of the office helpless, he would
15    roll a grenade on the floor.  And if it had -- as if it had remote
16    control, it rolled right up to me.  And then he said, "Look at the
17    Ustasha.  He even has a hand grenade," whereas I only had my underwear on.
18      I managed to pluck up so much strength to say, "I never saw any such
19    bombs like that one," because when I served in the military, I never saw
20    such a hand grenade.  This was of a newer brand.
21            Then he put the grenade in my mouth and said, "Shall I activate
22    it?"  And I begged God that he did that, because if one Muhamed got
23    killed, five Chetniks would get killed as well.  So I prayed to God that
24    he activate the bomb regardless; however, I knew he wouldn't do that,
25    because the Serbian soldiers are great cowards.  They are afraid for their
 1    own lives.  This was proven at the very end of the war when we formed our
 2    own real army.  And sometime around 5.00 p.m. a man walked into the
 3    office.  I can't recollect his name.  He was a police commander.  I'll
 4    remember his name later.  And he attacked them when he saw the condition I
 5    was in.
 6       Q.   Can you pause there for a moment, please.  First of all, when the
 7    interrogator, Gajic, came back in, was he questioning you each time about
 8    your brother?
 9       A.   Only that one time, the first time.  And then when he came in,
10    sometimes it was Gajic; on one occasion it was Zoran Vracar, a butcher.
11    He was also in the reserve force.  And a couple of other men I knew would
12    come in, and they always asked the same question:  "Muhamed, what was all
13    this for?  You're now against us.  Wasn't it fine when we were together?"
14    And I said, "I don't know who is rebelling.  We were always in favour of
15    co-existence with you."
16            However, I can state here that not a single inhabitant of Kljuc
17    hit me except for this one who put a grenade and a knife to my body, but
18    even he is not really from Kljuc.  He's from Donji Ribnik.  And when the
19    police commander came, he yelled at them and swore at them and said all
20    kinds of things like "Are you beasts or policemen?  How are you treating
21    the man?"  So I don't know whether that was what he really thought or
22    whether he was trying to impress me, but he said, "Take the man to the
23    cell."  And I was then taken about 5.00 p.m. to the cell.  And when I saw
24    all the people inside, it became clear to me that it had nothing to do
25    with what they called a rebellion.

11       Q.   All right.
12       A.   And my body was still warm.  It was only on the third day that I

25    were you kept in Mali Logor?
 1       A.   First of all, when they took us, there was another round of
 2    beating.  There were steps for us to climb up to board the truck.  And our
 3    hands were tied so tightly behind our backs that for four months I

 7            And as I fell, all of them kicked me, and a policeman,

10    And then they picked me up like a bag of cement, by the shoulders and the
11    legs, and threw me onto the truck.
12            When they drove us from Kljuc to Banja Luka, the truck stopped for
13    the first time.  I think it was around 7.00 p.m. when the truck stopped

15    came out and said, "You, leader, come here."  And then somebody says,
16     "There are no leaders," he said.  And he said, "Yes, there is.  Filipovic

18            And as I approached, the Serbs are cowards.  Though we were all
19    tied -- tied up, he didn't dare climb up onto the truck.  But from the
20    road he hit me twice with his fist in the chin and I fainted.  He yelled
21    that I should get up again, and I didn't want to, so I thought to myself,
22    He wouldn't dare climb up.  So this went for seven or eight times until we
23    reached the Mali Logor barracks.
24            In the meantime, Mrsic Enes managed somehow to untie his hands,
25    and he untied the hands of all of us, and we all tied our hands in front
 1    because after all it's easier to defend yourself when you have your hands

10    from the beatings.  And I was choking.  But all the beatings were focussed
11    on Mehmed Begic in Mali Logor.  He had a good leather jacket.  But from
12    the blows, the jacket was torn to pieces.
13            In the meantime, maybe after an hour of beating, Zoran came up. We

16    Yugoslavia in the reserve force, and he retired as a policeman.  He
17    pretended in front of us, and he cursed God and he said, "What are you
18    doing?  These are my people from Kljuc.  They're fine people."  My
19    colleague, Medo Fadil he said, "A colleague, this neighbour of ours, will
20    save us."  And I said, "Excuse me.  Fuck you.  No one is going to save
21    us."
22            And then Brkin to impress us yelled at them for a while, and they
