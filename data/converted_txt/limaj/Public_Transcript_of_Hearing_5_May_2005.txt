 1             JUDGE EL MAHDI: [Interpretation] Thank you.  Please sit down.
 2             Before we deliver the judgement, the Chamber would like to draw
 3     the Registry's attention to the fact that it's necessary to take all the
 4     necessary steps to ensure that the protective measures granted by the
 5     Chamber are in place.  In addition, the Chamber would like to point out
 6     to everyone present that it is prohibited to reveal the identity of
 7     protected persons or to reveal any other information that might lead to
 8     their identity being revealed.
 9             Having said that, I will now deliver the judgement in the case,
10     the Prosecutor versus Beqa Beqaj.
11             The Trial Chamber is sitting today to deliver orally a short
12     version of its judgement in the case of Beqa Beqaj.  The purpose of the
13     oral decision of today is to inform the parties of the outcome of the
14     Chamber's deliberations in this case.  The written judgement will be
15     issued as soon as practicable, and it is the written version that will
16     contain the full reasoned arguments of the Chamber which will be the
17     authoritative text.  Therefore, this oral delivery is just a summary with
18     the exception of the disposition, which is authoritative and does not
19     form a part of the written judgement.
20             I will now briefly set out the context and the facts of the case.
21     I will first start by mentioning the procedural background.
22             Beqa Beqaj, the accused, was born on the 10th of November, 1952
23     in Petrove, Kosovo.  He is married and has six children.  He used to work
24     in Slovenia, in the building trade.  On the 19th and 21st of October
25     2004, Beqaj was interviewed by investigators of the Office of the
 1     Prosecution on the suspicion of witness interference.  On the 21st
 2     October 2004, the Prosecution filed an indictment against Beqa Beqaj
 3     which was confirmed a week later.  The indictment alleges that the
 4     accused Beqaj interfered with a witness, whose pseudonym is B1, and a
 5     potential witness, whose pseudonym is B2, in the Limaj trial, on six
 6     occasions between June and October 2004, and that in so doing, he
 7     knowingly and wilfully interfered with the administration of justice in
 8     the case, the Prosecutor versus Fatmir Limaj, Haradin Bala and Isak
 9     Musliu; by, one, threatening; two, intimidating; three, offering a bribe
10     to; or, four, otherwise interfering with witnesses or potential
11     witnesses.
12             The Prosecution emphasised in its opening statement that this
13     case is primarily a case of witness interference and bribery, as opposed
14     to threats or intimidation.
15             On the 4th of November 2004, the accused, who had been detained
16     in Pristina at the request of the Prosecution, and upon an order issued
17     by UNMIK since the 19th October 2004, the accused was transferred to the
18     custody of the United Nations Detention Unit at The Hague.  On the 8th of
19     March, 2005, the accused was provisionally released in Kosovo.  On the
20     7th of April 2005, the Chamber scheduled the commencement of the trial
21     for the 25th of April 2005, and ordered the suspension of the provisional
22     release of the accused.  Beqaj returned to the custody of the Tribunal on
23     the 20th of April 2005.  In total, to date, the accused has been detained
24     for about five months.
25             The Prosecution called three witnesses to prove its case, namely
 1     the two alleged victims of the accused, witnesses B1 and B2, and Jonathan
 2     Sutch, an OTP investigator who interviewed the accused on the 19th and
 3     the 21st of October 2004.
 4             The Defence chose not to call any witnesses, and the accused
 5     expressed the wish to make a statement pursuant to Rule 84 bis on the
 6     25th of April 2005, in which he denied the charges in the indictment.
 7     The parties made their opening statements on the 25th of April 2005 and
 8     their closing arguments were presented on the 2nd of May 2005.
 9             The Chamber emphasis that in keeping with the jurisprudence of
10     the Tribunal, it has not looked at the evidence of each witness
11     separately.  It is the accumulation of all of the evidence in the case
12     that determined beyond reasonable doubt its findings.
13             I will now move on to the contempt allegations pursuant to
14     Rule 77.  I will first refer to Rule 77 and I will then provide you with
15     an overview of the facts in the case.
16             As a preliminary matter, the Chamber dealt with the Defence's
17     objections concerning the offence of contempt.  The Defence argued,
18     pursuant to the principle of legality that such an offence is not
19     punishable by the Statute of the Tribunal but is referred to in its Rules
20     of Procedure and Evidence which cannot create criminal offences.  The
21     Tribunal's chambers have consistently held - and this Chamber affirmed it
22     lately in a decision dated the 25th of April 2005 - that the Tribunal has
23     the inherent power to punish conduct which tends to obstruct, prejudice
24     or abuse its administration of justice.  This power is necessary to
25     ensure that the exercise of jurisdiction which is expressly given to it
 1     by its Statute is not obstructed and that its basic judicial functions
 2     are safeguarded.  The existence of that power does not depend upon a
 3     reference being made to in the Rules of Procedure and Evidence of the
 4     Tribunal.
 5             In its written judgement, the Chamber will further elaborate on
 6     the principle of legality which according to the Defence has been
 7     violated.
 8             In accordance with this inherent power and applying the legal
 9     standards which will be detailed in the written judgement, the Chamber
10     will determine whether the accused engaged in conduct which constituted
11     one of the modes of contempt as charged in this case.  The Chamber
12     considered the six incidents alleged by the Prosecution in turn.
13             Let us now move on to the facts.
14             The first incident took place on the 11th of June 2004.  The
15     Prosecution alleges that in June 2004, about six months before the start
16     of the Limaj trial, Beqaj committed the first of his six alleged
17     interferences, which involved Bashkim Beqaj, the accused's son, and
18     Witness B2.  Witness B2 testified that in early summer 2004, while
19     walking in the streets of Shtime, a small village in Kosovo, he was
20     accosted by the son of the accused, Bashkim Beqaj, just outside a
21     restaurant where Bashkim Beqaj had just had dinner.  The son of the
22     accused, accused him of having sent his uncle, Isak Musliu, to prison in
23     The Hague, and tried to bully him.  B2 then went to Beqaj's house and
24     Beqaj apologised for the behaviour of his son, offered him coffee and
25     promised that his son would behave well in future.
 1             The Defence suggested during the cross-examination that the
 2     accused's son was acting under the influence of alcohol and reiterated
 3     during its closing arguments that Beqaj's son and not the accused had
 4     been involved in this incident.
 5             The Chamber finds that the evidence adduced by the Prosecution in
 6     relation to this incident is inconclusive and fails to establish how
 7     Witness B2 was interfered with by the accused Beqaj.  Accordingly, no
 8     finding of contempt is made in relation to the allegations that the
 9     accused interfered with the potential testimony of Witness B2.
10             The second incident took place at the beginning of September,
11     2004.  The Prosecution alleges that in early September 2004, Beqa Beqaj
12     acting on behalf of Isak Musliu, an accused in the Limaj case, conveyed a
13     message to B1 through one of B1's relatives, a message stating that Isak
14     Musliu would give him land if he withdrew his statement.  The relevant
15     evidence adduced in relation to that incident is that in late August or
16     early September 2004, Witness B1's family members, his brother and
17     mother, called him to inform him that a relative from Shtime, hereinafter
18     the relative, came to see them to ask that B1 withdraw his statement and
19     say that Isak Musliu had called on the phone and said that he would give
20     B1 some land for his close relative who had been killed.  B1 explained
21     during his testimony that Isak Musliu had participated in the beatings
22     and in the torture of his close relative and himself, but that he had not
23     killed his close relative.  B1 testified that the relative had not
24     mentioned the name of Beqa Beqaj to his family, nor did he remember
25     whether Isak Musliu had called the relative directly or whether he had
 1     called Beqaj who had then called the relative.
 2             No evidence was adduced to conclusively establish that Beqaj was
 3     involved in this incident.  The Chamber finds no evidence which supports
 4     the Prosecution's allegations that Beqaj interfered with B1's testimony
 5     in early September 2004.
 6             I will now move on to incidents 3 and 4.
 7             On the 27th of September 2004, as far as incident number 3 is
 8     concerned, the Prosecution alleges that on the 27th of September 2004,
 9     Beqa Beqaj visited B1's relative and asked that relative to relay a
10     message that Beqaj was speaking on behalf of Fatmir Limaj and Isak Musliu
11     who wanted B1 to travel back to Kosovo urgently to meet with the Limaj
12     lawyers and that he should withdraw his statement.  The relative wanted
13     to give B1 the number of Beqaj so that they could speak directly.
14             As far as incident number 4 is concerned, the Prosecution alleges
15     that later that day, on the 27th of September 2004, Beqa Beqaj,
16     dissatisfied with B1's refusal to cooperate with his earlier request,
17     communicated a second time that B1 must go at great speed to Kosovo to
18     meet with the lawyers and the family of the accused in the Limaj case as
19     well as Beqaj and withdraw his statement.
20             Witness B1 testified that the relative called him at home one
21     evening and told him that he had had a discussion with Beqaj about B1
22     going back to Kosovo to withdraw his testimony with regard to Limaj and
23     Musliu and meet their lawyers and was giving him Beqaj's phone number so
24     he could talk to him directly.  Witness B1 further testified that the
25     relative told him that, "This would be the best for you and the best for
 1     them, for Isak Musliu and for Fatmir Limaj."
 2             Witness B1 testified that the relative called him perhaps three
 3     times in total.  The third time the relative called B1 he said, "Beqaj is
 4     waiting for your call.  Why aren't you calling him?"  "I gave you Beqaj's
 5     number.  I saw Beqaj in Shtime.  I asked him whether you had called him.
 6     He said no.  And then I decided to call you again and ask you why you
 7     hadn't called Beqa."
 8             The evidence does not conclusively establish that Beqaj had
 9     incited the relative to convince B1 to withdraw his statement and meet
10     Limaj and Musliu's lawyers.
11             Witness B1 testified that the relative and Beqaj had discussed
12     together about it being in the interests of B1 to withdraw his statement
13     and meet Limaj and Musliu's lawyers.  In view of the evidence adduced in
14     relation to incident number 3.  Even when considering the evidence
15     adduced in relation to incident number 2, there is no evidence which
16     shows beyond a reasonable doubt that the relative could not have acted on
17     his own initiative without being incited by Beqaj.  The Chamber is not
18     prevented, however, from using B1's evidence in relation to that incident
19     to strengthen or weaken other allegations.
20             I will now move on to incident number 5, on the 6th of October
21     2004.  The Prosecution alleges that because B1 told the investigators
22     about the communications he was receiving from the accused, a recorded
23     call to capture what was happening with B1 and Beqa Beqaj was set up on
24     the 6th of October 2004.
25             Relevant evidence in support of the Prosecution's allegations is
 1     contained in several portions of the recorded conversation between B1 and
 2     Beqaj.  Beqaj tells Witness B1, "To come and just give us one statement."
 3     "Just come and say that you have nothing to do with Fatmir Limaj and Isak
 4     Musliu."
 5             Beqaj admitted in this conversation that he had spoken to Fatmir
 6     Limaj's brother, Demir Limaj.  Beqaj then asked Witness B1 to come and
 7     meet the lawyers of the accused Limaj and Musliu in Pristina and say --
 8     and he said that nothing would happen to him.  He must "come fix
 9     something up, and give them "some help for the sake of "the entire
10     Albanian people of Kosovo."
11             During the cross-examination, Witness B1 emphasised that he
12     himself had called Beqa and that Beqa didn't call him back and that he
13     never thought that this recorded call would lead to Beqa's arrest.  The
14     Chamber is satisfied that the recording of the conversation between B1
15     and Beqaj gives sufficient indication that on the 6th of October 2004,
16     Beqaj interfered with a potential witness before this Tribunal.  The fact
17     that the witness called Beqaj and not the contrary could have had an
18     impact on proving whether the accused wilfully interfered with B1.
19     However, in this instance the words used by the accused in the
20     conversation do not leave any doubt as to his intention to influence the
21     testimony of Witness B1.  When considering the evidence in this case as a
22     whole, the Chamber is satisfied beyond reasonable doubt that the accused
23     knowingly and wilfully interfered with a potential witness before the
24     international Tribunal.
25             I shall now move on to incident number 6, that took place on the
 1     13th of October 2004.  According to the Prosecution, the most significant
 2     of the telephone calls is the one that was -- the intercepted telephone
 3     call between Beqa Beqaj and B1 on 13th of October 2004.  The Prosecution
 4     explained that in this call, Beqa Beqaj is again trying to interfere with
 5     B1's truthful testimony.  Beqaj asks B1 again to come back to Kosovo
 6     urgently to meet with the lawyers and Demir Limaj, Fatmir Limaj's
 7     brother, to make a statement that repudiates the earlier statement made
 8     in the Limaj case.  According to the Prosecution, the most important part
 9     of this recording is where Beqaj says, "We are not pressuring you as to
10     Bala," Bala being one of the three Limaj accused.
11             To support this allegation, the Prosecution submits that the
12     accused's phone line was tapped and that all the conversations he had on
13     the 13th of October 2004 with Isak Musliu; then with B1; then with an
14     unknown person; and lastly, with Dull Bajrami were intercepted, as was
15     the case with his conversation he had with Demir Limaj on 14 October 2004
16     -- was also intercepted.  The transcript of each of these intercepted
17     conversations was admitted into evidence.
18             The first conversation between Beqaj and Musliu, the first cousin
19     of the accused's wife, states that "the beams aren't done" and -- "the
20     roof wasn't put up."  The Prosecution suggests that these two people have
21     -- are relying upon coded language, because Beqaj is not in any way
22     surprised by the words spoken, meaning that Witness B1 was not convinced
23     and did not make a new statement.
24             The second part of the conversation is between Musliu and Beqaj's
25     son.  The second intercepted conversation was initiated by Witness B1 and
 1     constitutes the most substantial piece of evidence in support of the
 2     Prosecution's allegations.  Beqaj suggests during that call that B1 make
 3     either a new in-person statement to the lawyers or to Demir Limaj or that
 4     he send a facsimile.  Beqaj then confirmed to B1 that upon such a
 5     statement, the accused Limaj and Musliu would be released immediately and
 6     he emphasised, "about Bala I won't press you here."
 7             In the third intercepted call Beqaj talks to an unknown man about
 8     a call he had received at 5.00.  The Prosecution submits that the time
 9     mentioned is most probably a reference to B1, that called Beqaj at 5 p.m.
10             In the fourth intercepted call Beqaj talks to Dull Bajrami, who
11     according to Witness B1 and the accused's interview of 21 October 2004
12     may be the pseudonym for Abdullah Musliu, Beqaj's brother-in-law.  And he
13     tells him "There will be nothing from him."  "He just called me"; "We
14     can't hang our hopes on him"; "He's playing a game of words."  "I am so
15     angry."
16             Lastly, in the fifth intercepted call dated on the 4th of October
17     2004, the accused talks to a Demir Limaj and confirms that he has
18     received a call the night before from Salih Bajrami or Abdullah of Racak,
19     that he knows, "What this is about," and that he agrees to meet him in
20     Pristina.
21             The evidence constituted by the second and fourth intercepted
22     calls convinces the Chamber beyond reasonable doubt that the accused
23     interfered with B1.  The Chamber has no doubt that the accused knowingly
24     and wilfully intended to influence the testimony of Witness B1 in the
25     Limaj case.
 1             Let me now move on to our legal findings.  In view of the entire
 2     evidence adduced at trial, the Chamber makes the following findings in
 3     relation to each count of the indictment.
 4             Count 1, contempt of the Tribunal.  According to the following,
 5     one, threats, the Trial Chamber finds no evidence in support of the
 6     allegations that the accused threatened the potential witnesses, B1 and
 7     B2.
 8             Intimidations.  The Chamber finds no evidence in support of the
 9     allegations that the accused has intimidated potential witnesses, B1 and
10     B2.
11             Offering a bribe.  The Trial Chamber finds no evidence in support
12     of the allegations that the accused would have offered a bribe to Witness
13     B1.
14             Four, otherwise interfering with potential witnesses.  The Trial
15     Chamber finds no evidence in support of the allegations that the accused
16     interfered with the administration of justice.  In relation to the
17     potential Witness B2, the Chamber, however, is satisfied that there is
18     evidence which establish beyond reasonable doubt that the accused
19     wilfully and knowingly interfered in the administration of justice and
20     that this conduct constitutes contempt of the Tribunal.
21             Count 2, attempted contempt of the Tribunal.  The Trial Chamber
22     feels that no findings are required, given that this count is pleaded in
23     the alternative to Count 1.
24             Count 3, incite document contempt of the Tribunal.  The Trial
25     Chamber finds that there is no evidence which establishes beyond
 1     reasonable doubt that the accused wilfully and knowingly incited contempt
 2     of the Court.
 3             As far as the punishment is concerned, the Trial Chamber has
 4     considered the purposes of punishment.  Contempt requires punishment
 5     which serves not only as retribution for what has been done but also as a
 6     deterrence for others who may be tempted to act in the same way.  The
 7     Trial Chamber has given primary consideration to the gravity of Beqaj's
 8     offence and has also considered Beqaj's individual circumstances,
 9     including aggravating and mitigating circumstances.
10             As far as gravity of the offence is concerned, the Trial Chamber
11     regards the accused's contempt as very serious.  The Tribunal relies very
12     substantially upon the truthful testimony of witnesses.
13             As far as the aggravating circumstances are concerned, the
14     Chamber recalls that aggravating circumstances must be proved beyond
15     reasonable doubt.  The Prosecution submits that the vulnerability of
16     Victim [as interpreted] B1, who survived a massacre during which his
17     close relative was killed, and tortures, as well as two assassination
18     attempts and the fact that because of pressure exerted against him and
19     his family - he is now a witness under a protection programme - and this
20     makes up for an aggravating circumstance.  The Chamber accepts this
21     circumstance.  The offence was committed at a time when the victim was
22     particularly vulnerable.
23             As far as mitigating circumstances are concerned, the Trial
24     Chamber would like to recall that mitigating circumstances are proved on
25     the balance of probabilities.  The Defence submits that the character of
 1     Beqaj, who according to Witnesses B1 and B2 is good, his personal
 2     circumstances, he is the father of six children and he works in the
 3     building trade in Slovenia, his lack of criminal record and the
 4     cooperation he demonstrated during his provisional release are mitigating
 5     factors.  The Chamber accepts these circumstances as mitigating.  The
 6     Chamber recalls in particular that Witness B2 concluded his testimony by
 7     saying that he had never been afraid of Beqa Beqaj, who is "a good
 8     person," but that "his in-laws tricked him into doing these things" and
 9     that he wished "for him to be set free" and "for us to go back home
10     together if this is possible."
11             B1 testified similarly and said that Beqa Beqaj has -- had never
12     intimidated or threatened him, but had acted as a mediator between his
13     family and his wife's family and that it was unfortunate that the people
14     who had made these threats were relatives of Beqaj's wife's family.
15             Punishment to be imposed.  The Chamber considers that a term of
16     imprisonment in this case is appropriate to achieve the purposes for
17     which punishment is imposed.  Let me now move on to the disposition,
18     which I would like to remind the Trial Chamber is authoritative.
19             Mr. Beqaj, would you please rise.
20             Mr. Beqaj, would you kindly stand up, please.
21             Having considered the arguments and the evidence presented by the
22     parties, the Chamber finds you guilty on Count 1 and you are acquitted as
23     regards Counts 2 and 3.  The Trial Chamber hereby sentences you to four
24     months of imprisonment.  You are entitled to credit for days served in
25     the detention unit prior to this day.  You shall therefore be released
 1     immediately.  The Tribunal will arrange for your travel back to Kosovo.
 2     This brings the session to a close.
