 7                           [Prosecution Opening Statement]
 8             May it please the Court and Defence counsel.  Over the course of
 9     the next few days, the OTP will present to you evidence that proves that
10     Beqa Beqaj, the accused, interfered with a witness and a potential
11     witness in the Limaj trial.  In so doing he committed contempt of court
12     under Rule 77.  This case is primarily a case of witness interference and
13     bribery as opposed to threats or intimidations.  There were two targets
14     of this interference.  They are known in this case by their pseudonyms,
15     B1 and his close relative B2.  B1 is the principal victim.
16             The essential facts of this case are simple.  Between June and
17     November of 2004, Beqa Beqaj repeatedly sought - six times that we know
18     of - to convince B1 and B2 to withdraw their testimony against the Limaj
19     accused.  You will hear evidence that B1 is a critical witness in the
20     Limaj case.  In July of 1998, B1 was abducted and taken to a makeshift
21     prison camp in the village of Lapusnik in Kosovo.  Prisoners in this camp
22     were brutalised, tortured and murdered.  As the camp disbanded,
23     approximately 21 prisoners were marched into the mountains.  The guards
24     divided the 21 prisoners into two groups, one that was released and the
25     other that was to be massacred.  12 unlucky prisoners were in the second
 1     group, including B1.  They were lined up, a death sentence was
 2     pronounced, the guards opened fire on the defenceless prisoners.  B1
 3     survives this massacre by fleeing into the woods in a hail of bullets.
 4     You will hear that B1 is a critical witness.  He's a critical witness
 5     because he can testify to the acts of all three Limaj accused at
 6     Lapusnik.  He can testify firsthand to the beatings and torture that were
 7     given to him by two of the three Limaj accused.  And he can testify as an
 8     eyewitness to the massacre.  B1 was and is a critical witness in the
 9     Limaj case.  B1's importance in the Limaj case is the motive for the
10     interference in this case.  Beqa Beqaj is the means by which the
11     interferences are perpetrated.
12             You will hear evidence that Beqaj at all times relevant to this
13     indictment attempted to directly communicate with B1 without assistance.
14     B1 has been in witness protection since 2003 because of previous threats
15     to B1's safety.  He's had two assassination attempts, repeated direct
16     threats of violence and even death if he testified, and threats given to
17     his friends and family.
18             His protection measures that were awarded him or given to him
19     included moving him to a new secret location in the country to which he
20     had already fled; providing him with new telephone numbers; and giving
21     him 24-hour close protection, or 24-hour armed guards.  At this stage,
22     only his closest family members knew where he was and were able to
23     contact him.
24             This is where Beqa Beqaj enters our story.  Beqa Beqaj was
25     orphaned as a young boy and B1's father took in Beqaj and Beqaj spent
 1     much time with B1's family.  Beqaj knew B1's family and the family knew
 2     Beqaj.  Beqaj had the means to reach B1 by using list family contacts.
 3     And Beqaj had another ominous connection:  He was related by marriage to
 4     Isak Musliu, one of the Limaj accused.
 5             Now, turning our attention to June of 2004, the times relevant to
 6     our indictment.  The start of the Limaj trial is about six months away
 7     and Beqaj commits the first of his six interferences.  On June 6th,
 8     Bashkim Beqaj, who is Beqa Beqaj's son, accosts Witness B2.  B2 again is
 9     a relative of B1.  Bashkim is aggressive and he blames B2 for Isak Musliu
10     being on trial and for being in The Hague.  B2 is 68 and Bashkim Beqaj is
11     in his early 20s.  Luckily, bystanders are there to intervene and prevent
12     any escalation of that violence.  B2 goes directly from this encounter
13     and demands an explanation from Beqaj and Beqaj gives him one.  And the
14     explanation is:  that Isak Musliu has contacted Beqaj six times and
15     demanded that he go to B2 and demanded that he -- that Beqaj withdraw his
16     statement -- that Beqaj get B2 to withdraw his statement, sorry.  B2 is
17     also offered land if he withdraws his statement.
18             Early September, two months before the Limaj trial, the second
19     interference takes place.  Now B1 of course is in witness protection and
20     unreachable.  So Beqaj has to use his family connections to make contact.
21     Beqaj uses a relative of B1's to convey the following message:  that
22     Beqaj is acting on behalf of Musliu, that Musliu would give him land if
23     he withdrew his statement.
24             September 27th, interference number 3.  Beqa Beqaj visits B1's
25     relative and asks that relative to relay a message:  that Beqaj was
 1     speaking on behalf of Fatmir Limaj and Isak Musliu, that they wanted B1
 2     to travel back to Kosovo urgently to meet with the Limaj lawyers, and
 3     that he should withdraw his statement.  The relative wanted to give B1
 4     the number of Beqaj so they could speak directly.  B1 refused to take the
 5     number and refused to withdraw his testimony.
 6             Later that day, interference number 4.  Beqaj, dissatisfied with
 7     B1's refusal to cooperate with his earlier request, communicates a second
 8     time that B1 must go at great speed to Kosovo to meet with the lawyers,
 9     the family of the accused, as well as Beqaj and withdraw his statement.
10             During this period, of course, B1 is in regular communication
11     with the ICTY investigators who are preparing the case.  B1 tells the
12     investigators about these communications that he's getting so our
13     investigators set up a recorded call to capture what is happening with
14     B1.
15             And that brings us to interference number 5:  a call recorded by
16     between 1 and Beqa Beqaj.  Takes place on October 6th.  It's about a
17     month and a half before the start of the Limaj trial.  And you will hear
18     the audio recording of this conversation.  Similar to the previous
19     interferences, the essence of the call is that Beqa doesn't want B1 to
20     testify.  But in this case you will hear the actual language used by
21     Beqaj.  He says, Come meet with Limaj's brother and say you had nothing
22     to do with Limaj or Musliu.  He says, Fix something up.  Give us some
23     help.
24             As a result of this call, the OTP decides to conduct further
25     investigation.  We seek and we obtain a wire-tap of Beqaj's phone.  You
 1     will hear several of these recorded intercepted phone calls.  In these
 2     calls, Beqa uses -- Beqaj uses coded language to discuss witnesses.
 3     These conversations are the plans and they are the logistics of getting
 4     to these witnesses.  And Beqaj is involved.  In the most significant of
 5     these calls, you will hear interference number 6, a recorded call between
 6     Beqaj and B1 on October 13th.  In this call, Beqaj is again trying to
 7     interfere with B1's truthful testimony.  Beqaj again asks B1 to come back
 8     to Kosovo urgently to Kosovo to meet with lawyers and/or Demir Limaj,
 9     Fatmir Limaj's brother, to make a statement that repudiates his earlier
10     statement.  In this call they're clearly discussing B1's evidence that he
11     is to give at this institution, that he can give against Limaj and
12     Musliu.  Most importantly in this recording, Beqaj says the following:
13     "We are not pressuring you as to Bala," Bala being one of the three Limaj
14     accused.  "We are not pressuring you as to Bala."
15             You will hear evidence from ICTY investigator Jonathan Sutch who
16     interviews Beqa Beqaj twice after these intercepted calls are reviewed
17     and analysed by the OTP.  He's interviewed twice, on October 19th and the
18     21st.  In the first interview, Sutch begins the interview by advising
19     Beqaj of his rights including his right to counsel.  And at that point,
20     Beqaj makes a spontaneous admission.  He says, I don't need a lawyer
21     because I've only spoken to one witness.  He admits he's spoken to one of
22     our witnesses.
23             Sutch continues with the advisal and conducts the interview in
24     which Beqa then denies all knowledge of witnesses in our trials, all
25     knowledge of B1 and B2 in particular, denies having any contact with any
 1     witness or potential witnesses.
 2             You will hear evidence two days later that Sutch conducts the
 3     second interview and this time Beqaj is represented by counsel.  And in
 4     this interview Beqaj's story changes.  In the interview Beqaj initially,
 5     again, denies any involvement with witnesses.  But ultimately, after
 6     questioning, admits that, yes, he did have a conversation with B1 and,
 7     yes, he did have a conversation with Musliu about B1.  And you will hear
 8     those sections of that interview.
 9             That is the evidence in this case, Your Honours.  It won't take
10     us long to present it.  But it will clearly show that Beqa Beqaj
11     interfered with a witness in the Limaj trial and a potential witness in
12     the Limaj trial and it will show that he did so knowingly and it will
13     show that he did so wilfully.
14             Under Rule 77, you have the inherent power to protect your
15     proceedings, to protect what are your witnesses, to protect your trials,
16     and we are going to ask that at the end of this case, that you do just
17     that by finding Beqa Beqaj guilty.
