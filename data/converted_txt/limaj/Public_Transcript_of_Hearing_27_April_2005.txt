16        Q.   Okay.  So after the second assassination attempt and you are in
17     close protection for eight months, which is very difficult, I know, you
18     decide to leave.  Can you tell the Court what happens at that point.
19        A.   I thought that, as the Prosecutors told me, the persons who had
20     committed the murders and maltreated people would be arrested, even
21     though, as I said, I didn't know how long it takes for these things to
22     happen.  But when the UNMIK police took very long to do things and
23     conduct investigations, the most difficult thing for me was to see my
24     kids staying home and not going to school, to stay as I am now in a hall
25     like there and see your children unable to go to school.  For me this was
 1     very difficult.  And then I had a conversation, then I said, "I am
 2     willing to accept any assassination attempt but I can't stand it any
 3     more.  Even if I lived for a week or two weeks, I preferred to be free."
 4             So I signed to go back to the place where I used to work, even
 5     though I was facing major difficulties, because the firm I was working
 6     for had dismissed me and for eight months they hadn't paid anything.  But
 7     I still returned.
 8             To cut a long story short, I found another job.  I settled down
 9     there, and kept in touch with the UNMIK police.  They told me that now
10     the situation has become very grave and it is going to be treated by the
11     Tribunal, the Hague Tribunal.  And the work with the Hague Tribunal
12     investigators began.  They asked me to give a statement from the
13     beginning, from A to Z, (redacted)

18     police at this point?

20     as they called it, but in a way, I was protected.  I had always to inform
21     them when I was sleeping, when I was working, which of the persons I was
22     working with, things which they wanted me to tell them.

13        Q.   At this stage, you were put into ICTY witness protection because

16        A.   The protection measures were so that I could escape death, so
17     that I could not get killed.  What protection can be else for?
18        Q.   Let me ask the question a different way, as far as where you were
19     living.  What -- did they relocate you?
20        A.   For the moment, no.  Although I have completed secondary school,
21     I don't know the circumstances in which the security forces work.
22     However, in a very short time, I got an authorisation from the
23     authorities to withdraw from that place where I was living and it was
24     very difficult for me and my family.  I had used to that place.  I had --
25     I was used to work with that family, and my family as well was used to
 1     that place.  But their explanation was in order for you to be safe and
 2     not to get killed, you have to move from this place.  And I was forced to
 3     accept that.  Everything was difficult for us but in order to survive, I
 4     had to do everything, as they told me.  Therefore, I had to move from the
 5     original place where I lived.
 6        Q.   And what about your telephones?  Were you allowed to keep your
 7     old telephone numbers?
 8        A.   During the first move, that happened through the phone that is we
 9     had.  I had a phone, my wife had a phone.  So these were the phones that
10     were used.  Although everyone who was interested in the trial proceedings
11     followed the proceedings.  I did the same.  And as the trial approached,
12     that's how tension rose.  Especially three, four months before the trial
13     began.  This is what the authorities there, security there, told me as
14     well.
15        Q.   My question to you is:  As part of the witness protection
16     programme, did you have to change the place where you were living and the
17     phone numbers that you had, in order to protect you?  And how many times
18     was that done?
19        A.   Six months prior to the commencement of the trial, I had to
20     change my place of living, of residence, for three times.
21        Q.   So just so we are clear, from November 2003 until about six
22     months before trial, you had to change your residence and associated
23     phone numbers about three times, or three times, sorry?
24        A.   Three times, and this is all because of the security issue.
25        Q.   Okay.  And you also had at that time close protection.  You were
 1     assigned law enforcement officers to protect you.  And how many and where
 2     were those people in proximity to you and your family?
 3        A.   In fact, I could not leave my apartment at all without informing
 4     the protection forces.  They were at all times in civilian clothes, and
 5     my extra meetings with investigators or similar figures, I was escorted
 6     by cars.  But just for a simple thing like having a coffee in a
 7     restaurant, I was escorted by three or four persons, and this was very
 8     difficult.  And I wouldn't want you to misunderstand me, but even to go
 9     to a toilet was difficult because you were aware that three persons were
10     waiting there and keeping eye on you while you're there.  (redacted)
11  (redacted)
12  (redacted) I had to work for them.  But the Tribunal
