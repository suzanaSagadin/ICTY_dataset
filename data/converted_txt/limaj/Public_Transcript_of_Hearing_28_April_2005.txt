13                           WITNESS:  JONATHAN PETER SUTCH
14                           Examined by Mr. Dominguez:
15        Q.   Good morning.  Could we have your name for the record, please.
16        A.   My name is Jonathan Peter Sutch.
17        Q.   What is your occupation?
18        A.   I'm an investigator with the Office of the Prosecutor.
19        Q.   How long have you had this position?
20        A.   Since 2000.
21        Q.   What did you do before that?
22        A.   Prior to that, from 1986 to 2000 I was a police officer in the
23     United Kingdom, having been a detective since 1992.
24        Q.   Have you had the occasion to speak with the accused, Beqa Beqaj?
25        A.   Yes, I have.
 1        Q.   When did that take place?
 2        A.   I first spoke to him on the 19th of October last year, 2004.
 3        Q.   And where was that?
 4        A.   At his home address in Shtime, in the region of Kosovo.
 5        Q.   Can you -- one moment.  What was the purpose of your going to
 6     this town to speak with the accused?
 7        A.   Information had been received by the Office of the Prosecutor
 8     concerning the defendant and his contact with witnesses in another case
 9     before the Tribunal.
10        Q.   Are you aware of the pseudonyms in this case for the witnesses --
11        A.   Yes.
12        Q.   -- or do I need to refresh your recollection off the record?
13        A.   No.  I'm aware.  I'm aware.
14        Q.   Did you work with another investigator in this case?
15        A.   In Kosovo, no.
16        Q.   When you speak about the incidents that led up to your occasion
17     to meet with the accused, how did you learn of the facts that you were to
18     discuss with the accused?
19        A.   Most if not all of the information was passed to me by Howard
20     Tucker, another investigator, based in The Hague.
21        Q.   Okay.  At this point I'm going to ask if you can go into the
22     incidents that led up to your interviewing the accused.  I'll start with
23     can you describe the behaviour or alleged behaviour that you were going
24     to interview him about.
25        A.   There had been a number of telephone calls made to a witness who
 1     had been relocated from the region of Kosovo, whereby that person was
 2     being asked not to testify in the case before the Tribunal.  The content
 3     of those telephone conversations had been recorded, and upon analysis of
 4     those transcripts of the recordings, it seemed on the face of it that the
 5     person using the telephone and speaking to the witness could be
 6     committing offences under the Statute of the Tribunal.  I'm sorry.
 7        Q.   Let me just ask a question to confirm.  You mentioned the
 8     witness.  Can you give us the pseudonym for this witness, please, for the
 9     record.
10        A.   B1.
11        Q.   Can you now go into detail about the offences that may have been
12     committed.
13        A.   Specifically, interfering with witnesses, contempt of the
14     Tribunal.  I'm sorry.
15        Q.   Go ahead and take a minute if you need to.
16        A.   No, it's okay.  It's okay.
17        Q.   Did you have occasion to review the wire-tapped conversations
18     before you spoke with the accused?
19        A.   No, I didn't review the conversations, but I did have meetings,
20     mainly telephone conversations, with colleagues concerning the content of
21     the wire-tapped conversations.
22        Q.   And they prepared you for the interview with the accused?
23        A.   Yes, they did.
24        Q.   Do you remember in detail these conversations with the
25     investigator, Howard Tucker?
 1        A.   No, not in detail.  I can obviously recall the basic content of
 2     the conversations, though.
 3        Q.   Let's go to October 19th, when you went to the accused's house.
 4     How did you proceed at that point?
 5        A.   The intention was at that time just to make an arrangement for an
 6     interview with the accused.  I went with an interpreter from the Tribunal
 7     who was permanently based in Kosovo.  Went to the accused's house and --
 8     I'm sorry, I'm going to have to take another drink of water.
 9             Went to the accused's house, where I identified myself to the
10     accused and -- thank you.
11             I was invited in to speak to him.  In his home, I fully
12     introduced myself, explained why I wanted to speak to him, that we had
13     reason to believe that he may have been involved in actions that could
14     constitute an offence under the Statute of the Tribunal and that it was
15     my intention to interview him, not at his home address but in the ICTY
16     offices in Pristina.  I further informed him at that time that if
17     necessary, he could have somebody present during the interview, legal
18     representation, and if he didn't have a lawyer, we would arrange to have
19     one present for him.
20        Q.   At that point, how did the accused respond?
21        A.   He said that he'd only been in contact with one witness, or words
22     to that effect, at which point I interrupted him and cautioned him
23     telling him that he didn't have to say anything but anything that he did
24     say at that point would be used in evidence.  Again he continued saying
25     that -- words to the effect of he had spoken to somebody and if they
 1     wanted to give evidence, that was a matter for them.
 2             I didn't go into detail with him at this point because I wasn't
 3     in a position to electronically record the conversation, and it was my
 4     intention to conduct the interview not at his home address.  So we made
 5     an arrangement to meet two days later at the Pristina field office.
 6        Q.   Did he mention the name of the witness?
 7        A.   No, he didn't, not at that time, no.
 8        Q.   Okay.  What happened with the arrangements?  Can you specify what
 9     the arrangements were?
10        A.   He later called to say that he didn't want to meet two days later
11     at the field office, and that it was his intention not to come.
12        Q.   What did you do at that point?
13        A.   This information was passed to my colleagues here in The Hague,
14     and subsequently a suspect summons was issued by the Prosecutor
15     concerning the accused in order that we could then take further steps to
16     ensure an interview took place.
17        Q.   What steps did you then take?
18        A.   Later that day, I went back to his home address.  I'd previously
19     been introduced to him.  I presented him, served him with the summons,
20     and -- excuse me.  He agreed to come with me to the offices in Pristina,
21     some 20, 30 minutes away by car.
22             And we subsequently conducted an interview later that night, just
23     myself, an interpreter and Mr. Beqaj who had at that time declined the
24     offer of legal representation during the interview.
25        Q.   Did you record the interview?
 1        A.   On this occasion, yes.  The interview was tape recorded.
 2        Q.   Who did the tape recording?
 3        A.   I did.
 4        Q.   I'm going to ask some foundational questions regarding the tape
 5     recording, in terms of its admissibility.
 6             Were you familiar with the machine you used to record the
 7     interview?
 8        A.   Yes, I was, yes.
 9        Q.   Did you test it before you taped the interview?
10        A.   Yes, as always, yes.
11        Q.   Did you listen to the tape after the interview?
12        A.   Yes, I did, yes.
13        Q.   Did it accurately reflect the interview?
14        A.   Yes, it did.
15        Q.   Do you know if the accused received a copy of the tape?
16        A.   Yes, a copy would have been served, yes, although not
17     immediately.
18        Q.   How did the interview proceed?
19        A.   The interview itself actually didn't really centre on any of the
20     telephone intercepts, the reason being at that stage was that it was the
21     OTP's decision, or the investigation's decision, that we wouldn't
22     disclose that element.  So the interview centred on the accused's
23     knowledge of people giving evidence in a specific case before the
24     Tribunal and his contact with anybody of that time.
25        Q.   Did the accused in general admit that he had any knowledge of
 1     witnesses in the case or -- let me stop there.  Any knowledge of
 2     witnesses in the case?
 3        A.   No.  The interview was essentially a number of denials concerning
 4     knowledge of people involved in the case, the only exceptions being that
 5     the elements that were known generally throughout Kosovo as a result of
 6     media coverage.
 7        Q.   So at this point, you had information about specific calls from
 8     the wire-taps.  And did he verify or deny information that you had from
 9     these wire-taps?
10        A.   No, he did not, no.  Well, sorry, there were denials.  There was
11     no verification.
12        Q.   Okay.  Based on the interview, what happened?
13        A.   Based on that interview, a provisional request for arrest was
14     issued by the Prosecutor under Article 40 of the Tribunal, and -- under
15     the Statute, sorry.  And he was subsequently taken into custody by the
16     UNMIK police, the UN police in Kosovo.
17        Q.   Did you have occasion to interview him again after the October
18     19th interview?
19        A.   I interviewed him two days later, this time with legal
20     representation, which I subsequently arranged for him.
21        Q.   What was the name of the legal representative?
22        A.   It was a man by the name of Azem Vllasi, who is a Kosovo-based --
23     Kosovo Albanian lawyer, who was asked by name actually by the accused.
24        Q.   Who else was present at this interview?
25        A.   There was myself, Mr. Pekka Havarinen, who is a former
 1     investigator for the Tribunal, and Mr. Artan Grubi, who is the Tribunal
 2     interpreter who had been used previously.
 3        Q.   And the accused?
 4        A.   And the accused, of course, yes.
 5        Q.   Did you record this interview?
 6        A.   Again, using the same equipment, in fact.  The interview was
 7     recorded throughout and the same conditions applied.  The equipment was
 8     tested.
 9        Q.   You tested it beforehand and afterwards?
10        A.   Yes, yes.
