 5            JUDGE JORDA: [Interpretation] Turning to the registrar, would you
 6    please tell us what case is on the docket this morning, and bring in the
 7    accused.
 8                          [The accused entered court]
 9            THE REGISTRAR: [Interpretation] This is the Prosecutor versus
10    Tihomir Blaskic, case IT-95-14-T.
11            JUDGE JORDA: [Interpretation] Thank you.  I would first like to
12    ask who is representing the Prosecution.
13            MR. HARMON:  Good morning, Your Honours.  My name is Mark Harmon,
14    and appearing with me is Mr. Gregory Kehoe.
15            JUDGE JORDA: [Interpretation] For the Defence, please.
16            MR. NOBILO: [Interpretation] Good morning.  Anto Nobilo,
17    attorney-at-law, counsel for General Blaskic.
18            JUDGE JORDA: [Interpretation] Are the interpretation booths
19    ready?  Does everybody hear?  General Blaskic, do you hear?
20            THE ACCUSED: [Interpretation] Your Honours, I do.
21            JUDGE JORDA: [Interpretation] Thank you.
22            Trial Chamber I of the International Tribunal, composed of
23    Presiding Judge Claude Jorda, Judge Jan, and Judge Fouad Riad, render the
24    following decision:
25            Trial Chamber I, noting the indictment presented by the Prosecutor
 1    against General Tihomir Blaskic, hereafter called "the accused," confirmed
 2    on 10 November 1995 and amended on 22 November 1996; noting the decisions
 3    of the President of the International Criminal Tribunal, hereafter known
 4    as "the Tribunal," the decision of 3 and 17 April 1996 in respect of the
 5    detention conditions of the accused; noting the order of Trial Chamber I
 6    of 25 April 1996 rejecting a request for provisional release of the
 7    accused; noting the invitation extended to the host country, the
 8    Netherlands, pursuant to Rule 65 of the Rules of Procedure and Evidence of
 9    the Tribunal, hereinafter "the Rules," and the written comments submitted
10    consequently by the representatives of the authorities of the host country
11    on 19 December 1996; having heard the arguments of the parties at the
12    hearing of 19 December 1996;
13            Considering that pursuant to Rule 64 of the Rules the accused is
14    currently being held under modified conditions of detention, that a
15    further application for modification of the conditions of detention has
16    been submitted to the President of the Tribunal, that the relevant
17    decision is under review;
18            Considering that in the application dated 11 December 1996
19    presented to Trial Chamber I the accused requests that he be released
20    provisionally and that the request is accompanied by several proposals
21    serving to guarantee his appearance at trial;
22            Considering that the argument of law and fact presented by the
23    accused in support of his request and the response of the Prosecutor filed
24    with the Registry on 16 December 1996 and argued at the hearing of 19
25    December 1996 should be recalled;
 1            Considering that in support of its request the Defence put forth
 2    the following arguments:  1, the accused surrendered himself voluntarily
 3    to the Tribunal which, I quote, "makes the circumstances of this case
 4    exceptional"; 2, the Prosecutor is not prepared to go to trial and has not
 5    met her obligations to disclose evidence as required by the provisions of
 6    sub-Rule 66(A) of the Rules, whereas the Defence has spared no effort to
 7    move the case forward; 3, the accused is prepared to agree to any
 8    conditions to which the Trial Chamber would make his provisional release
 9    subject, including, inter alia, house arrest in Zagreb, surrender of his
10    passport to the Tribunal, the posting of a bail bond in the amount of 1
11    million German marks; 4, the government of Croatia is prepared to give any
12    guarantee to ensure the appearance of the accused should he not wish to
13    surrender himself voluntarily to the Tribunal for trial; 5, the accused
14    would pose no danger to any victim or witness; 6, the current family
15    situation of the accused; 7, the presence in Zagreb of the accused would
16    permit him to prepare his defence more effectively;
17            Considering that in reply the Prosecutor emphasises:  1, that
18    there are reasonable grounds for believing that the accused has committed
19    the crimes of which he has been accused and that he played a leading part
20    in such crimes; 2, that the length of the detention is not unreasonable
21    and does not violate international standards in such matters; 3, that the
22    measures proposed by the accused to ensure his presence at trial are
23    insufficient; 4, that provisional release of the accused might pose a
24    danger to victims and witnesses; 5, that the family situation of the
25    accused is not unique, that it is common to many detainees, and that the
 1    modified conditions of detention which he enjoys are in excess of those of
 2    the other detainees; 6, that provisional release of the accused might
 3    discourage many witnesses from appearing;
 4            Considering that the Trial Chamber will first review the legal
 5    argument based on the analysis of Rule 65 of the Rules and on the
 6    principles of governing preventive detention in the light of the facts in
 7    the case in point; that it will then review the other arguments invoked by
 8    the Defence;
 9            First, regarding Rule 65 and the system of preventive detention at
10    the Tribunal;
11            Considering that a reading of Rule 65 of the Rules demonstrates
12    that "provisional release may be ordered by a Trial Chamber only in
13    exceptional circumstances," and that is a quote;
14            Considering that both the letter of this text and the spirit of
15    the Statute of the International Tribunal require that the legal principle
16    is detention of the accused and that release is the exception; that in
17    fact the gravity of the crimes being prosecuted by the International
18    Tribunal leave no place for any other interpretation even if it is based
19    on the general principles of law governing the applicable provisions in
20    respect of national laws which, in principle, may not be transposed to
21    international criminal law;
22            Considering that Rule 65 provides that the Trial Chamber may not
23    order provisional release unless, and I quote, "it is satisfied that the
24    accused will appear for trial and if released will not pose a danger to
25    any victim, witness, or other person";
 1            Considering that the three above-mentioned criteria must all be
 2    satisfied;
 3            Considering that the proof of the existence of exceptional
 4    circumstances which this Trial Chamber found in a previous case, in the
 5    extreme gravity of the current state of health of General Djukic,
 6    incompatible with any form of detention, this was the decision supporting
 7    the indictment and provisional release, IT-96-20-T, dated 24 April 1996,
 8    has in no manner been shown in the case in point; and that although it
 9    justified the modification of his conditions of detention, which is the
10    decision of the President of the Tribunal of 3 April 1996, the fact that
11    the accused surrendered voluntarily does not in and of itself invalidate
12    the principles invoked above; and that other conditions must also be
13    present;
14            Considering that the guarantees that the accused will reappear
15    which might be offered by the government of the Republic of Croatia as
16    proposed by the Defence are in no manner sufficient to ensure that should
17    he be released the accused would appear before this Tribunal at any
18    moment;
19            Considering that in fact the Trial Chamber notes that despite its
20    international obligations as expressly recognised by its having signed the
21    peace agreements in Paris on 14 December 1996, Croatia has already failed
22    to satisfy its obligations to cooperate with the Tribunal; that it notes
23    that Trial Chamber II of the Tribunal has noted the refusal of this State
24    to cooperate in the Rajic case, and that is dated 13 November 1996, Rule
25    61 of the Rules; and that in this case, Croatia has not transferred to
 1    date the accused Zlatko Aleksovski, which it is holding in detention, nor
 2    any of General Blaskic's co-accused whose names appear in the original
 3    indictment;
 4            Considering that the other guarantees which the accused proposes,
 5    including the posting of a bail bond, even one which is very high, are not
 6    sufficient to satisfy the Trial Chamber that the accused will appear at
 7    his trial because of the gravity of the criminal acts of which he stands
 8    accused, and also of the severity of the penalties to which he is liable,
 9    and last, of his offer to reside in Zagreb;
10            Considering that the Defence has not provided the proof which
11    would permit the Trial Chamber to believe that should he be released
12    provisionally he would not constitute a danger for the witnesses; that
13    furthermore the Trial Chamber, as it noted in the order rejecting an
14    initial motion for provisional release dated 25 April 1996, considers
15    that, I now quote, "the very fact of his provisional release might
16    discourage witnesses from appearing and thus hindering the proper
17    administration of justice," end of quotation;
18            Regarding the length of the preventive detention, we will now look
19    at the length of the preventive detention;
20            Considering that Trial Chamber I notes that neither the Statute of
21    the Tribunal nor the Rules provide a specific time period beyond which
22    provisional release would be a right, and that it does not appear
23    appropriate to make a comparison between the various national systems
24    given the very peculiar context in which the Tribunal finds itself;
25            Considering, however, that given any lack of referential legal
 1    system, the Judges, who are the guarantors of individual liberties, must
 2    examine the consequences of a period of preventive detention which would
 3    appear excessive to them in light of the general principle of law,
 4    including the right of the accused to be tried without excessive delay,
 5    Article 21.4(C) of the Statute and Article 5(3) of the European Convention
 6    on the Protection of Human Rights and Fundamental Freedoms, hereafter
 7    known as "the European Convention";
 8            Considering that the reasonable nature of the length of the
 9    preventive detention must, I quote, "be evaluated in the light of the
10    circumstances of each case," this is from the Sanchez Raez [phoen] case
11    against Switzerland, 21 October, series A, number 107, paragraph 55, CEDH,
12    European Court; and that the following criteria should be examined when
13    considering this question:  1, the effective length of the detention; 2,
14    the length of the detention in relation to the nature of the crime; 3, the
15    physical and psychological consequences of the detention on the detainee;
16    4, the complexity of the case and the investigations; 5, the conduct of
17    the entire procedure;
18            Considering that in the case in point the detention of the accused
19    to date is less than nine months, having begun on 1 April 1996, whereas
20    the crimes of which he is accused are extremely serious; and that in
21    respect of Article 5(3) of the European Convention on the Commission of
22    Human Rights, hereinafter "the Commission," as well as the European Court,
23    could consider reasonable in the cases in which the criteria which it has
24    identified were satisfied time periods of 19 months to five years, and our
25    reference to the European Court, already quoted, case W. versus
 1    Switzerland, Ventura versus Italy - I won't give all the references - X.
 2    versus Germany, 6 July 1976, and these quotations are in the written text;
 3            Considering that the modification of the conditions of detention
 4    granted to the accused in part relieve the usual effects of incarceration;
 5            Considering that the real complexity of the case which led the
 6    Prosecutor to draft an amended indictment containing 19 counts and the
 7    great difficulties encountered in the conduct of the on-site
 8    investigations should be noted, see European Court, inter alia, case
 9    Newmeister [phoen] versus Austria, and Sertinlai [phoen] versus
10    Switzerland, 1968 and 1980;
11            Considering that the Trial Chamber, in respect of the conduct of
12    the proceedings, deplores the delays with which the Prosecutor has
13    satisfied its obligation to disclose materials to the Defence, pursuant to
14    the provisions of sub-Rule 66(A) of the Rules, but recalls, however, that
15    the new schedule for the proceedings set into place by the Trial Chamber
16    at the hearing of 4 December 1996 also derives from the many preliminary
17    motions filed by the Defence;
18            Considers, therefore, that the preventive detention of the accused
19    does not exceed the reasonable time period pursuant to international
20    principles and particularly those of the European Convention as
21    interpreted by the Commission and the European Court;
22            Last, in respect of the other arguments raised by the Defence;
23            Considering that the accused already enjoys a privileged condition
24    of detention, the Trial Chamber finds that the arguments raised by the
25    Defence concerning the beneficial effect which his presence in Zagreb
 1    would have both to prepare his defence and to meet his family obligations
 2    are not relevant to the case in point;
 3            Considering that the conditions to which Rule 65 of the Rules
 4    subjects the provisional release of an accused have not been satisfied in
 5    the case in point;
 6            For the foregoing reasons, rejects the request for provisional
 7    release filed with the Registry by the Defence counsel of General Blaskic
 8    on 11 December 1996.
 9            Done in French and English, with the French version being
10    authoritative; done this 20th day of December, 1996, in The Hague, the
11    Netherlands.
12            The court stands adjourned.
