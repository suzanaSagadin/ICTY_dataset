 21   MR. NOBILO:  Your Honours, as you have received a written motion pursuant 
 22   to Rule 65 for provisional release until the beginning of trial for 
 23   General Tihomir Blaskic.  We all know that in order for someone to be 
 24   provisionally released there has to be exceptional circumstances.  The 
 25   host country has to be heard and  the Trial Chamber has to be 
  1   satisfied that the accused will appear for trial upon all summons and 
  2   that he will not pose any danger to any victim, witness or other 
  3   person.
  4   		Therefore, the first point that we are going to argue, and 
  5   this Chamber will have to establish, is the existence or lack of 
  6   exceptional circumstances in this particular case.  Your Honours, we 
  7   are all aware, and you most of all, of the difficulties this Tribunal 
  8   is faced with.  The International Tribunal finds itself in a very 
  9   special situation.  You do not have a police of your own and you do 
 10   not belong to any State which controls any particular territory.  You 
 11   simply do not have the possibility to order and execute by your own 
 12   authorities the arrest of any detainee.  The biggest problem of this 
 13   trial is how to bring the indictees to justice.
 14   		Except for General Blaskic, the International Tribunal does 
 15   not have in its custody any other person of such a high rank or any 
 16   person of such importance from any of the three parties to the 
 17   conflict.  Unlike national jurisdictions, unlike in national 
 18   jurisdictions, the indictees here are aware of the possibility to 
 19   avoid appearing before the Tribunal, because in national jurisdictions 
 20   if the accused refuses to appear on his own he is apprehended by the 
 21   police and brought to the court.
 22   		Here we are aware of this sad circumstance, that many of the 
 23   indictees enjoy protection of their own countries.  They have 
 24   protection even of the police or military in their own countries.  
 25   Therefore, the police and soldiers of the United Nations do not dare 
  1   arrest the indictees in order to avoid shooting and unnecessary 
  2   casualties, also to avoid a possible  resumption of hostilities in 
  3   Bosnia and Herzegovina.
  4   		This is a special situation of this Tribunal and while viewing 
  5   the case of General Blaskic, one should bear this particular situation 
  6   in mind which is different from the situation of any other Tribunal, 
  7   any other court in the world. So, to sum up the greatest difficulty, 
  8   the biggest problem, of this Tribunal is the fact that the persons 
  9   indicted for war crimes could, assisted even by the police or the 
 10   military, avoid appearing before this Tribunal.
 11   		No one has appeared voluntarily before this Tribunal and if 
 12   they do not wish to do so, it is relatively simple for them to avoid 
 13   it.  The political situation in the former Yugoslavia is not a very 
 14   good one at the moment, and it is not favourable to apprehension of 
 15   indictees.  According to, for example, laws in Serbia, this 
 16   extraditional surrender is not possible.  Croatia has adopted such 
 17   legislation but with certain objections and reservations, and in 
 18   Bosnia and Herzegovina after a three-member presidency has been 
 19   established, it seems that they will no longer be able to execute 
 20   decisions on surrender.
 21   		Given that situation, in Croatia even before the legislation, 
 22   the relevant legislation was passed, General Blaskic had decided to 
 23   appear voluntarily before this Tribunal. It is obvious that General 
 24   Blaskic is the only person from all sides, parties to the conflict, 
 25   who had decided on his own voluntarily to appear before this trial.  
  1   No one before him did so, and no one, as far as I know, is intending 
  2   to do so.
  3   		In this situation, the rank of General Blaskic, who has 
  4   surrendered himself voluntarily to the Tribunal, it seems to  me that 
  5   this constitutes exceptional circumstances.  This is not a rule.  This 
  6   is an exception because we cannot find any similar example.
  7   		Let me just stress that General Blaskic is a General of the 
  8   Croatian Defence Council and Croatian army, and this Tribunal does not 
  9   have in its custody anybody with such a high rank.  I believe that 
 10   these circumstances differentiate General Blaskic from all other 
 11   indictees and from all other cases being prosecuted before this 
 12   Tribunal.  This, therefore, constitutes an exceptional circumstance 
 13   which is a positive one, both for the work of this Tribunal and the 
 14   situation in the former Yugoslavia.  I, therefore, believe that all 
 15   the conditions set forth in Rule 65 have been met.  I think that his 
 16   situation, his very courageous act, should be appropriately valued by 
 17   this Tribunal, also given in view of the situation in the former 
 18   Yugoslavia.  I think that this should be taken into consideration.  
 19   We, therefore, believe that all elements, all requirements, for 
 20   exceptional conditions or special conditions, whatever you want to 
 21   call them, have been met.
 22   		If we add to all this the personal situation of General 
 23   Blaskic, we will see that these circumstances are even more favourable 
 24   in that respect.  General Blaskic was indicted in November '95, and he 
 25   surrendered himself in good faith to the Tribunal in April '96.
  1   		He knew that an indictment had been issued, and he knew that 
  2   the investigation had been completed and he believed that enough 
  3   evidence had been gathered.  So he appeared voluntarily before this 
  4   Tribunal, rightfully expecting his right  to an expeditious trial to 
  5   be granted.  He was also aware of a provision from the criminal code 
  6   of former Yugoslavia, a provision which is now valid both in Croatia 
  7   and Bosnia and Herzegovina, which says that the accused who is 
  8   detained in detention, that his trial should begin within two months, 
  9   two months since the indictment against him was issued.
 10   		This is a provision which is valid in Croatia and Bosnia and 
 11   Herzegovina, that the accused should be tried two months after the 
 12   indictment was issued.  This is a similar provision to the provision 
 13   of this Tribunal which speaks about the right to a fair and 
 14   expeditious trial.  Therefore, he arrived, he presented himself in 
 15   good faith.  He wanted to defend himself and he wanted to clear his 
 16   name.
 17   		Six months have passed.  The trial has not started yet.  A 
 18   year will probably have passed and the trial would begin and maybe it 
 19   would not.  In its response, the Prosecution stated certain examples, 
 20   but they keep forgetting that we do not object to the duration of the 
 21   investigation until the issuance of the indictment, but we do object 
 22   if from the moment the indictment was issued, the Prosecution states 
 23   that they are not yet ready because they do not have enough evidence 
 24   and eight months have passed in the meantime.
 25   		In that case, we do have objections and we respectfully submit 
  1   that all these facts point to the conclusion that in the case of 
  2   General Blaskic exceptional circumstances do exist, and that he is a 
  3   unique example at this Tribunal, and we do not believe that there are 
  4   any indications that anybody would be in a similar situation as 
  5   General Blaskic very soon. 
  6   		Detention is not a sanction.  It is not a repression. It is 
  7   our opinion (and we believe that you will agree with us) detention is 
  8   just a safety measure to ensure that the indictee will not pose danger 
  9   for witnesses and victims, that he will not abscond and so on.  
 10   Therefore, just as any other sanction, it should be minimum, it should 
 11   be a minimum sanction which is necessary to ensure those objectives.  
 12   If we analyse the situation of General Blaskic, his behaviour so far 
 13   and all the facts that have been established so far in this case, then 
 14   I believe that we can come up with a conclusion that there is no 
 15   danger that General Blaskic will escape and that he will not appear 
 16   before this Tribunal if summoned.
 17   		So General Blaskic again has come to the Hague on his own.  He 
 18   was not under any kind of pressure, and if he arrived here on his own 
 19   in April, then you can be assured that he would appear before this 
 20   Tribunal upon any request.
 21   		The very fact of his coming to the Tribunal on his own is the 
 22   strongest argument when we say that there is no danger that General 
 23   Blaskic would either hide or abscond.  Not a single fact has been 
 24   established that General Blaskic would pose any danger for witnesses 
 25   or victims.  If we analyse the file, we can conclude that General 
  1   Blaskic did not pose any danger for victims or witnesses during the 
  2   war and especially not now.
  3   		We have read all the witness statements submitted to us by the 
  4   Prosecution and we do not have any objection, I do not see any need to 
  5   influence those witnesses.  We are perfectly satisfied with those 
  6   witnesses and with what they say.
  7   		Therefore, I believe that circumstances, the  situation, of 
  8   General Blaskic do not point out to any danger of escape or influence 
  9   of witnesses.  But in order to support this and to give full 
 10   assurances to you, we have proposed a whole range of measures that 
 11   guarantee that General Blaskic will appear for trial and will not 
 12   interfere with witnesses.  I would like to stress something that may 
 13   not have been written in our submission, but we propose the 
 14   provisional release take place until the beginning of the trial and he 
 15   will be available to you in the conditions set forth by the Trial 
 16   Chamber.
 17   		We propose that his ability to travel be limited and that he 
 18   can reside only in Zagreb.  We are, therefore, prepared to hand over 
 19   his passport and we are prepared to be exposed to any kind of control 
 20   of your office in Zagreb, by telephone or in any other way.  The 
 21   Republic of Croatia is prepared to give its guarantee, to offer its 
 22   guarantee, that General Blaskic will return to trial and we propose 
 23   the bail bond to the amount of 1 million German marks.  This is 
 24   offered to the Tribunal as an additional guarantee that General 
 25   Blaskic will not go into hiding and that he will come back for trial.
  1   		Last but not least, General Blaskic is prepared to give his 
  2   word of honour that he will come back to the Hague, he will come back 
  3   to the Tribunal for trial and his behaviour so far is a sufficient 
  4   guarantee in this respect.
  5   		To sum up, I would like to express my hope that I think it can 
  6   be concluded that all the conditions, all the requirements, for 
  7   provisional release have been met.  We are proposing that General 
  8   Blaskic reside in the Republic of Croatia and it is, of course, 
  9   possible to consult the country in which  he will reside and the host 
 10   country in this case would be, therefore, Republic of Croatia.  The 
 11   Republic of Croatia is prepared to give any assurances, any guarantee, 
 12   requested by this Tribunal.
