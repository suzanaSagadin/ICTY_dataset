 9    IT-97-25/1-PT, the Prosecutor against Mitar Rasevic and Savo Todovic.

11    please.
12            MS. UERTZ-RETZLAFF:  Good morning, Your Honour.  My name is
13    Hildegard Uertz-Retzlaff, appearing for the Prosecution together with case
14    manager Helena Tavares-Borges.

16    Defence counsel to introduce themselves, please.
17            MR. DOMAZET: [Interpretation] Good morning, Your Honour.  Vladimir
18    Domazet, counsel representing Mitar Rasevic.
19            MR. LAZAREVIC:  Good morning, Your Honour.  I am Aleksandar
20    Lazarevic, counsel for Mr. Todovic.
21            JUDGE ANTONETTI: [Interpretation] I would like to welcome all the
22    people present in the courtroom; the registrar who is helping us today, I
23    would like to welcome the usher, the representative of the Prosecution,
24    the legal officer, and the two Defence counsels, as well as Mr. Rasevic
25    who is also attending this hearing.  I have not forgotten to mention the
 1    interpreters who are giving us a helping hand this morning.
 2            As you know, this is a Status Conference.  Mr. Rasevic is the sole
 3    accused to attend this hearing today.  Mr. Todovic let us know that he was
 4    unable to come for medical reasons.  He therefore shall be represented
 5    today by Mr. Lazarevic.  As you know, according to Article 68 bis, the
 6    Trial Chamber must convene a hearing every 120 days so that -- to
 7    facilitate exchange between the parties and to -- so that I can assess the
 8    situation and see that this trial does not run late.
 9            As you know, on the 4th of November, 2004, motions had been filed
10    pursuant to Rule 11 bis to refer this case to Bosnia-Herzegovina.
11    Following these motions a -- this has been referred to the Tribunal of
12    Bosnia-Herzegovina pursuant to Article 11 bis of the Rules of Procedure
13    and Evidence.  Following this decision, the accused Todovic and the
14    accused Rasevic have applied -- have appealed the decision taken --
15    decision for referral.
16            Eight months have passed now, and the Appeals Chamber has been
17    seized of this appeal and to date no decision has been rendered yet.  The
18    Prosecution could perhaps let us know where the procedure stands and where
19    we stand vis-a-vis the Appeals Chamber.
20            MS. UERTZ-RETZLAFF:  Your Honour, actually, what you said is
21    correct.  All that's -- I don't know more than that; that is, the appeal
22    is still pending.  And I don't know why.
23            JUDGE ANTONETTI: [Interpretation] Very well.  We shall wait for
24    the Appeals Chamber to render a decision.  Either it will confirm the
25    decision and the case is referred to Bosnia-Herzegovina or the case is not
 1    confirmed, in which case it would be tried before this Tribunal.  This is
 2    all the more important as, as you know, the accused Todovic had filed a
 3    motion pursuant to Rule 72(A) pertaining to the form of the indictment,
 4    and this motion for the time being has not -- the Trial Chamber has not
 5    rendered a decision vis-a-vis this particular motion.  As far as I'm
 6    concerned, I feel that if the case is referred to a Tribunal in
 7    Bosnia-Herzegovina, the Prosecutor in that country is -- can then draft
 8    the indictment and refer it to the competent authorities.  So I don't see
 9    what the purpose of rendering a decision thereof on a particular legal
10    issue; i.e., the form of the indictment, if it is being referred to
11    another jurisdiction.  However, if the Appeals Chamber hands down a
12    decision which sets aside this particular issue, in that case -- and if
13    this is to be tried before this Tribunal, in that case we will have enough
14    time to respond to this motion.  This does not raise any particular
15    problem, because as you know, a judgement has already been handed down by
16    the Appeals Chamber in the Krnojelac case.  A decision pertaining to the
17    form of the indictment has already been rendered following the motion
18    filed by the accused Rasevic.  So, as things stand today, we already have
19    the legal basis enabling us to render a decision on the merits of the
20    filed by Mr. Todovic.
21            At this stage of the hearing I notice that this -- a decision
22    could have been taken a while ago, but as things stand, no decision has
23    been taken yet.  It's also important to note that in the case the -- this
24    case would be referred to a national jurisdiction pursuant to Rule 11 bis
25    and Rule 10 of the Rules of Procedure and Evidence, the Prosecution will
 1    be entitled to follow the proceedings and may ask the national
 2    jurisdiction to be disqualified, which would yet again extend the
 3    dead-line.  So I cannot tell Mr. Rasevic when he will be tried before a
 4    court because I have no power of decision on the dead-lines.  As I had
 5    already stipulated a while ago already, that this case could have been
 6    tried a year and a half already ago.
 7            At this stage, pursuant to Rule 66(A) of the Rules of Procedure
 8    and Evidence, I must mention the issue of disclosure of material.  I took
 9    stock of the situation a while ago, I don't think there is any particular
10    problem here.  All the material has been disclosed, and I don't think
11    anything special needs to be mentioned here.  The same applies to Rule 68;
12    I think everything has been disclosed and I think that has been settled.
13            If there are no particular issues arising out of Rule 66 and 68, I
14    would like to address the additional issue of the computer which Todovic
15    has been provided with.  In October 2005 Mr. Lazarevic told us that his
16    client did not have a computer in his cell.  I investigated the matter,
17    and this is a fairly relevant issue, given that over the last few months
18    we have now -- we now have an EDS system, which can be used by the accused
19    in the Detention Centre, and electronic disclosure can only be carried out
20    by using this -- by using a computer, but in order for this to work, the
21    accused have to have a computer.  If they don't have a computer, I don't
22    see how the system could work.  So Mr. Lazarevic will tell me about this,
23    but from what I understand, the accused Todovic still does not have a
24    computer because there is a problem with the computer company that was
25    supposed to provide the computer.  The contract with the said company
 1    still hasn't been signed and therefore the accused is still without a
 2    computer.  Mr. Lazarevic, could you confirm this, please.
 3            MR. LAZAREVIC:  By all means, Your Honour.  The PC that is
 4    currently waiting in the Detention Unit for Mr. Todovic was brought in The
 5    Hague by myself and I believe that he could work on this computer,
 6    although it is mine, however the situation that appeared seems that a
 7    company was called to provide with computers all the detainees and so far
 8    it hasn't been performed, so Mr. Todovic still does not have a PC.
 9            JUDGE ANTONETTI: [Interpretation] So I know that Mr. Todovic still
10    doesn't have a computer but I don't think he is the only one to be in that
11    position.  A number of other accused do not have a computer either.  That
12    said, some of the accused had been given a computer by family members, but
13    these cannot be used because in the meantime it has been decided that the
14    accused must use the computers provided by the Registry.  So let's wait
15    for this computer to reach the accused.  There is no reason for despair,
16    and I'm sure there will be a positive outcome here.
17            Before I give the floor to Mr. Rasevic, does the Prosecution have
18    anything to add?
19            MS. UERTZ-RETZLAFF:  No, thank you, Your Honour.
20            JUDGE ANTONETTI: [Interpretation] Mr. Lazarevic, would you like to
21    add anything, specify anything?
22            MR. LAZAREVIC:  I believe that Your Honour is already informed
23    regarding Mr. Todovic's medical condition.  I would just like to give one
24    further explanation.  Mr. Todovic broke his arm, and he had a minor
25    surgery yesterday, and that is the only reason why he couldn't attend this
 1    Status Conference, and I was asked by Mr. Todovic to tell this before the
 2    Trial Chamber.  He has no complaints regarding medical treatment, I have
 3    to say this.  However, I will follow closely the development of his
 4    medical problem and if -- in any case I will inform the Trial Chamber in
 5    case of an emergency.
 6            JUDGE ANTONETTI: [Interpretation] Thank you, Mr. Lazarevic.
 7            Mr. Domazet, you have the floor.
 8            MR. DOMAZET: [Interpretation] Your Honour, as far as Mr. Rasevic
 9    is concerned, there is no problem.  The sole problem being that we are
10    just awaiting the decision.  He would also like to say that there is no
11    particular problem.
12            JUDGE ANTONETTI: [Interpretation] Mr. Rasevic, could you stand up,
13    please.  I have two questions to put to you regarding your health.  Is
14    there anything special you would like to mention or not?
15            THE ACCUSED RASEVIC: [Interpretation] Your Honour, there are no
16    problems regarding my medical condition.  In that respect everything is
17    perfect, thank you.
18            JUDGE ANTONETTI: [Interpretation] Fine.  The second question
19    relates to your conditions in detention.  Do you have any complaints to
20    file or any comments to make?
21            THE ACCUSED RASEVIC: [Interpretation] As far as the conditions in
22    the Detention Unit, from the very first day I didn't have any complaints
23    and the situation in that respect is also perfect.
24            JUDGE ANTONETTI: [Interpretation] Fine, thank you.  Would you like
25    to say anything about any other topic?
 1            THE ACCUSED RASEVIC: [Interpretation] Please allow me just to say
 2    one or two sentences.  It's a personal thing.  I've spent already two and
 3    a half years here, so I would first of all like to thank you for treating
 4    me fairly.  I see you have put it already eight months have passed since
 5    the beginning of the 11 bis proceedings appeal.  I have at first talked
 6    with my Defence about my wishes and I said I did not wish to raise an
 7    appeal because in that way maybe the decision would have been expedited.
 8    I do not care really personally which Tribunal will be trying me, I
 9    believe that any Tribunal will do its job in accordance with the law and
10    the evidence.  I believe that this court in Sarajevo will deal with me in
11    the same manner, therefore I do not see why is it necessary for this to be
12    postponed for a period of eight months.  It's a simple decision to make.
13    I will either be tried here or not.  This way this is being postponed and
14    postponed again, nothing is happening there, we are waiting for one thing
15    to get settled, but then things grind to a halt, we have to start again,
16    and then we have to wait for something else.
17            I would like to ask, therefore, both the Chamber and Madam
18    Prosecutor, can you somehow please ask them to expedite this issue so that
19    it's sorted out in a reasonable time-frame?  This is my request.  Thank
20    you very much.
21            JUDGE ANTONETTI: [Interpretation] Very well, Mr. Rasevic.  I shall
22    let the Appeals Chamber know what you have just said, and I shall ask them
23    to take their decision speedily.  As you have just indicated, you have
24    been waiting for the last eight months for this final decision.  I would
25    like to thank you, you may sit down.
 1            I hope that we will not need to see each other again, because if
 2    we meet again this will be in 120 days' time.  I hope that in the mean
 3    time everything will be settled.  As I have just said, I shall, as I have
 4    committed to, inform the Appeals Chamber of this particular situation.  If
 5    there are no other issues to be raised, I would like to thank you.  And
 6    the court stands adjourned.
