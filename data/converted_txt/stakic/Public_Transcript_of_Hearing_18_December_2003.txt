 9            JUDGE MERON:  Thank you.  The Defence has also recently filed
10    another request for an extension of time for the filing of its appeal
11    brief.  This was filed on the 17th of December.  That was yesterday.  On
12    the 30th of October, I granted an extension of time allowing the Defence
13    to file its brief on or before the 6th of January, 2004.  In its motion
14    requesting a further extension, the Defence states that due to the volume
15    of material on the electronic database to which it was given access by the
16    Prosecutor on the 24th of November and due to the fact that the judgement
17    of the Trial Chamber was only made available to Dr. Stakic in B/C/S
18    language on 9 of December, it is not possible for it to meet that
19    deadline.
20            It seeks a further extension of between 30 and 45 days for the
21    filing of that brief, and then a motion under Rule 115 of the Rules of
22    Procedure and Evidence.  At this time, I would like to ask Mr. Farrell
23    whether the Prosecution is able to indicate its position on the motion by
24    the Defence for an extension.  Mr. Farrell.
25            MR. FARRELL:  Your Honour, there are two issues that are raised in
 1    the motion, as you've indicated.  One relates to the search for additional
 2    evidence, and the other relates to the appeal brief.  The normal practice,
 3    as you're fully aware, Mr. President, is that the additional evidence
 4    isn't related to the appeal brief.  The appeal brief is on the record and
 5    any additional evidence is filed separately.  In that regard, although
 6    obviously there's an onerous burden in light of the workload of my learned
 7    colleagues, what I would suggest to Your Honour, in my submission, is that
 8    we keep those two process separate.  But for the appeal brief that's due
 9    on January 6th, the Prosecution has no objection to a reasonable extension
10    of time, in light of the fact that the B/C/S version of the judgement came
11    a week or so late.  My understanding from your previous decision was that
12    the reasonable amount of time included the time for them to have the B/C/S
13    version.
14            So since it's been delayed a short period of time, we have no
15    objection to a short extension of time to take that into consideration for
16    them filing their appeal brief and that that's not dependent on a review
17    of the additional evidence.  That's just on the record.  So whatever
18    reasonable period of time it is for them to file their appeal brief, we
19    have no objection to that in light of that consideration.
20            The other issue is whether or not there's an extension of time for
21    the 115 motion because of the additional evidence.  I note that the 115
22    motion, if there was to be one, was actually due on October the 14th.  So
23    it's now already two months passed that.  And even though there has been
24    access to the disclosure database in November, any motion should have been
25    filed long before they even got access to that database.  The database
 1    itself isn't a basis, in the Prosecution's submission, for why there
 2    wasn't something filed on the 14th or an extension of time, of course,
 3    sought on the 14th.
 4            Also, the database doesn't alleviate the Prosecution from
 5    reviewing and disclosing Rule 68 at this stage, with the present rules the
 6    way they are, so though of course my learned colleagues are at liberty to
 7    review the database in case they find anything which will assist them to
 8    continue to do investigations and find new evidence, that database in and
 9    of itself isn't, in my view, a basis for claiming that there is material
10    there they haven't received that would be Rule 68.
11            What I would propose is that with respect to the appeal brief, a
12    reasonable period of time, in light of the delay in getting the B/C/S
13    version.  In relation to the 115 material, since the date is already past,
14    and since they'll need to seek leave, what I would suggest is once they
15    have Rule 115 material that they intend to file - and it's not clear to me
16    whether they do or they don't from their motion - once they have any Rule
17    115 material that they would seek to file, since they're already past the
18    deadline, they should just bring it at that time with a motion seeking
19    leave, at that time explaining why it wasn't available when the deadline
20    was due and we'll address it then when they bring the 115 motion.  That
21    way we can dispose of the motion for the appeal brief.  That can be dealt
22    with in a reasonable period of time.
23            I appreciate, of course, it's over the holidays, so I don't want
24    my learned colleagues to have to work too extensively during that period.
25    So a reasonable period for the appeal brief is acceptable to us, and then
 1    in relation to the 115, once they have had a chance to get their 115
 2    material together, they can file a motion at that time indicating why they
 3    weren't able to file it back on October 14th.  I think that would be the
 4    most reasonable way to deal with it.  Unless you have any questions, that
 5    would my submission.

13            Turning to the Defence, as I have indicated, the motion for an
14    extension also seeks an extension of time for the filing of a motion under
15    Rule 115, under Rule 115(A), as you all know, motions for additional
16    evidence on appeal must be submitted within 75 days of the date of
17    judgement.  The judgement was issued on the 31st of July 2003, which means
18    that motions under Rule 115 were due on the 14th of October.
19            Under the Tribunal precedents, a request for an extension of time
20    to file a Rule 115 motion should have been filed by that date as well.  I
21    recall that the Defence filed a motion for an extension of time to file
22    its appeal brief on that day, which I granted, but neither that motion nor
23    my order mentioned Rule 115 at the time.  And I would like to ask the
24    Defence counsel why it has -- why has the Defence waited so long to
25    request an extension for the 115 filing?
 1            MR. OSTOJIC:  Good afternoon, Your Honour.  John Ostojic on behalf
 2    of Dr. Stakic.  To address the Court's question specifically, we believe
 3    that it would not have been prudent at that time to seek an extension of
 4    time and claim that there's any basis for 115 unless we actually had the
 5    material.  Mr. Lukic has specifically conducted through the electronic
 6    version of the database provided to us research and has found some
 7    material.  We thought it would be premature to anticipate, A, that the
 8    Prosecution failed in their obligation and duty to give us all the
 9    material, and B, we did not want to get a response from the Prosecution
10    saying that we're filing for an extension of time but have no basis for an
11    extension of time.  If we had the documents and needed additional time to
12    review them, naturally we would have filed it timely and within the
13    deadlines in the Rules.  Since we didn't have it, we thought that it was
14    inappropriate to file at that time because we would be asking for
15    something that we had no knowledge if were going to pursue.
16            I'd like to also address a complicated situation with respect to
17    these documents.  The Municipality of Prijedor is unique in the sense that
18    the searches that were conducted in the Prijedor Municipality allowed the
19    representative parties, the Prosecutor namely, to seize and take away all
20    the documents in the municipality, in the police station, and in various
21    barracks.  Copies were not made at the site.  Those were taken by the
22    Prosecution.
23            The Defence does not have access and has never had access to all
24    those searches that were done.  Hopefully now with this database, that
25    will be clarified and we have an avenue to review all the documents and we
 1    will try to, if necessary, seek leave at the appropriate time attaching
 2    the documents and our arguments as to why we think its appropriate to have
 3    those documents before the Appeals Chamber.

21    motion under Rule 115 on or before February 1, 2004.
22            I remind the Defence counsel, however, that the Tribunal will not
23    take kindly to belated applications for extensions of time.  I would also
24    like to remind both of the parties that the appeals proceedings must be
25    conducted as expeditiously as possible.  If the Prosecutor can assist the
 1    Defence in conducting its searches with the electronic database, it would
 2    be in the interests of all that it does so.

 4    filed this afternoon or, at the latest, tomorrow.
