 5            JUDGE MERON:  Mr. Registrar, would you please call the case.
 6            THE REGISTRAR:  Good afternoon, Your Honour.  This is case number
 7    IT-97-24-A, the Prosecutor versus Milomir Stakic.
 8            JUDGE MERON:  Appearances of the parties.  Prosecution, please.
 9            MR. TRACOL: [Interpretation] Good afternoon, Mr. President.
10    Xavier Tracol for the Prosecutor with Helen Brady and Susan Grogan, our
11    case manager.

14    Dr. Stakic this afternoon.

16            Mr. Stakic, I just wanted to make sure that you can hear me and in
17    a language that you understand.  Did you understand what I said?
18            THE APPELLANT: [Interpretation] Good day, Your Honour.  I can
19    follow in a language that I understand.
20            JUDGE MERON:  Thank you.  You may be seated.
21            This is a Status Conference called in accordance with Rule 65 bis
22    of the Rules of Procedure and Evidence.  Rule 65 bis (B) requires that a
23    Status Conference be convened within 120 days of the last Status
24    Conference to allow any person in custody pending appeal the opportunity
25    to raise issues in relation thereto, including the mental and physical
 1    condition of that person.  Our last Status Conference in this case was on
 2    February 23, 2005.
 3            Mr. Stakic, I would like to know whether you have any issues that
 4    you would like to raise now in relation to your detention, please.
 5            THE APPELLANT: [Interpretation] All I can say is that I continue
 6    to receive medical treatment.  This has been the case for six months and
 7    I'm provided with medicine three times a day.  I am managing to keep my
 8    blood pressure under control.  Occasionally it is 150/90, but on the
 9    whole, it is not that high.  And I do not have any other problems.
10            JUDGE MERON:  Thank you, Mr. Stakic.  You may be seated.
11            At the moment, the parties' submissions on their respective
12    appeals from the trial judgement are complete.  While at the last Status
13    Conference I told you that the appeal hearing would likely be scheduled
14    sometime in June, it has for various reasons been necessary to delay.  The
15    hearing is now scheduled for the 4th and the 5th of October, 2005.  An
16    order to this effect will be issued soon.  Although there are two days
17    that we have scheduled for the hearing, I would like to stress that the
18    Appeals Chamber in no way requires that you use all of this time, and
19    indeed we would be grateful if submissions could be made as concisely as
20    possible.
21            I understand that two briefs have been filed confidentially.
22    These are Milomir Stakic re-filed Appellant's brief in support of his
23    notice of appeal filed on the 9th of March 2004 and the Prosecution's
24    response brief filed on 8 April 2004.
25            Pursuant to Rule 78 and 107, the Appeals Chamber requires in
 1    addition to any confidential brief that public briefs be filed so as to be
 2    able, inter alia, to conduct a public appeal hearing and render a public
 3    judgement.
 4            In reviewing the briefs filed in this case, the reason for their
 5    confidentiality was not immediately apparent to me.  Therefore, I would
 6    like confirmation and an explanation from the Prosecution and the Defence
 7    of the confidential nature of these filings.
 8            I turn to the Defence.  Mr. Ostojic, could you kindly explain your
 9    position on the confidentiality of the briefs?
10            MR. LUKIC:  It is Mr. Lukic.  I know that it's much easier to
11    remember the big guy.
12            JUDGE MERON:  Sorry.
13            MR. LUKIC:  Yes.  Our confidentiality is actually based basically
14    on use of some closed sessions, transcripts from closed sessions, and we
15    are ready to make public version but probably we'll need up to three weeks
16    to revise this confidential brief.
17            JUDGE MERON:  In other words, you could not just make it public,
18    you would have to have a redacted version; correct?
19            MR. LUKIC:  I'm afraid, Your Honour, that that's the truth.
20            JUDGE MERON:  Do you need three weeks or do you think I could
21    persuade you to do it in two?
22            MR. LUKIC:  We need three weeks, but you can persuade to us do it
23    in two weeks.
24            JUDGE MERON:  So I do appreciate your cooperation on that, and I
25    take it that the Defence has agreed to two weeks.
 1            And now I turn to the Prosecution.  Now, I presume that you -- did
 2    you make your brief confidential simply because this was in response to
 3    the confidential brief or you had reasons of your own?
 4            MR. TRACOL: [Interpretation] Yes, Your Honour.  That's exactly the
 5    reason.  We responded to the brief of the Defence, and since the brief
 6    filed by the Defence was done so confidentially, we filed our brief in
 7    response confidentially because the brief refers several times to
 8    transcripts that took place in closed session, of hearings that took place
 9    in closed session.
10            The Prosecution would be ready to file a redacted version of its
11    brief in response.  What we suggest, Your Honour, is to -- for you to
12    grant us the same time that was granted to the Defence in order to file
13    this redacted version of the brief in response, but it might be
14    appropriate for the Prosecution to file this redacted version only after
15    the Defence has filed its own brief in a redacted version, because the
16    brief of the Prosecution depends on the brief filed by the Defence.
17            JUDGE MERON:  This problem makes sense to have a sequential, not a
18    concurrent, redaction of your brief, but then I really don't think that if
19    you simply sort of would follow the model of the Defence, that you need
20    two weeks for that, so I suggest that you will make do with one week.  Two
21    weeks for the Defence and one week for you to adjust.  It should not be
22    too complicated.
23            MR. TRACOL: [Interpretation] Yes, Your Honour.  We would be in a
24    position to file the redacted version of our brief in response two weeks
25    after the filing by the Defence of its redacted -- of the redacted version
 1    of its own brief.
 2            JUDGE MERON:  I'm not sure you understood me.  I meant one week
 3    after the filing by the Defence.  Defence agreed to two weeks.  I don't
 4    think you need two weeks to --
 5            MR. TRACOL: [Interpretation] I apologise.
 6            JUDGE MERON:  -- your brief in response, do you?
 7            MR. TRACOL: [Interpretation] Yes, one week could be appropriate,
 8    although two weeks would probably be better but one week would be all
 9    right.
10            JUDGE MERON:  I very much appreciate the cooperation of Mr. Lukic
11    and of Mr. Tracol in adjusting these briefing periods.  And is there
12    another question that the Defence or the -- you may be seated, Mr. Tracol.
13    Unless you want to make an argument of anything.
14            Is there anything on which you would, like either the Defence or
15    the Prosecution, any issue which you would like to raise?  You would like
16    to, Mr. Tracol, please.
17            MR. TRACOL: [Interpretation] There is another issue I'd like to
18    raise today, Your Honour.  And that is related to the decision rendered by
19    the Appeals Chamber on the admission of additional evidence on appeal.
20    It's your decision of 25 of January 2005.  I note that it is a
21    confidential decision.  Therefore, it might be adequate for us to move
22    into closed session in order to discuss the decision.
23            JUDGE MERON:  That might be prudent to do.  We will now go into
24    closed session.
